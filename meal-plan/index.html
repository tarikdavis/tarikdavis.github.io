Breakfast
Mushroom & feta omelette
~340 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 tsp butter, 1 tsp olive oil
  • Fresh chives, snipped
  • Salt & black pepper
Method
  1. Sauté mushrooms in oil over medium-high heat 4–5 mins until golden. Set aside.
  2. Whisk eggs with seasoning.
  3. Melt butter in non-stick pan, pour in eggs. Draw edges to centre as they set.
  4. When mostly set, scatter mushrooms, feta and chives across one half. Fold over.
  5. Slide onto plate.
Don't overcook — pull it off heat while the top still looks slightly glossy. Residual heat will finish the job.
Lunch
Asparagus & poached egg on sourdough
~360 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 6 British asparagus spears, woody ends snapped
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, shaved
  • Splash white wine vinegar (for poaching)
  • Squeeze of lemon, olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Steam or boil asparagus 3–4 mins until just tender.
  2. Simmer water with vinegar. Swirl and poach egg 3 mins.
  3. Toast sourdough. Drizzle with olive oil.
  4. Pile asparagus on toast, top with egg, shave over parmesan.
  5. Lemon, pepper, eat immediately.
British asparagus season is short and brilliant (Apr–Jun). Snap rather than slice the ends — they break exactly where they need to.
Dinner
Spinach & paneer curry with rice
~490 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g paneer, cubed
  • 200g baby spinach
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 2 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp cumin, ½ tsp turmeric
  • 80g brown rice (dry)
  • 1 tbsp natural yoghurt to serve
Method
  1. Cook rice per packet.
  2. Fry paneer cubes in a little oil until golden on all sides. Set aside.
  3. Soften onion 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger, spices — 1 min.
  4. Add tomatoes, simmer 8 mins. Add spinach by the handful until wilted.
  5. Stir paneer back in, simmer 3 mins. Serve over rice with yoghurt.
Frying the paneer first keeps it from breaking up in the sauce and gives a nicer texture.

Ingredients

Lunch
Watercress, new potato & boiled egg salad
~380 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 250g Jersey Royal or other new potatoes
  • 50g watercress
  • 3 radishes, sliced
  • 2 salad onions, sliced
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard, 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • Salt & black pepper
Method
  1. Boil potatoes 12–15 mins until tender. Drain and halve.
  2. Soft-boil eggs 7 mins. Cool, peel, halve.
  3. Whisk mustard, oil, vinegar with seasoning.
  4. Toss warm potatoes in dressing. Layer watercress, radishes, onions, eggs on top.
Dressing the potatoes while warm helps them soak up the flavour — the trick that turns a side salad into a proper lunch.
Dinner
Tofu & broccoli stir-fry with noodles
~460 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 150g purple sprouting broccoli, trimmed
  • 80g egg or wholewheat noodles
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 2 tbsp reduced-salt soy sauce
  • 1 tsp honey, 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tsp neutral oil for frying
Method
  1. Press tofu 10 mins. Cube and fry until golden on all sides. Set aside.
  2. Boil noodles per packet. Drain.
  3. Stir-fry onion 2 mins, add PSB stems 2 mins, then florets and garlic/ginger 1 min.
  4. Mix soy, honey, sesame oil. Add to pan with tofu and noodles. Toss 1 min to coat.
Purple sprouting broccoli is in peak season now and far sweeter than standard broccoli — cook just briefly to keep the bite.

Breakfast
Smashed avocado & broad bean on toast
~370 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ ripe avocado
  • 80g podded broad beans (fresh or frozen)
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Small handful fresh mint leaves, chopped
  • Pinch chilli flakes, sea salt, black pepper
  • 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds
Method
  1. Blanch broad beans 2 mins. Drain, refresh in cold water, pop out of grey skins for a brighter green.
  2. In a bowl, roughly mash avocado with lemon and a pinch of salt.
  3. Stir in broad beans and mint.
  4. Pile onto toast. Top with chilli, pepper and pumpkin seeds.
Slipping broad beans out of their skins is fiddly but doubles the flavour — and they're a properly British May treat.
Lunch
Roasted carrot, feta & lentil bowl
~370 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 medium carrots, halved lengthways
  • 1 × 400g tin green/brown lentils, drained
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp honey
  • Salt & pepper
Method
  1. Toss carrots with cumin, half the oil and seasoning. Roast at 200°C, 20–25 mins.
  2. Warm lentils in a pan with a splash of water.
  3. Whisk remaining oil with vinegar and honey.
  4. Pile lentils onto plate, top with carrots, rocket, feta. Drizzle dressing.
Roast the carrots in the morning if you can — cold roasted carrots in a packed lunch are a small joy.
Dinner
Quorn cottage pie with new potato mash
~470 kcal · 32g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g Quorn mince
  • 400g new potatoes (skins on)
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 2 garlic cloves
  • 100g frozen petits pois
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée, 1 tsp Marmite or veg stock cube
  • 300ml veg stock, 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 tbsp whole milk, 1 tsp butter (for mash)
Method
  1. Boil potatoes 15 mins until tender. Drain. Crush roughly with milk, butter, salt.
  2. Fry onion and carrot 7 mins. Add garlic, thyme 1 min.
  3. Add Quorn, stir 3 mins. Add tomato purée, Marmite, stock — simmer 10 mins.
  4. Stir in peas. Tip into ovenproof dish. Top with crushed potatoes.
  5. Bake at 200°C, 15–20 mins until top is golden.
Crushed new potatoes give a more rustic texture than smooth mash — and skip the peeling step.

Breakfast
Banana & peanut butter porridge
~340 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 250ml whole milk (or half milk, half water)
  • 1 banana, sliced
  • 1 tbsp smooth peanut butter
  • 1 tsp honey (optional)
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Combine oats and milk in a small pan. Simmer 4–5 mins, stirring often.
  2. When thick and creamy, stir in half the banana to break down.
  3. Spoon into bowl. Top with remaining banana, dollop of peanut butter, drizzle of honey, pinch of cinnamon.
Stir half the banana into the porridge while cooking — it sweetens the whole pot without any added sugar.
Lunch
Halloumi, courgette & quinoa salad
~400 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 125g halloumi, sliced
  • 1 small courgette, sliced into rounds
  • 60g quinoa (dry weight)
  • Handful rocket
  • 4 cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Juice of ½ lemon, 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, black pepper
Method
  1. Cook quinoa per packet (~12 mins). Drain, fluff with fork.
  2. Griddle or pan-fry courgette slices 2 mins each side until char-marked.
  3. Pan-fry halloumi 2 mins each side until golden.
  4. Toss quinoa with rocket, tomatoes, courgette, lemon juice, oil and oregano.
  5. Top with halloumi, plenty of black pepper.
Eat while halloumi is still hot — it squeaks beautifully but turns rubbery cold.
Dinner
Chickpea & spinach dahl with flatbread
~440 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 100g red lentils, rinsed
  • 200g baby spinach
  • 200ml light coconut milk
  • 400ml veg stock
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp ginger
  • 2 tsp curry powder, 1 tsp turmeric, ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 wrap or flatbread, warmed
Method
  1. Toast cumin seeds 30 sec in dry pan. Add a little oil and onion, fry 5 mins.
  2. Add garlic, ginger, spices — 1 min.
  3. Add lentils, coconut milk and stock. Simmer 15–18 mins until lentils break down.
  4. Add chickpeas and spinach — stir until spinach wilts, 2 mins.
  5. Season and serve with a warmed wrap to scoop with.
Red lentils don't need soaking — they collapse into a creamy base in under 20 minutes. The easiest curry to keep in your back pocket.

Dinner
Aubergine parmigiana with side salad
~470 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large aubergine, sliced into 1cm rounds
  • 250g tomato passata
  • 1 garlic clove, 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 125g mozzarella, torn
  • 30g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • Handful rocket, ½ tbsp olive oil, lemon (for salad)
  • Fresh basil (optional)
Method
  1. Brush aubergine slices with oil. Griddle 2–3 mins each side until char-marked. Or roast at 220°C, 20 mins.
  2. Warm passata with crushed garlic and oregano, 5 mins.
  3. Layer in ovenproof dish: sauce, aubergine, mozzarella. Repeat. Top with parmesan.
  4. Bake at 200°C, 20 mins until bubbling and golden.
  5. Rest 5 mins. Serve with rocket salad dressed in oil and lemon.
Resting matters — straight from the oven it's a slurry. Five minutes lets it settle into proper layers.

Breakfast
Shakshuka with feta
~360 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 1 small onion, sliced
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 garlic clove, 1 tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp cumin
  • Fresh parsley, 1 slice sourdough to dip
Method
  1. Fry onion and pepper in a little oil over medium heat, 7–8 mins until soft.
  2. Add garlic and spices, stir 1 min.
  3. Add tomatoes, simmer 8 mins. Season.
  4. Make 3 wells, crack an egg into each. Cover, cook 5–6 mins until whites are set but yolks still soft.
  5. Crumble feta, scatter parsley. Serve with sourdough.
A proper weekend breakfast — and it scales: cook for one in a small pan, four in a big one. Cover with a lid so the yolks set on top.
Lunch
Black bean & avocado burrito wrap
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • ½ ripe avocado, sliced
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 tbsp salsa or chopped tomato
  • Handful babyleaf or rocket
  • Squeeze of lime, hot sauce (optional), salt & pepper
Method
  1. Warm beans in a small pan with a pinch of cumin, mash slightly. Season.
  2. Warm wrap in a dry pan 20 seconds each side.
  3. Spread beans down the centre. Top with avocado, salsa, cheese, leaves.
  4. Lime, hot sauce. Fold in the ends and roll tightly.
Roll seam-side down, then sear in the dry pan for a minute on each side — turns it into a proper toasted burrito.
Dinner
Mushroom & pea risotto
~470 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g arborio rice
  • 200g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 100g frozen petits pois
  • 1 small onion, 2 garlic cloves
  • 500ml veg stock, hot
  • 30g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 tbsp butter, 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful rocket, fresh thyme (optional)
Method
  1. Fry mushrooms in oil over high heat until golden, 5 mins. Set aside.
  2. In same pan, soften onion in butter 5 mins. Add garlic 1 min.
  3. Stir in rice 1 min to coat. Add stock a ladle at a time, stirring until absorbed before adding more (~18 mins).
  4. When rice is just tender, stir in peas, mushrooms, parmesan. Season.
  5. Plate up, scatter rocket and thyme.
Risotto needs attention but rewards it — stay near the pan, stir gently, and add stock only when the previous ladle has been absorbed.

Eggs Florentine with spinach
~380 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 1 English muffin, split and toasted
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard, squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch nutmeg, splash white wine vinegar (for poaching)
  • Salt & black pepper
Method
  1. Wilt spinach in a dry pan over medium heat, 1–2 mins. Squeeze out excess water. Season with nutmeg.
  2. Stir yoghurt with mustard, lemon, salt and pepper for a quick "hollandaise".
  3. Poach eggs in simmering water with vinegar, 3 mins.
  4. Top muffin halves with spinach, then eggs. Spoon over yoghurt sauce.
A leaner take on classic eggs Florentine — yoghurt-mustard sauce delivers the tang without the egg yolks and butter of a true hollandaise.

Dinner
Veggie sausage roast with PSB & new potatoes
~480 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 vegetarian sausages (Cumberland-style or similar)
  • 250g new potatoes, halved if large
  • 150g purple sprouting broccoli, trimmed
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp dried thyme or rosemary
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 1 tbsp Greek yoghurt + 1 tsp Dijon mustard (sauce)
  • Salt & black pepper
Method

  4. Stir yoghurt with mustard for the sauce.
  5. Plate sausages, potatoes and PSB. Spoon over yoghurt-mustard.
A proper, low-effort Sunday roast. The tray takes care of the timing — start the potatoes first, then bring the sausages in.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
