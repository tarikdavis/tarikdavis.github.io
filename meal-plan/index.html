Breakfast
Raspberry & toasted almond overnight oats
~360 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 120ml whole milk
  • 80g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g raspberries
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. The night before, stir the oats, milk, half the yoghurt, the flaxseed and honey together in a jar. Lid on, into the fridge.
  2. In the morning, toast the flaked almonds in a dry pan for 2–3 mins until golden — watch them, they catch fast.
  3. Crush half the raspberries into the oats so they turn pink, and loosen with a splash more milk if it's stiff.
  4. Top with the rest of the yoghurt, the whole raspberries and the toasted almonds.
Overnight soaking does the same softening job as cooking porridge, cold — the oats drink up the milk and the flaxseed turns them almost creamy. Make two jars at once; they keep three days.
Lunch
Halloumi, cherry tomato & runner bean bulgur salad
~430 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g halloumi, in thick slices
  • 50g bulgur wheat (dry)
  • 100g runner beans, stringed and sliced on the angle
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Cover the bulgur with 100ml boiling water, lid on, 12 mins, then fluff and cool.
  2. Boil the runner beans 3 mins until just tender with a squeak, then refresh under cold water to hold the green.
  3. Fry the halloumi in a dry non-stick pan over medium-high heat, 2 mins a side, until deeply golden and squeaky.
  4. Fold the beans, tomatoes, mint, lemon, oil and chilli through the bulgur, season, and lay the halloumi on top.
Slice runner beans on a long diagonal — more surface catches the dressing, and the angled cut cooks through faster than a fat chunk. Pull the stringy seam off first.
Dinner
Paneer & pea curry with basmati rice
~500 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g paneer, cubed
  • 100g frozen peas
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Fry the paneer in the oil over medium-high heat until golden on several sides, then lift out.
  3. Soften the onion 6 mins, add the garlic, ginger and spices for 1 min, then the tomatoes; simmer 8 mins until split and darkened.
  4. Stir in the yoghurt off the boil so it doesn't curdle, then the peas and paneer. Warm through 3 mins and finish with coriander, over the rice.
Add the yoghurt with the pan off the fierce heat and stir it in a spoonful at a time — go too hot and it splits into grainy curds instead of a silky sauce.

Breakfast
Sweetcorn & spring onion fritters with poached egg
~380 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 120g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • 1 large egg (in the batter) + 1 for poaching
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • ½ tbsp neutral oil
  • Splash of vinegar (for poaching)
Method
  1. Mash half the sweetcorn roughly, then stir in the rest with the egg, flour, spring onions, chilli and a pinch of salt to a thick batter.
  2. Fry heaped spoonfuls in the oil over medium heat, 2–3 mins a side, until set and golden. You'll get 3 or 4.
  3. Bring a pan of water with the vinegar to a bare simmer, slide in the second egg from a cup and poach 3 mins.
  4. Stack the fritters, sit the egg on top, and serve with the raw cherry tomatoes alongside.
Mashing half the corn is the trick to fritters that hold together — the starchy paste binds the whole kernels so they don't crumble apart in the pan.
Lunch
Roasted pepper hummus & feta wrap
~420 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • 3 tbsp hummus
  • 1 red pepper
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • ¼ cucumber, in batons
  • Handful rocket
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Char the whole pepper under a hot grill, turning, until blackened all over, 12 mins. Steam in a covered bowl 5 mins, then slip off the skin and tear into strips.
  2. Warm the wrap in a dry pan for 20 secs a side so it folds without cracking.
  3. Spread the hummus over, then layer the pepper strips, cucumber, rocket and feta. Squeeze over the lemon and add the chilli.
  4. Fold in the sides and roll tightly, then cut on the diagonal.
Roasting your own pepper takes twelve minutes and tastes worlds better than a jar — but a good jarred roasted pepper, patted dry, is an honest shortcut on a busy day.
Dinner
Red lentil & tomato dhal with spinach
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 90g dried red lentils
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 100g baby spinach
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp turmeric
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 naan bread
  • Squeeze of lemon, handful coriander
Method
  1. Rinse the lentils until the water runs clear. Simmer with 400ml water for 15 mins, skimming any foam, until collapsing and soft.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion in the oil, 6 mins, then add the garlic, ginger and spices for 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Stir the spiced onion and the tomatoes into the lentils and simmer 5 mins, then wilt in the spinach.
  4. Finish with lemon and coriander, season well, and serve with the warmed naan for scooping.
Rinsing red lentils really does matter — the cloudy starch that washes off is what makes an unrinsed dhal turn gluey. Rinse until the water runs almost clear.

Breakfast
Cherry, pistachio & Greek yoghurt bowl
~350 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 150g cherries, pitted and halved
  • 1 tbsp shelled pistachios, roughly chopped
  • 20g rolled oats
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Toast the oats and pistachios in a dry pan with the cinnamon, 3 mins, until they smell nutty.
  2. Warm half the cherries in the pan for 1 min with a drop of water so they release a little syrup. Cool slightly.
  3. Spoon the yoghurt into a bowl and drizzle with the honey.
  4. Top with the warm and raw cherries and scatter the toasted oats and pistachios over.
A cherry stoner is worth the drawer space in July, but the tip of a small piping nozzle or a sturdy straw will push the stone straight through in a pinch.
Lunch
Courgette, pea & mint soup with cheddar toastie
~410 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 courgette, chopped
  • 100g frozen peas
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 400ml hot vegetable stock
  • Small handful mint
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 40g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Soften the onion in the oil, 6 mins. Add the garlic and courgette and cook 5 mins until slumped.
  2. Pour in the stock, simmer 6 mins, then add the peas for the last 2 — no longer, or you lose the fresh green.
  3. Blitz with the mint and a squeeze of lemon until smooth. Season boldly.
  4. Butter the sourdough on the outside, fill with the cheddar, and fry in a dry pan 3 mins a side until molten and crisp. Cut into soldiers for dunking.
Adding the peas at the very end keeps the soup vivid green — boil them long and the colour dulls to khaki and the sweet, grassy flavour cooks straight out.
Dinner
Roasted vegetable & feta orzo
~490 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 75g orzo (dry)
  • 1 courgette, in chunks
  • 1 red pepper, in chunks
  • 150g cherry tomatoes
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the courgette, pepper and tomatoes with the oil, oregano and garlic, and roast 25 mins until the edges catch and the tomatoes burst.
  2. Meanwhile cook the orzo in well-salted water until just tender, then drain, saving a little of the water.
  3. Tip the roasted vegetables and all their pan juices into the orzo with a splash of the reserved water.
  4. Fold through half the feta so it melts into the sauce, then scatter the rest with the basil on top.
Orzo looks like rice but behaves like pasta — salt the water as you would for spaghetti and stop it a minute early, because it keeps softening in the hot vegetables.

Breakfast
Mushroom, spinach & feta omelette
~360 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 tsp butter, ½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the oil over high heat until golden, 5 mins, then wilt the spinach through and tip both out.
  2. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt and plenty of pepper.
  3. Melt the butter in the pan over medium heat, pour in the eggs and, as they set, drag the edges in and tilt so the runny egg fills the gaps.
  4. While the top is still just soft, scatter over the mushrooms, spinach and feta, fold in half and slide onto the plate. Serve with the sourdough.
Take the omelette out of the pan while the centre still looks a touch underdone — the residual heat finishes it on the plate, and that's the line between soft and rubbery.
Lunch
Greek-style chickpea, cucumber & feta salad
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • ½ cucumber, in thick half-moons
  • 2 large vine tomatoes, in wedges
  • 50g feta, in a slab
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Small handful black olives
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • 1 wholemeal pitta
Method
  1. Salt the tomato wedges and leave 10 mins to draw out their juice.
  2. Toss the chickpeas, cucumber, onion and olives with the oil, vinegar and half the oregano.
  3. Add the tomatoes with their juices and pile onto a plate.
  4. Sit the slab of feta on top — don't crumble it — and finish with the rest of the oregano and a grind of pepper. Serve with the warmed pitta.
In a proper Greek salad the feta goes on as one thick slab, not crumbled through — it stays creamy and cool against the warm, salted tomatoes rather than dissolving into the dressing.
Dinner
Black bean & sweetcorn chilli with rice
~490 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 120g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • 1 red pepper, diced
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp smoked paprika, pinch chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, handful coriander
  • Squeeze of lime
Method
  1. Cook the rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, and rest.
  2. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil, 8 mins. Add the garlic and spices, 1 min.
  3. Tip in the tomatoes and beans with a splash of water and simmer 12 mins until thick, crushing a few beans against the pan to bind it.
  4. Char the sweetcorn in a dry pan until spotted, then stir most of it through. Serve over the rice with the yoghurt, coriander, lime and the last of the corn on top.
Crushing a few of the beans against the side of the pan releases their starch and thickens the chilli into something glossy — no need for any thickener or long reduction.

Breakfast
Plum & ricotta cinnamon toast
~370 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 100g ricotta
  • 2 ripe plums, sliced
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
  • Zest of ½ lemon
Method
  1. Toast the flaked almonds in a dry pan for 2 mins until golden. Tip out.
  2. In the same pan, warm the plum slices cut-side down for 2 mins with the honey and cinnamon until they soften and glaze.
  3. Beat the ricotta with the lemon zest and a small pinch of salt, and spread thickly on the toast.
  4. Pile the warm plums over, drizzle with the pan syrup and scatter with the almonds.
A quick warm in the pan turns even a slightly underripe plum sweet and jammy — the gentle heat breaks down the flesh and concentrates the sugars the fruit already has.
Lunch
New potato, pea & egg frittata with green salad
~430 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 150g new potatoes, halved
  • 60g frozen peas
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful salad leaves + squeeze of lemon
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the new potatoes 12 mins until tender, adding the peas for the last minute. Drain well.
  2. Beat the eggs with the spring onions, mint, a pinch of salt and plenty of pepper.
  3. Warm the oil in a small ovenproof pan, tip in the potatoes and peas, then pour the egg over and cook on a low heat 5 mins until the base sets.
  4. Finish under a hot grill 3 mins until puffed and just set. Slide out, cool a little, and serve with the dressed leaves.
A frittata wants a low, patient heat — rush it on a high flame and the base burns before the middle sets. The grill only has to finish the top, not cook the whole thing.
Dinner
Aubergine & chickpea tagine with couscous
~490 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 small aubergine, in chunks
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 50g couscous (dry)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 3 dried apricots, chopped
  • ½ tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp harissa (or to taste)
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Brown the aubergine in the oil over a good heat until soft and golden, 8 mins — it drinks oil at first but gives it back as it collapses.
  2. Add the onion, 5 mins, then the garlic, spices and harissa for 1 min.
  3. Stir in the tomatoes, chickpeas, apricots and a splash of water, and simmer 15 mins until thick and glossy.
  4. Cover the couscous with 75ml boiling water, lid on, 5 mins, then fork through. Spoon the tagine over and finish with coriander.
Don't stint on the heat when browning aubergine — a timid pan just steams it grey and spongy. You want proper colour, which is where all the deep, savoury flavour comes from.

Breakfast
Avocado & soft-boiled egg on rye with tomatoes
~370 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 ripe avocado
  • 2 slices rye bread
  • 1 large vine tomato, sliced
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • Flaky salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Lower the eggs into gently boiling water and cook 6½ mins for a set white and a just-molten yolk. Cool briefly under the cold tap, then peel.
  2. Toast the rye bread.
  3. Mash the avocado with the lemon, flaky salt and pepper, and spread thickly.
  4. Lay the tomato over, halve the eggs on top, and finish with the chilli and a little more salt.
Six and a half minutes from gently boiling water is the sweet spot for a soft-boiled egg you can peel — any less and the white is too loose to hold when you halve it.
Lunch
Caprese butter bean salad with basil & sourdough
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 2 large ripe vine tomatoes, chunked
  • Big handful basil
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Flaky salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Salt the tomatoes and leave 10 mins so they release their juice into the bowl.
  2. Add the butter beans and toss with the oil and balsamic so they soak up the tomato water.
  3. Tear the mozzarella over and tuck in the basil leaves whole.
  4. Finish with flaky salt and pepper, and serve with the sourdough to mop the dressing.
Butter beans borrow the whole idea of a caprese — soft, creamy, mild — and turn a starter into lunch. Their velvety skins soak up the tomato-and-oil dressing far better than a leaf ever could.
Dinner
Courgette, tomato & mozzarella pasta bake
~500 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 75g penne (dry)
  • 1 courgette, in half-moons
  • 250g passata
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 20g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Cook the penne 2 mins short of the packet time — it finishes in the oven — and drain.
  2. Soften the onion and courgette in the oil, 8 mins, then add the garlic and oregano for 1 min and pour in the passata. Simmer 5 mins.
  3. Toss the pasta through the sauce with half the basil and tip into a baking dish.
  4. Dot with the mozzarella, scatter the hard cheese over, and bake 20 mins until bubbling and bronzed. Rest 5 mins and finish with basil.
Undercook the pasta before it bakes — it keeps absorbing sauce in the oven, and pasta boiled to full softness first turns to mush by the time the top is golden.

Breakfast
Wholemeal blueberry & lemon pancakes with yoghurt
~390 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g wholemeal flour
  • 1 large egg
  • 80ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g blueberries
  • Zest of ½ lemon
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp butter
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, egg, milk and lemon zest to a thick batter, then fold in half the blueberries.
  2. Let the batter sit 5 mins — the baking powder starts to work and the pancakes rise higher.
  3. Fry spoonfuls in the butter over medium-low heat, 2 mins a side, until risen and set. You'll get 4 or 5.
  4. Stack with the yoghurt, scatter over the remaining blueberries and drizzle with honey.
Resting pancake batter for a few minutes lets the flour hydrate and the raising agent get going, so they cook up fluffier. Don't overmix — a few lumps make a lighter pancake than a smooth batter.
Lunch
Giant couscous salad with roasted courgette & butter beans
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g giant (pearl) couscous
  • 1 courgette, in chunks
  • ½ × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • ½ red pepper, chunked
  • 1 tbsp pine nuts
  • Handful flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • Handful rocket
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Roast the courgette and pepper with half the oil for 22 mins until caught at the edges.
  2. Toast the giant couscous in a dry pan 2 mins, then simmer in salted water 8 mins until tender with a bite. Drain.
  3. Toast the pine nuts in the same dry pan until golden — seconds, so watch them.
  4. Fold the roasted veg, butter beans, parsley, pine nuts, lemon and remaining oil through the couscous. Season and serve on the rocket.
Toasting giant couscous in a dry pan before it simmers gives it a nutty depth and stops the pearls clumping — the same trick that makes toasted pasta and rice taste of more.
Dinner
Spinach & ricotta pasta shells al forno
~500 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 12 large conchiglioni (giant pasta shells)
  • 150g ricotta
  • 150g baby spinach
  • 250g passata
  • 25g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 1 garlic clove
  • Grating of nutmeg
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Boil the shells until just pliable but still firm, 9 mins, then drain and cool on a tray so they don't tear.
  2. Wilt the spinach in a dry pan, squeeze it bone dry in a tea towel, chop, and mix with the ricotta, half the hard cheese, nutmeg and plenty of pepper.
  3. Warm the garlic in the oil 30 secs, add the passata and simmer 5 mins, then spread over the base of a small dish.
  4. Spoon the ricotta mix into each shell, sit them on the sauce, scatter the rest of the cheese over, and bake at 190°C for 25 mins until bubbling. Finish with basil.
Cool the boiled shells on a tray in a single layer before you fill them — piled hot in the colander they stick together and tear the moment you try to prise them apart.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
