Breakfast
Cherry & almond bircher muesli
~350 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 120ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g British cherries, halved and stoned
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • ½ apple, coarsely grated
  • 1 tsp honey, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. The night before: stir the oats, milk, grated apple and half the yoghurt together in a jar. Refrigerate.
  2. In the morning, loosen with a splash more milk if it's too thick.
  3. Toast the flaked almonds in a dry pan, 2 mins, shaking often.
  4. Top with the remaining yoghurt, the cherries, almonds, a thread of honey and a squeeze of lemon.
UK cherries land in late June — buy the dark, glossy ones and stone them with the flat of a knife or a clean hairpin. The grated apple sweetens the soak so you need barely any honey.
Lunch
Halloumi, new potato & green bean salad
~420 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 90g halloumi, sliced
  • 200g new potatoes, halved
  • 100g green beans, trimmed
  • Handful rocket
  • Small handful mint & dill, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp Dijon
  • Squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the new potatoes 12–15 mins until tender, adding the green beans for the last 3 mins. Drain and cool slightly.
  2. Whisk the oil, vinegar and Dijon into a dressing and toss through the warm potatoes and beans.
  3. Dry-fry the halloumi 2 mins per side until golden.
  4. Fold the rocket and herbs through the potatoes, top with the halloumi, lemon and pepper.
Dress the potatoes while they're still warm — they drink up the vinaigrette far better than cold ones. Jersey Royals and other new potatoes are at their best from late May through summer.
Dinner
Red lentil & coconut dahl with rice
~480 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g red lentils, rinsed
  • 150ml coconut milk (from a 400ml tin)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cumin, ¼ tsp turmeric
  • 100g (¼ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • Handful baby spinach, fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger and spices, 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Add the lentils, tomatoes, coconut milk and 250ml water. Simmer 18–20 mins, stirring, until the lentils collapse into a thick dahl.
  4. Wilt the spinach through at the end, season, finish with lemon and coriander. Serve over the rice.
Red lentils need no soaking and break down in under 20 minutes — keep stirring near the end so they don't catch. The leftover coconut milk freezes well in an ice-cube tray for next time.

Breakfast
Spinach & mushroom omelette on toast
~340 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 80g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Splash of milk, few chives, snipped
  • 1 tsp butter, 1 tsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the olive oil over high heat, 4 mins, until golden. Wilt the spinach through, then tip out and keep warm.
  2. Whisk the eggs with the milk and a pinch of salt.
  3. Melt the butter in the pan over medium-low, pour in the eggs, and draw the edges in as they set.
  4. While the top is still just-soft, spoon the mushrooms and spinach over one half and fold. Slide onto the toast, finish with chives and pepper.
Cook the mushrooms hard and fast in a hot pan so they colour rather than stew — crowding them is what makes them watery. Fold the omelette while the centre is still glossy; it keeps cooking off the heat.
Lunch
Greek butterbean & feta salad
~400 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin butterbeans, drained
  • 40g feta, in a slab
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Small handful mint & dill, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp dried oregano
Method
  1. Salt the tomatoes and cucumber, leave 5 mins to release their juice.
  2. Whisk the oil, vinegar and oregano, and toss through the butterbeans, tomatoes, cucumber and onion.
  3. Fold the herbs through.
  4. Sit the slab of feta on top and grind over plenty of black pepper — break it up as you eat.
Leave the feta in a slab rather than crumbling it, Greek-style — it stays creamy instead of disappearing into the dressing. Cucumber and tomatoes are at their summer best and barely need anything else.
Dinner
Paneer jalfrezi with basmati rice
~490 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g paneer, cubed
  • 1 red pepper, sliced; ½ onion, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cumin, ¼ tsp turmeric, pinch chilli
  • 150g (just under ½ tin) chopped tomatoes, 1 tsp tomato purée
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • Fresh coriander, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Fry the paneer in half the oil until golden, set aside.
  3. Stir-fry the onion and pepper in the rest of the oil over high heat, 5 mins, keeping them with some bite.
  4. Add garlic, ginger, spices and purée, 1 min, then the tomatoes. Simmer 5 mins to a thick, clingy sauce.
  5. Return the paneer, warm through, finish with lemon and coriander. Serve over the rice.
Jalfrezi is a dry, stir-fried curry — keep the heat high and the peppers crisp rather than letting it turn saucy. Frying the paneer first stops it crumbling when you fold it back in.

Breakfast
Peanut butter & banana porridge
~370 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 250ml whole milk (plus a splash of water)
  • 1 banana, half sliced, half mashed
  • 1 tbsp peanut butter
  • 1 tsp mixed seeds
  • Pinch of cinnamon, 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Stir the oats, milk, water and the mashed banana into a pan over medium heat.
  2. Cook 5–6 mins, stirring often, until thick and creamy.
  3. Stir in the peanut butter and cinnamon until it ribbons through.
  4. Top with the sliced banana, the seeds and a thread of honey.
Mashing half the banana into the porridge as it cooks sweetens it naturally and gives a silky texture — the riper the banana, the better. A pinch of salt with the oats makes everything taste rounder.
Lunch
Courgette, pea & mint soup with cheddar toast
~390 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 courgettes, sliced
  • 100g frozen peas
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 400ml vegetable stock
  • Small handful mint
  • 1 thick slice sourdough + 40g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Soften the onion in the oil, 5 mins. Add garlic and courgettes, cook 5 mins.
  2. Pour in the stock, simmer 10 mins, then add the peas for the last 3 mins to keep them vivid.
  3. Blitz smooth with the mint and a squeeze of lemon; season.
  4. Grill the cheddar on the sourdough until bubbling, cut into soldiers, and serve alongside for dunking.
Add the peas right at the end and blitz straight away — long cooking dulls both their colour and their sweetness. A squeeze of lemon at the end lifts a green soup from flat to fresh.
Dinner
Smoky black bean chilli with rice
~500 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes, 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 red pepper, diced; ½ onion, 1 carrot, diced; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp ground cumin, pinch chilli
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, fresh coriander, ½ lime
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Cook the rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Soften the onion, carrot and pepper in the oil, 8 mins. Add garlic, spices and purée, 1 min.
  3. Add the beans, tomatoes and a splash of water. Simmer 15 mins, lightly crushing some beans to thicken.
  4. Season, finish with lime. Serve over the rice with yoghurt and coriander.
Crushing a few of the beans against the side of the pan thickens the chilli without any flour. Smoked paprika is doing the heavy lifting here — it gives that deep, barbecue-like warmth.

Breakfast
Cherry & raspberry yoghurt & seed bowl
~350 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g cherries, halved and stoned
  • 75g raspberries
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds, 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 20g rolled oats
  • 2 tsp honey, pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Toast the oats, seeds and almonds in a dry pan, 4 mins, until golden and fragrant. Stir in 1 tsp honey and the cinnamon, tip out to cool.
  2. Lightly crush half the raspberries with a fork — they ripple through the yoghurt like a quick coulis.
  3. Spoon the yoghurt into a bowl, swirl in the crushed raspberries.
  4. Top with the cherries, whole raspberries, the toasted clusters and the rest of the honey.
Crushing half the berries gives you a free fruit ripple without any sugar or cooking. Add the toasted oat-and-seed mix only at the table so it stays crunchy against the cold yoghurt.
Lunch
Falafel & salad pitta with tzatziki
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 falafel (shop-bought or homemade)
  • 1 wholemeal pitta
  • 100g Greek yoghurt + ¼ cucumber, grated
  • ½ garlic clove, grated; small handful mint, chopped
  • Handful salad leaves, 4 cherry tomatoes, ¼ red onion
  • 1 tsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. For the tzatziki: squeeze the grated cucumber dry, then stir into the yoghurt with the garlic, mint and lemon. Season.
  2. Warm the falafel through (oven or pan, 8 mins) and toast the pitta until it puffs.
  3. Split the pitta and fill with the salad leaves, tomatoes and onion.
  4. Tuck in the falafel, spoon over the tzatziki, and add a little extra lemon.
Squeezing the grated cucumber dry is what stops the tzatziki going watery within minutes. Toasting the pitta until it balloons makes it far easier to split open into a pocket.
Dinner
Aubergine parmigiana with garlic bread
~500 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 aubergine, sliced into rounds
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes; ½ onion, 1 garlic clove
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, handful basil
  • 1 thick slice sourdough + ½ garlic clove, for the garlic bread
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Brush the aubergine with oil, roast 20 mins until soft and golden.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion in oil, add garlic, tomatoes and oregano, simmer 10 mins to a thick sauce.
  3. Layer aubergine, sauce, mozzarella and basil in a small dish; repeat, finishing with parmesan.
  4. Bake 20 mins until bubbling. Toast the sourdough, rub with the cut garlic clove and a little oil, and serve alongside.
Roasting the aubergine rather than frying skips the oil-soaking that makes parmigiana heavy. Let it sit 5 minutes out of the oven before serving so the layers set and slice cleanly.

Breakfast
Mixed berry baked oats
~350 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 120ml whole milk
  • 1 egg
  • 75g raspberries
  • 50g blueberries
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds, 1 tsp honey
  • ½ tsp baking powder, ½ tsp vanilla, pinch cinnamon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 180°C. Whisk the milk, egg, honey, vanilla, baking powder and cinnamon, then stir in the oats.
  2. Fold through most of the berries and tip into a small baking dish.
  3. Scatter the remaining berries and the almonds on top.
  4. Bake 22–25 mins until set and golden at the edges. Eat warm, with yoghurt if you like.
Baked oats are a make-ahead win — assemble the night before and bake in the morning, or bake a double batch and reheat slices through the week. The egg and baking powder give it an almost cake-like lift.
Lunch
Roasted pepper, chickpea & feta salad
~410 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • Handful rocket
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Small handful mint & parsley, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp cumin, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Toss the pepper and chickpeas with half the oil and the cumin; roast 20 mins until the pepper softens and the chickpeas crisp.
  2. Whisk the rest of the oil with the vinegar and lemon.
  3. Toss the warm pepper and chickpeas through the dressing with the onion.
  4. Fold in the rocket and herbs, scatter the feta over the top.
Roasting the chickpeas alongside the pepper gives them a nutty crunch that a tin straight from the cupboard can't match. Dress them while warm so they soak up the cumin vinaigrette.
Dinner
Mushroom & spinach risotto with parmesan
~480 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 75g risotto (arborio) rice
  • 150g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 500ml hot vegetable stock
  • 25g vegetarian parmesan, grated; 1 tsp butter
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the oil over high heat until golden, then set aside.
  2. Soften the onion in the same pan, 5 mins, add garlic and the rice, stirring 1 min until glossy.
  3. Add the hot stock a ladle at a time, stirring, until the rice is creamy and just-tender, about 18 mins.
  4. Stir the mushrooms back in with the spinach, then beat in the butter and parmesan off the heat. Finish with lemon and pepper.
Keep the stock hot in a separate pan — adding cold liquid stalls the cook and makes the rice gluey. The final beating-in of butter and parmesan off the heat is what gives risotto its signature creaminess.

Breakfast
Eggs Florentine on an English muffin
~370 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 English muffin, split
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 1 tsp butter, splash of milk
  • 15g mature cheddar, grated
  • Splash of vinegar for poaching, black pepper, grating of nutmeg
Method
  1. Wilt the spinach in the butter with the nutmeg, then stir in the milk and cheddar to make a quick creamed spinach. Keep warm.
  2. Poach the eggs in barely-simmering water with a splash of vinegar, 3 mins for soft yolks.
  3. Toast the muffin halves.
  4. Pile the creamed spinach onto the muffins, sit a poached egg on each, and finish with pepper.
A quick cheddar-and-nutmeg creamed spinach is a far easier stand-in for hollandaise and just as luxurious. Swirl the poaching water into a gentle vortex before sliding the egg in to keep it neat.
Lunch
Mozzarella, tomato & basil orzo salad
~410 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g orzo pasta
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Big handful fresh basil
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Black pepper, pinch of salt
Method
  1. Cook the orzo in salted water, 8–9 mins, until just tender. Drain and cool under the tap.
  2. Salt the halved tomatoes and leave 5 mins to release their juice.
  3. Toss the orzo with the tomatoes and their juice, the oil and balsamic.
  4. Fold through the torn mozzarella, basil and rocket just before eating.
Cool the orzo under the tap so it stops cooking and doesn't clump into a paste. Add the mozzarella and basil last — basil bruises and mozzarella weeps if dressed too early.
Dinner
Tofu & cashew stir-fry with rice
~480 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 1 red pepper, sliced; 1 carrot, ribboned; 2 spring onions
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • Sauce: 1½ tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp honey, 1 tsp cornflour, splash of water
  • 1 tbsp cashews, ½ lime, 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Fry the tofu in the oil until golden on all sides, then set aside.
  3. Stir-fry the pepper and carrot over high heat, 3 mins, add the garlic and ginger, 30 secs.
  4. Return the tofu, pour in the mixed sauce, and toss until glossy. Fold the spinach and spring onions through to wilt.
  5. Scatter the cashews over, finish with lime, and serve over the rice.
Press the tofu under a weighted plate for 15 minutes first — drier tofu fries golden instead of steaming grey. The teaspoon of cornflour in the sauce is what gives that takeaway-style glossy cling.

Breakfast
Pea & courgette fritters with poached egg
~360 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 small courgette, coarsely grated
  • 75g frozen peas, defrosted
  • 1 large egg (for the batter) + 1 to poach
  • 25g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 tbsp plain flour, 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful mint, chopped; handful rocket to serve
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, splash of vinegar for poaching, black pepper
Method
  1. Squeeze the grated courgette dry in a cloth. Lightly crush the peas, then mix with the courgette, one egg, the cheddar, flour, spring onions and mint; season.
  2. Fry heaped spoonfuls in the oil, 2–3 mins per side, until golden and set.
  3. Poach the second egg in barely-simmering water with a splash of vinegar, 3 mins.
  4. Stack the fritters, sit the poached egg on top, add rocket and a grind of pepper.
Wring the courgette out hard or the batter won't hold together. Crushing rather than blitzing the peas keeps little pops of sweetness through the fritters — they're at their seasonal peak right now.
Lunch
Beetroot, walnut & goat's cheese salad
~410 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g cooked beetroot (plain, not in vinegar), wedged
  • 50g soft goat's cheese, crumbled
  • Handful rocket
  • 2 tbsp walnut pieces
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Small handful mint & dill, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp honey, ½ tsp Dijon
Method
  1. Toast the walnuts in a dry pan, 2 mins, until fragrant.
  2. Whisk the oil, vinegar, honey and Dijon into a dressing.
  3. Toss the beetroot wedges and rocket through the dressing with the herbs.
  4. Scatter the goat's cheese and walnuts over, and serve with the toast.
Add the goat's cheese at the very end so it stays in soft, creamy nuggets rather than smearing pink. A little honey in the dressing balances the earthiness of the beetroot beautifully.
Dinner
Spinach & feta filo pie with salad
~490 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g baby spinach, wilted and squeezed dry
  • 120g feta, crumbled
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 4 sheets filo pastry
  • 2 spring onions, sliced; small handful dill & mint, chopped
  • Grating of nutmeg, 2 tbsp olive oil (for brushing)
  • Side salad: cherry tomatoes, cucumber, rocket, lemon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Mix the squeezed spinach with the feta, egg, spring onions, herbs and nutmeg; season (go easy on salt — feta is salty).
  2. Brush each filo sheet with oil and layer into a small dish, letting the edges overhang.
  3. Spoon in the filling, then fold the overhanging filo loosely over the top and brush with oil.
  4. Bake 28–30 mins until crisp and deep golden. Rest 5 mins, then serve with the lemon-dressed side salad.
Keep the filo under a damp tea towel as you work — it dries to a brittle crack within minutes of being exposed. Squeezing the spinach bone-dry is the difference between crisp pastry and a soggy base.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

  • Whey or pea protein, unflavoured ~30 servings
  • Extra free range eggs +6 (18 total)
  • Extra Greek yoghurt +500g (1.5kg total)
  • Cottage cheese 300g
  • Mozzarella 1 ball (125g)
  • Extra mature cheddar +50g (150g total)
  • Creatine monohydrate (optional) 300g tub, 3–5g/day

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
