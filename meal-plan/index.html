Breakfast
Strawberry, oat & Greek yoghurt smoothie bowl
~360 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g strawberries, hulled
  • ½ banana
  • 30g rolled oats
  • Splash of whole milk
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Blitz two-thirds of the strawberries with the banana, half the yoghurt, the oats and a splash of milk to a thick, spoonable purée.
  2. Pour into a bowl and swirl the rest of the yoghurt through.
  3. Slice the remaining strawberries and lay them over the top.
  4. Scatter with the mixed seeds and finish with a thread of honey.
Blending the oats straight into the base is what makes a smoothie bowl thick enough to eat with a spoon rather than drink — they swell and hold the fruit and seeds on top instead of letting them sink.
Lunch
Broad bean, pea & feta smash on sourdough
~420 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g broad beans (frozen)
  • 80g frozen peas
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Boil the broad beans and peas for 3 mins, drain, and cool briefly under the cold tap.
  2. If you have the patience, pop the grey skins off the broad beans — they'll be sweeter and greener for it.
  3. Crush them with the peas, mint, lemon and oil to a rough, chunky smash. Fold in most of the feta and season.
  4. Toast the sourdough, pile the smash on, and finish with the last of the feta and the chilli flakes.
Double-podding broad beans — slipping off the leathery skin after cooking — takes five minutes but transforms them from grey and starchy to bright, sweet and tender. Worth it while they're in season.
Dinner
Halloumi & courgette skewers with herby giant couscous
~500 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g halloumi, in chunks
  • 1 courgette, in thick half-moons
  • 100g cherry tomatoes
  • 60g giant (pearl) couscous
  • ½ red onion, in wedges
  • Small handful mint & parsley, chopped
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
Method
  1. Toast the couscous in a dry pan 2 mins, then simmer in salted water 8 mins until tender with a bite. Drain.
  2. Thread the halloumi, courgette, tomatoes and onion onto skewers, brush with half the oil and the oregano.
  3. Griddle or grill on a high heat, turning, 8–10 mins until the halloumi is bronzed and the courgette is soft and charred at the edges.
  4. Fork the herbs, lemon and remaining oil through the couscous, and sit the skewers on top.
Give halloumi a really hot pan or grill and leave it alone until it releases — poke it too early and it tears. A proper deep-gold crust is what stops it squeaking like rubber.

Breakfast
Soft scrambled eggs with tomatoes on toast
~370 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 large vine tomato, roughly chopped
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tsp butter
  • ½ tbsp olive oil
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Soften the tomato and spring onions in the oil over medium heat, 4 mins, until slumped and jammy. Tip to one side of the pan.
  2. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt. Melt the butter in the pan over low heat and pour them in.
  3. Stir slowly and patiently with a spatula, pulling the curds in from the edges — take it off the heat while still glossy and just set.
  4. Toast the sourdough, pile on the eggs and the tomatoes, and finish with plenty of pepper.
Scrambled eggs keep cooking in their own heat after they leave the pan, so pull them off while they still look a touch too soft — thirty seconds later on the plate they'll be perfect, not dry.
Lunch
Sweetcorn, black bean & avocado salad bowl
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 120g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • 1 avocado, diced
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ red onion, finely diced
  • Handful coriander
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pinch chilli flakes, handful salad leaves
Method
  1. Char the sweetcorn in a dry pan over high heat until spotted brown, 4 mins, then tip out to cool.
  2. Soak the diced red onion in the lime juice for 5 mins — it takes the raw bite off and turns it sweet and pink.
  3. Toss the black beans, corn, tomatoes and onion with the oil, chilli and most of the coriander.
  4. Fold the avocado through gently, sit it all on the leaves, and finish with the last of the coriander.
A dry pan and a fierce heat give frozen sweetcorn a smoky, roadside-barbecue char — don't stir it too often, let each batch sit and catch before you toss it.
Dinner
Chickpea, spinach & tomato curry with rice
~490 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • Squeeze of lemon, handful coriander
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil 6 mins, then add the garlic, ginger and spices for 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Add the tomatoes and chickpeas with a splash of water and simmer 12 mins, crushing a few chickpeas against the pan to thicken it.
  4. Wilt in the spinach, finish with lemon and coriander, season well, and serve over the rice.
Crushing a handful of the chickpeas against the side of the pan releases their starch and gives the sauce body — the difference between a thin, watery curry and one that clings.

Breakfast
Cottage cheese pancakes with blueberry compote
~390 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g cottage cheese
  • 1 large egg
  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • Splash of whole milk
  • 100g blueberries
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Whisk the cottage cheese, egg, flour, baking powder and a splash of milk to a thick, slightly lumpy batter.
  2. Warm half the blueberries in a small pan with the honey and a drop of water for 3 mins until they burst into a loose compote.
  3. Fry spoonfuls of batter in the butter over medium-low heat, 2 mins a side, until golden and set. You'll get 4 or 5.
  4. Stack the pancakes, spoon over the warm compote and scatter with the remaining fresh blueberries.
Cottage cheese melts into the batter as it cooks, leaving the pancakes moist and quietly high in protein — no one need know they're in there. Don't overmix; a few lumps make them lighter.
Lunch
Roasted red pepper & lentil soup with cheese toast
~410 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 red peppers, in chunks
  • 60g dried red lentils
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 400ml hot vegetable stock
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 40g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Roast the peppers with half the oil for 25 mins until soft and blackening at the edges.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion in the rest of the oil, 6 mins, add the garlic and paprika for 1 min, then the rinsed lentils and stock. Simmer 18 mins.
  3. Tip the roasted peppers into the pan and blitz smooth. Loosen with hot water if it's thick, and season well.
  4. Toast the sourdough, pile on the cheddar and grill until bubbling. Cut into fingers for dipping.
Roasting the peppers before they go in deepens the whole soup — the caught, blistered edges bring a smoky sweetness that a raw pepper simmered in stock never develops.
Dinner
Courgette & pea risotto with parmesan
~500 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g risotto (arborio) rice
  • 1 courgette, half diced, half coarsely grated
  • 80g frozen peas
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 600ml hot vegetable stock
  • 25g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 1 tsp butter, 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon, handful basil
Method
  1. Soften the onion in the oil, 6 mins, add the garlic and the diced courgette for 3 mins, then the rice, stirring 1 min until glassy.
  2. Add the hot stock a ladle at a time, stirring often, letting each go in before the next — about 18 mins.
  3. Halfway, stir in the grated courgette; it melts down and makes the risotto silky. Add the peas for the last 3 mins.
  4. Off the heat, beat in the butter, cheese and lemon, cover 2 mins, then serve loose and creamy with the basil.
Grating half the courgette in is a quiet trick — it dissolves into the rice and does the job a knob of extra butter would, giving you a creamy risotto without piling on the fat.

Breakfast
Baked eggs with peppers & tomato
~380 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ½ onion, sliced; 1 garlic clove
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil in a small pan, 8 mins, until sweet. Add the garlic and spices for 1 min.
  2. Pour in the tomatoes with a splash of water and simmer 8 mins until thick.
  3. Make two wells in the sauce, crack in the eggs, cover, and cook gently 5–6 mins until the whites are just set and the yolks still soft.
  4. Scatter with coriander and mop up with the toasted sourdough.
Keep the heat low once the eggs go in and put a lid on — you're steaming the tops gently while the bases set in the sauce, so the yolks stay runny instead of overcooking.
Lunch
Falafel & salad wrap with tzatziki
~440 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 falafel
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • ¼ cucumber, grated and squeezed dry
  • 3 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 large vine tomato, sliced
  • Handful salad leaves
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • Squeeze of lemon, drizzle of olive oil
Method
  1. Warm the falafel through in a hot oven or dry pan for 8 mins until crisp outside and hot in the middle.
  2. Stir the grated cucumber, mint, lemon and a pinch of salt into the yoghurt for a quick tzatziki.
  3. Warm the wrap 20 secs a side so it folds without cracking, then spread the tzatziki down the middle.
  4. Layer the leaves, tomato and falafel, crushing the falafel a little, drizzle with oil, and roll up tightly.
Squeeze the grated cucumber hard in a clean tea towel before it goes in — it holds a surprising amount of water, and skipping this is what turns a thick tzatziki into a runny puddle.
Dinner
Roasted aubergine & tomato pasta alla norma
~500 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 aubergine, in chunks
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 75g rigatoni or penne (dry)
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 25g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp dried oregano, pinch chilli flakes
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the aubergine with 1½ tbsp oil and roast 25 mins until deeply soft and golden.
  2. Warm the garlic and chilli in the rest of the oil 30 secs, add the tomatoes and oregano, and simmer 10 mins to a thick sauce.
  3. Cook the pasta in well-salted water until just al dente, saving a mugful of the water before draining.
  4. Fold the roasted aubergine and pasta through the sauce with a splash of the water, then most of the cheese and basil. Finish with the rest on top.
Roasting the aubergine instead of frying it saves a small ocean of oil and still gives you that meltingly soft, savoury result — the dry oven heat concentrates the flavour rather than diluting it.

Breakfast
Banana & peanut butter porridge with seeds
~380 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 250ml whole milk
  • 1 banana
  • 1 tbsp peanut butter
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Simmer the oats with the milk and a splash of water over low heat, stirring, 5 mins until thick and creamy.
  2. Mash half the banana and stir it through the porridge with the cinnamon so it turns sweet and silky.
  3. Swirl the peanut butter in loosely so it stays as ripples rather than fully melting.
  4. Slice the rest of the banana over the top, scatter with the seeds and finish with the honey.
Stirring mashed banana into porridge sweetens it naturally, so you need barely any honey — the riper the banana, the sweeter and more caramel-like the result.
Lunch
Spiced chickpea & spinach flatbread with yoghurt
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 1 flatbread (or large wrap)
  • 3 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 garlic clove
  • ½ tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon, pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Pat the chickpeas dry, then fry in the oil over high heat with the cumin and paprika, 6 mins, until crisp and popping.
  2. Add the garlic for the last minute, then wilt the spinach through off the heat.
  3. Stir the lemon and a pinch of salt into the yoghurt and spread over the warmed flatbread.
  4. Pile the spiced chickpeas and spinach on top, scatter with chilli, and fold or eat open.
Drying the chickpeas thoroughly before they hit the pan is the whole secret to getting them crisp — any surface water just steams them, and they'll stay soft instead of crackly.
Dinner
Quorn & vegetable stir-fry with noodles
~490 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g Quorn pieces
  • 1 nest egg noodles
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 80g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove; 2cm ginger, grated
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil, 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the noodles to the packet time, drain, and toss with a drop of the sesame oil so they don't clump.
  2. Get a wok or big pan smoking hot with the neutral oil. Fry the Quorn 4 mins until browned at the edges, then lift out.
  3. Stir-fry the pepper and sweetcorn 3 mins, keeping them moving, then add the garlic and ginger for 30 secs.
  4. Return the Quorn and noodles, pour over the soy, honey and remaining sesame oil, and toss over high heat 2 mins. Finish with the spring onions.
A stir-fry lives or dies by the heat — get the pan properly smoking and cook in stages so everything sears rather than stews. A crowded, cool pan just weeps water and goes soggy.

Breakfast
Big veggie breakfast — poached egg, mushrooms, tomatoes & beans
~390 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large egg
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, halved
  • 1 large vine tomato, halved
  • ½ × 400g tin baked beans
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Splash of vinegar (for poaching)
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Warm the baked beans gently in a small pan while everything else cooks.
  2. Fry the mushrooms and the tomato cut-side down in the oil over medium-high heat, 6 mins, until the mushrooms are golden and the tomato collapses.
  3. Bring a pan of water with the vinegar to a bare simmer, slide the egg in from a cup and poach 3 mins.
  4. Toast the sourdough, plate up the beans, mushrooms and tomato, sit the egg on top, and grind over plenty of pepper.
Give the mushrooms room and don't salt them until they're golden — salt too early draws out their water and they braise grey in the pan instead of frying to a nutty brown.
Lunch
Panzanella — tomato & sourdough salad with mozzarella
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 slices sourdough (slightly stale is ideal)
  • 3 large ripe vine tomatoes, chunked
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • Big handful basil
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • Flaky salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Tear the sourdough into chunks and toast in a dry pan or hot oven until crisp and golden.
  2. Salt the tomatoes in a big bowl and leave 10 mins to release their juices, then whisk the oil and vinegar into that juice.
  3. Add the toasted bread and toss so it soaks up the dressing and softens slightly — 5 mins is enough.
  4. Fold in the cucumber, onion, mozzarella and basil, season with flaky salt and pepper, and serve at room temperature.
Panzanella is the great rescue for yesterday's bread — stale, sturdy sourdough drinks up the tomato dressing and stays chewy, where fresh soft bread just dissolves into mush.
Dinner
Feta, rice & pine nut stuffed peppers
~500 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large red peppers, halved and deseeded
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, quartered
  • 1 tbsp pine nuts
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp dried oregano, handful mint or parsley
Method
  1. Cook the rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, and drain. Heat oven to 200°C.
  2. Soften the onion in half the oil, 6 mins, add the garlic and oregano 1 min, then fold through the rice, tomatoes, herbs and most of the feta.
  3. Sit the pepper halves in a dish, brush with the rest of the oil, and spoon the filling in.
  4. Scatter the pine nuts and remaining feta over, cover with foil, and bake 25 mins, then uncover 10 mins more until the peppers are soft and the tops are golden.
Cover the peppers with foil for the first stretch so they steam soft, then uncover to colour the tops — go straight in uncovered and the filling scorches long before the pepper itself gives in.

Breakfast
Baked eggs florentine on a toasted muffin
~380 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 1 wholemeal English muffin
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Grating of nutmeg, black pepper
Method
  1. Wilt the spinach in the butter with the nutmeg, 2 mins, then squeeze out any water and season.
  2. Bring a pan of water to a bare simmer and poach the eggs 3 mins for soft yolks.
  3. Toast and butter the muffin halves, and pile the spinach on each.
  4. Sit a poached egg on top, spoon over the yoghurt loosened with the lemon as a quick cheat's hollandaise, and finish with pepper.
Lemon-spiked Greek yoghurt stands in beautifully for hollandaise — all the tang and richness against the egg, none of the split-sauce anxiety, and a good deal more protein into the bargain.
Lunch
Warm new potato, green bean & egg salad
~430 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 150g new potatoes, halved
  • 100g fine green beans, trimmed
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Small handful black olives
  • Handful salad leaves
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard, 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Boil the new potatoes 12 mins, adding the green beans for the last 4, until both are just tender. Drain.
  2. Meanwhile boil the eggs 8 mins for just-set yolks, cool under the cold tap, peel and quarter.
  3. Whisk the mustard, vinegar and oil with a pinch of salt into a sharp dressing.
  4. Toss the warm potatoes and beans in the dressing, then fold through the tomatoes, olives and leaves and top with the eggs.
Dress the potatoes while they're still warm — they drink up the mustardy vinaigrette as they cool, seasoning from the inside out, where a cold potato just lets it slide off.
Dinner
Mushroom & spinach lasagne
~510 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 lasagne sheets
  • 150g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 150g baby spinach
  • 250g passata
  • 150g whole milk + 15g butter + 1 tbsp flour (white sauce)
  • 25g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, grating of nutmeg
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the oil over high heat until golden, 6 mins, add the onion and garlic for 5 mins, then the passata; simmer 5 mins and wilt in the spinach.
  2. For the white sauce, melt the butter, stir in the flour 1 min, then whisk in the milk over low heat until thickened. Season with nutmeg and pepper.
  3. Layer in a small dish: mushroom ragù, lasagne sheets, white sauce — twice — finishing with white sauce and the grated cheese.
  4. Bake at 190°C for 30 mins until the top is bronzed and bubbling. Rest 10 mins before cutting so it holds its shape.
Let a lasagne rest a full ten minutes out of the oven before you cut it — straight from the heat the layers slide apart in a molten heap, but a short rest lets them set into clean slices.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
