Breakfast
Cottage cheese & strawberry breakfast bowl
~310 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g reduced-fat cottage cheese
  • 100g British strawberries, hulled and halved
  • 30g granola
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon, zest of ½ lemon
Method
  1. Spoon cottage cheese into a bowl. Loosen with a splash of milk if it feels too stiff.
  2. Top with strawberries, scatter granola and seeds.
  3. Drizzle honey, dust with cinnamon, finish with a fine grating of lemon zest.
Cottage cheese gets a bad rap — but treated like Greek yoghurt with sweet toppings, it delivers nearly twice the protein per gram. Lemon zest cuts the tang.
Lunch
New potato, broad bean & egg niçoise
~410 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Jersey royal or new potatoes, scrubbed
  • 100g podded broad beans (fresh or frozen)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 30g watercress
  • 8 cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Small handful black olives, pitted
  • 1 tsp capers, drained
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp Dijon, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Boil potatoes 12 mins until tender. In the last 6 mins, lower the eggs into the same pan. In the last 2 mins, add broad beans.
  2. Drain everything. Cool the eggs under cold water 1 min, peel and halve. Pop broad beans out of their grey skins for the bright inner beans.
  3. Slice potatoes thickly. Whisk oil with Dijon, lemon and a pinch of salt.
  4. Layer watercress, potatoes, broad beans, tomatoes, olives and capers. Top with the eggs.
  5. Spoon dressing over, plenty of pepper.
One pan does it all — potatoes, eggs and broad beans on a single timer. The eggs taste cleaner cooked alongside the potatoes than alone.
Dinner
Pea, mint & goat's cheese risotto
~480 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g arborio risotto rice (dry)
  • 150g frozen petits pois
  • 50g soft goat's cheese
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • ½ onion, finely diced
  • 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
  • 400ml hot vegetable stock
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp butter
  • Zest & juice of ½ lemon, small handful fresh mint
Method
  1. Soften onion in oil over low heat, 5 mins. Add garlic, 30 seconds.
  2. Stir in rice, toast 1 min. Add a ladle of stock and stir until absorbed.
  3. Continue ladling stock and stirring, 15 mins, until rice is tender with the slightest bite.
  4. Stir in peas in the last 3 mins. Off heat, beat in butter, parmesan, lemon zest & juice. Season.
  5. Spoon into bowls, crumble goat's cheese on top, tear over mint.
Add the peas at the very end — straight from frozen. They thaw, brighten and stay sweet rather than going olive-grey.

Breakfast
Mushroom & spinach omelette with sourdough
~340 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Big handful baby spinach
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, finely grated
  • 1 tsp butter, ½ tsp olive oil
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Fresh chives or parsley, black pepper
Method
  1. Fry mushrooms in butter and oil over high heat, 4 mins, until golden. Toss in spinach for 30 seconds to wilt. Tip onto a plate.
  2. Whisk eggs with a splash of water, salt and pepper.
  3. Wipe the pan, set back over medium. Pour in eggs, swirl, pull edges in with a spatula 1 min until just set on the base but glossy on top.
  4. Scatter mushroom-spinach down the middle, sprinkle parmesan. Fold one side over.
  5. Slide onto a plate, scatter chives. Serve with toast.
A French-style fold (rather than flip) keeps the inside silky. Pull the omelette off when the top still looks slightly wet — residual heat finishes it perfectly.
Lunch
Beetroot, lentil & feta bowl with rocket
~400 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin green or brown lentils, drained
  • 2 cooked beetroots (vacuum-packed), sliced
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds, toasted
  • 1 tsp Dijon, 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Whisk Dijon, olive oil, balsamic and lemon with a pinch of salt.
  2. In a bowl, toss lentils with half the dressing.
  3. Layer rocket, then lentils, then beetroot.
  4. Crumble feta over, scatter toasted pumpkin seeds.
  5. Spoon over remaining dressing, plenty of pepper.
Toast the pumpkin seeds in a dry pan for 90 seconds — they go from soft to nutty and crunchy. Make extra; they keep in a jar for a week.
Dinner
Courgette, lemon & ricotta tagliatelle
~480 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g tagliatelle (dry)
  • 1 large courgette, half-moon sliced
  • 100g ricotta
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, finely grated
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely sliced
  • Zest & juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, pinch chilli flakes
  • Fresh basil, salt & black pepper
Method
  1. Cook tagliatelle per packet. Drain, reserve a ladle of pasta water.
  2. Meanwhile, heat oil in a pan. Fry courgette over high heat 5 mins until golden.
  3. Add garlic and chilli, 30 seconds. Season.
  4. Off heat, stir in ricotta, lemon zest & juice, half the parmesan and a splash of pasta water — it'll loosen into a creamy sauce.
  5. Toss in pasta, adjust with more water if needed. Plate up, scatter remaining parmesan and basil.
Ricotta is your shortcut to creamy pasta without any double cream — and lemon keeps it from going claggy. Add it off the heat or it splits.

Breakfast
Bircher muesli with grated apple & yoghurt
~340 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 small apple, grated (skin on)
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey, pinch of cinnamon
  • Squeeze of lemon (to stop the apple browning)
Method
  1. The night before: combine oats, milk, half the yoghurt and cinnamon in a jar. Stir well.
  2. Cover, refrigerate overnight (or at least 4 hours).
  3. In the morning: grate apple, mix immediately with a squeeze of lemon.
  4. Stir apple into the oat base. Top with remaining yoghurt, seeds, drizzle of honey.
Bircher is overnight oats' Swiss grandfather — invented as hospital food in 1900. Grating (not chopping) the apple is the technique that makes it.
Lunch
Halloumi, cucumber & couscous tabbouleh
~420 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g couscous (dry)
  • 100g halloumi, sliced into 1cm slabs
  • ½ cucumber, diced
  • 6 cherry tomatoes, quartered
  • ½ red onion, finely diced
  • Big handful fresh parsley, chopped
  • Small handful fresh mint, chopped
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon, salt & pepper
Method
  1. Pour 90ml boiling water over couscous in a bowl. Cover for 5 mins, fluff with a fork.
  2. Meanwhile, dry-fry halloumi over medium-high heat, 2 mins each side until golden.
  3. Toss couscous with cucumber, tomato, red onion, parsley and mint.
  4. Whisk olive oil with lemon, drizzle over and toss again. Season generously.
  5. Plate the tabbouleh, top with halloumi.
A proper tabbouleh is more herbs than grain — pile in the parsley until the bowl looks green, not beige. The couscous is just a supporting cast.
Dinner
Aubergine & chickpea tagine with bulgur
~470 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 medium aubergine, cubed
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, thumb of ginger
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp ground coriander, 1 tsp paprika
  • 1 tsp harissa (or chilli paste)
  • 60g bulgur wheat (dry)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon, fresh parsley
Method
  1. Heat oil in a wide pan. Brown aubergine over high heat 6 mins until golden — it'll soak up the oil then release it again. Tip out.
  2. Soften onion 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger, spices and harissa, 1 min.
  3. Return aubergine. Add chickpeas, tomatoes, plus a splash of water. Simmer 20 mins until thick and rich.
  4. Meanwhile, cover bulgur with 150ml boiling water. Lid on, 12 mins. Fluff with fork.
  5. Squeeze lemon over the tagine, scatter parsley. Serve over bulgur.
Brown the aubergine hard at the start — it transforms from spongy to silky and absorbs the spices instead of staying watery. Don't crowd the pan.

Smashed avocado & cottage cheese on rye
~340 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 ripe avocado
  • 100g cottage cheese
  • 2 slices rye bread, toasted (or 3 rye crispbreads)
  • Pinch of chilli flakes
  • Juice of ½ lime
  • Sea salt, cracked black pepper
  • Fresh chives or coriander to scatter
Method

  3. Spread the avocado across the toast.
  4. Spoon cottage cheese over the top, scatter herbs and a final pinch of chilli.
Cottage cheese with avocado tastes nothing like cottage cheese alone — the curds get coated in oil and lime, and the protein is doing real work. A weekday-quick power breakfast.
Lunch
Egg-fried rice with peas & spring onion
~420 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g brown rice (dry) — or 150g cold cooked rice
  • 2 large eggs, lightly beaten
  • 100g frozen petits pois
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
  • 1 small piece ginger, grated
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (groundnut or sunflower)
Method
  1. Cook rice ahead and chill (ideally day before). If using freshly-cooked, spread on a tray and cool 15 mins.
  2. Heat neutral oil in a wok or non-stick pan over high heat. Add garlic and ginger, 20 seconds.
  3. Push to one side, pour eggs into the space, scramble loosely.
  4. Tip in rice and peas, toss to coat in oil. Stir-fry 3 mins, breaking up rice clumps.
  5. Splash with soy and sesame oil, scatter most of the spring onions, toss. Plate up, top with remaining spring onion.
Cold rice is non-negotiable — fresh rice goes gluey in the wok. Cook it the night before, or use a microwave pouch and cool fast on a tray.
Dinner
Baked feta with cherry tomatoes & orzo
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g feta block (single slab)
  • 300g cherry tomatoes
  • 3 garlic cloves, smashed (skin on)
  • 80g orzo (dry)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, pinch of chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Fresh basil leaves, black pepper
  • Handful rocket on the side
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Put feta block in a small baking dish, surround with tomatoes and garlic.
  2. Drizzle oil over everything, scatter oregano and chilli. Bake 25 mins until tomatoes burst and feta softens.
  3. Meanwhile, cook orzo per packet (~9 mins). Drain, reserve a splash of water.
  4. Squeeze garlic out of skins, mash into the feta-tomato mix. Stir to a sauce.
  5. Tip in orzo, toss to coat. Loosen with reserved water. Scatter basil. Plate with rocket on the side.
The trick is to wait until the tomatoes burst — they release their juice and emulsify with the molten feta into something resembling a creamy sauce. Patience pays.

Banana, oat & yoghurt smoothie bowl
~330 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 ripe banana, sliced and frozen overnight
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 30g rolled oats
  • 100ml whole milk
  • 1 tbsp almond butter
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • ½ tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. The night before: slice banana, freeze on a tray.
  2. In the morning: blitz frozen banana with yoghurt, milk, oats, vanilla and cinnamon until thick and creamy.
  3. Spoon into a bowl. Top with almond butter and mixed seeds.
Freezing the banana the night before is the difference between a smoothie and a soft-serve. Slice before freezing — solid bananas wreck blenders.
Lunch
Tomato & white bean bruschetta board
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 3 ripe vine tomatoes, diced
  • 2 slices sourdough, toasted
  • 2 garlic cloves (1 to rub, 1 to mash)
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, shaved
  • Handful fresh basil leaves
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Handful rocket on the side
Method
  1. Mash cannellini beans roughly with 1 garlic clove, half the oil and a pinch of salt — leave some texture.
  2. Toast sourdough. Rub each slice with the cut face of the remaining garlic clove.
  3. Toss tomatoes with the remaining oil, balsamic, salt and pepper. Let sit 5 mins.
  4. Spread bean mash thickly on toast. Spoon tomato salad over the top.
  5. Top with basil and shaved parmesan. Rocket on the side.
Salt the tomatoes first and let them sit — they release sweet juice that becomes the dressing. Pour every drop over the toast at the end.
Dinner
Spinach & ricotta cannelloni in tomato sauce
~490 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 8 cannelloni tubes (dry)
  • 200g ricotta
  • 250g baby spinach
  • 1 large egg
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 small onion, 2 garlic cloves
  • 30g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • Pinch of nutmeg, 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Fresh basil, olive oil, salt & black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Wilt spinach in a dry pan, 2 mins. Cool, squeeze out water, chop.
  2. Mix ricotta with egg, spinach, half the parmesan, nutmeg, salt & pepper.
  3. Soften onion in oil 5 mins. Add garlic and oregano, 1 min. Pour in tomatoes plus 100ml water, simmer 10 mins, season.
  4. Spoon a third of the sauce into a baking dish. Pipe or spoon the spinach mix into cannelloni tubes (a teaspoon works fine, just push it in).
  5. Lay stuffed tubes in dish, pour remaining sauce over, scatter remaining parmesan. Bake 30 mins until pasta is tender. Scatter basil.
Use the back of a teaspoon to push the filling into the tubes — much easier than a piping bag, and the cannelloni will soften in the sauce as it bakes anyway.

Breakfast
Eggs Florentine with yoghurt-Dijon sauce
~380 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 wholemeal English muffin, split and toasted
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, finely grated
  • 1 tsp butter, splash white wine vinegar, pinch of nutmeg
  • Squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Wilt spinach in a hot pan with butter and a pinch of nutmeg, 1 min. Season, squeeze out excess water with a wooden spoon.
  2. Whisk yoghurt with Dijon, lemon, parmesan, a splash of warm water and pepper — that's your cheat's hollandaise.
  3. Simmer water with vinegar in a small pan. Crack each egg into a cup, slide into a gentle swirl. Poach 3 mins.
  4. Toast the muffin halves. Pile spinach on each.
  5. Lift eggs out with a slotted spoon, drain on kitchen paper, set on the spinach. Spoon yoghurt sauce over, cracked pepper.
Yoghurt + Dijon + a pinch of parmesan tastes uncannily like hollandaise without the butter mountain or fear of splitting. The protein bump comes for free.

Dinner
Mushroom, leek & cheddar savoury crumble
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 large leek, sliced
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 200ml whole milk
  • 1 tbsp plain flour (for the sauce) + 30g flour (for crumble)
  • 60g rolled oats
  • 30g cold butter, cubed (plus 1 tsp for cooking)
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard, fresh thyme
  • Handful spinach (optional, to fold in)
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Soften leek in 1 tsp butter, 5 mins. Add mushrooms, fry over high heat 5 mins until golden and water has cooked off. Season.
  2. Sprinkle 1 tbsp flour, stir 1 min. Pour in milk gradually, whisking, to make a thin white sauce. Bubble 2 mins. Stir in Dijon, thyme and a handful of spinach if using.
  3. Tip into a baking dish.
  4. Rub 30g flour, 60g oats, 30g cold butter and most of the cheddar between your fingers until rubbly. Scatter over the mushroom mix. Top with remaining cheddar.
  5. Bake 25 mins until top is golden and edges bubble.
Cold butter, light hands. Treat it like a sweet crumble topping — rub for 60 seconds max, then stop. You want pebbles, not paste.

Breakfast
Cottage cheese pancakes with strawberries
~360 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g cottage cheese
  • 2 large eggs
  • 40g plain flour
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract, pinch of salt
  • 1 tsp butter for the pan
  • 100g British strawberries, sliced
  • 1 tsp honey to serve
Method
  1. Whisk cottage cheese with eggs and vanilla until smooth-ish (some curds are fine).
  2. Fold in flour, baking powder, salt to a thick batter.
  3. Heat a non-stick pan over medium-low. Melt a little butter.
  4. Drop heaped tablespoons of batter into the pan. Cook 2 mins until bubbles form, flip and cook 1 min more.
  5. Stack on a plate. Top with strawberries, drizzle of honey.
Cottage cheese in pancake batter is the protein hack of the year — over 20g per stack without tasting "healthy". The curds disappear into a tender, almost cheesecake-like texture.
Lunch
Asparagus, pea & poached egg on toast
~370 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large egg
  • 6 British asparagus spears, woody ends snapped
  • 60g frozen petits pois
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, shaved
  • 1 tsp butter, splash white wine vinegar (for poaching)

Method

A farewell to British asparagus before its season ends (late June). The crushed pea base is half-puree, half-salad — and a useful technique for any green vegetable on toast.
Dinner
Vegetable & paneer biryani with cucumber raita
~490 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g paneer, cubed
  • 80g basmati rice (dry)
  • 200g cauliflower florets
  • 50g frozen peas
  • 1 onion, sliced; 2 garlic cloves, thumb of ginger
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp turmeric, 1 tsp garam masala, 1 cinnamon stick
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 100g Greek yoghurt, ½ cucumber, fresh mint, fresh coriander (for raita)
  • Squeeze of lemon, salt
Method
  1. Soak rice 15 mins. Cook in 200ml salted water with cinnamon stick, 12 mins, lid on, until just done. Rest.
  2. Heat oil. Fry paneer cubes 3 mins until golden on all sides. Lift out.
  3. Same pan: cumin seeds 30 sec, then onion 6 mins until brown at edges. Add garlic, ginger, turmeric, garam masala, 1 min.
  4. Add cauliflower with a splash of water, lid on, 8 mins until just tender. Add peas, 2 mins.
  5. Fold in rice, paneer and a squeeze of lemon. Heat through.
  6. For raita: grate cucumber, squeeze out water. Stir into yoghurt with chopped mint, salt.
  7. Serve biryani topped with coriander, raita alongside.
Pan-fry the paneer first — straight from the pack it's a bit squeaky and bland, but a quick golden crust transforms the texture and lets it carry the spice.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
