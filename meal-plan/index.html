Breakfast
Raspberry & almond overnight oats with Greek yoghurt
~370 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 120ml whole milk
  • 100g raspberries
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. The night before, stir the oats, half the yoghurt, the milk and a little honey together in a jar, cover and chill overnight.
  2. In the morning the oats will have drunk up the milk and turned thick and creamy — loosen with a splash more milk if you like.
  3. Crush half the raspberries through the oats so they streak it pink, and spoon the rest of the yoghurt on top.
  4. Scatter over the whole raspberries, the flaked almonds and seeds, and finish with a thread of honey.
Overnight oats are a make-ahead breakfast in the truest sense — five minutes the night before buys you a proper, protein-rich meal you can eat straight from the fridge with no cooking at all.
Lunch
Courgette, sweetcorn & feta fritters with cherry tomato salad
~430 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 courgette, coarsely grated
  • 80g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • 1 large egg
  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Small handful mint
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Salt the grated courgette, leave 5 mins, then squeeze the water out hard in a clean tea towel — this is what keeps the fritters crisp.
  2. Mix the courgette, sweetcorn, feta, egg, flour and spring onions into a thick batter and season.
  3. Fry heaped spoonfuls in the oil over medium heat, 3 mins a side, until golden and set — you'll get 4 or 5.
  4. Toss the tomatoes and mint with the lemon and a little oil, and serve alongside the warm fritters.
Wringing the water out of grated courgette is the single most important step — skip it and the batter turns sloppy and the fritters steam soft instead of frying to a crisp, lacy edge.
Dinner
Paneer & summer vegetable jalfrezi with rice
~500 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 120g paneer, cubed
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 1 courgette, in half-moons
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, sliced; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Fry the paneer in half the oil over medium-high heat until golden on all sides, then lift out.
  3. Soften the onion and pepper in the rest of the oil, 6 mins, add the garlic, ginger and spices for 1 min, then the courgette and tomatoes. Simmer 10 mins.
  4. Return the paneer to warm through, season, finish with coriander, and serve over the rice.
Browning the paneer before it goes into the sauce gives it a firmer, tastier crust that holds up in the pan — dropped in raw it stays pale and can break apart into the gravy.

Breakfast
Mushroom & Cheddar omelette with grilled tomatoes
~375 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 large vine tomato, halved
  • 1 tsp butter
  • ½ tbsp olive oil
  • Small handful parsley
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the oil over medium-high heat until golden, 5 mins, and grill or fry the tomato halves cut-side down alongside until soft.
  2. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt. Melt the butter in a non-stick pan over medium heat and pour them in.
  3. As the edges set, draw them into the middle and tilt the pan so the raw egg runs underneath — 2 mins, until just set but still glossy.
  4. Scatter the mushrooms and cheese over one half, fold over, and slide onto the plate with the tomatoes, parsley and plenty of pepper.
Take an omelette off the heat while the top still looks a fraction underdone — it carries on cooking as you fold it, so what looks perfect in the pan is usually a touch dry on the plate.
Lunch
Butter bean, roasted pepper & rocket salad with pesto
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 1 red pepper
  • Big handful rocket
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • 1 tbsp green pesto
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp pine nuts
Method
  1. Char the whole pepper under a hot grill, turning, until blackened all over, then seal in a bowl 5 mins and peel off the skin. Tear into strips.
  2. Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan until golden — a minute, watching closely.
  3. Loosen the pesto with the oil and lemon into a dressing.
  4. Toss the butter beans, peppers, tomatoes and cucumber in the dressing, pile onto the rocket, and scatter with the toasted pine nuts.
Blistering and peeling a pepper yourself gives you something silkier and sweeter than any jar — the charred skin slips off easily once it's steamed a few minutes under a bowl.
Dinner
Roasted tomato & cannellini bean orzo with basil
~490 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g cherry tomatoes
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 75g orzo (dry)
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 25g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp dried oregano, pinch chilli flakes
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Roast the tomatoes with 1½ tbsp oil, the oregano and chilli for 20 mins until bursting and jammy.
  2. Cook the orzo in well-salted water until just al dente, saving a mugful of the water before draining.
  3. Warm the garlic in the rest of the oil 30 secs, add the roasted tomatoes, crushing some, and the cannellini beans. Loosen with a little pasta water.
  4. Fold the orzo through with most of the cheese and basil, adding pasta water until glossy. Finish with the rest on top.
Roasting the tomatoes hard concentrates them into a sweet, almost sauce-like jam — far more flavour than simmering raw ones, and the caught edges bring a depth you can't rush on the hob.

Breakfast
Greek yoghurt bowl with granola, plum & toasted seeds
~380 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 40g granola
  • 2 ripe plums, sliced
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Warm the plum slices in a small dry pan with the honey and cinnamon for 3 mins until they soften and turn syrupy at the edges.
  2. Spoon the yoghurt into a bowl and swirl the warm plums through it.
  3. Scatter the granola and seeds over the top for crunch.
  4. Finish with a last drizzle of honey and any plum syrup from the pan.
Add the granola only just before eating — stirred in early it sinks into the yoghurt and goes soft, where scattered on top at the last moment it stays properly crunchy against the cool yoghurt.
Lunch
Hummus, roasted courgette & pepper wrap
~420 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 tbsp hummus
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • ½ courgette, in ribbons
  • ½ red pepper, sliced
  • Handful baby spinach
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the courgette and pepper with the oil and roast 20 mins until soft and catching at the edges.
  2. Warm the wrap 20 secs a side so it folds without cracking, then spread the hummus down the middle.
  3. Layer the spinach, roasted veg and red onion on top, squeeze over the lemon and scatter with chilli.
  4. Fold in the sides and roll up tightly, then cut in half on the diagonal.
Roast a whole tray of courgette and pepper at once and keep the extra in the fridge — they turn tomorrow's sandwich or salad into a two-minute job and only get more mellow overnight.
Dinner
Sweetcorn, courgette & black bean quesadillas
~500 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large tortilla wraps
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 100g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • ½ courgette, diced
  • 60g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful coriander
  • Greek yoghurt to serve
Method
  1. Fry the courgette and sweetcorn in the oil over high heat until charred at the edges, 5 mins, add the spices and black beans for 2 mins, crushing some of the beans, then the spring onions and coriander.
  2. Lay a wrap in a dry pan, scatter over half the cheese, spread the filling, then the rest of the cheese, and top with the second wrap.
  3. Cook over medium heat 3 mins until golden, press down, then flip carefully and crisp the other side.
  4. Slide out, rest a minute so the cheese sets, then cut into wedges and serve with a spoon of yoghurt.
Let the quesadilla sit for a minute before you cut it — straight out of the pan the melted cheese slides and the filling spills, but a short rest sets it just enough to slice into clean wedges.

Breakfast
Scrambled tofu with tomatoes & spinach on toast
~360 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g firm tofu
  • 1 large vine tomato, chopped
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Drain the tofu and press it between kitchen paper to squeeze out water, then crumble it roughly with your hands.
  2. Soften the spring onions and tomato in the oil, 4 mins, then add the crumbled tofu and turmeric, stirring, 4 mins until hot and golden.
  3. Wilt the spinach through at the end and season well with salt and pepper.
  4. Pile onto the toasted sourdough and finish with chilli flakes.
Turmeric does the heavy lifting here — a half-teaspoon turns pale tofu a convincing scrambled-egg gold, and a good pinch of salt at the end is what wakes the whole thing up.
Lunch
Greek-style salad with feta, chickpeas & olives
~430 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g feta, in a slab
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 3 large vine tomatoes, chunked
  • ½ cucumber, chunked
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • Small handful black olives
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
Method
  1. Pile the tomatoes, cucumber, red onion, olives and chickpeas into a big bowl.
  2. Whisk the vinegar, oil and a pinch of salt, pour over and toss so everything glistens.
  3. Sit the slab of feta on top rather than crumbling it — that's the traditional way — and scatter with the oregano.
  4. Drizzle the feta with a little more oil and leave 5 mins for the flavours to mingle before eating.
Salt the tomatoes and let the salad stand a few minutes before serving — they release their sweet juices, which mix with the oil and vinegar into a dressing better than anything you'd make separately.
Dinner
Tomato, pepper & chickpea traybake with feta & couscous
~495 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 2 red peppers, in chunks
  • 200g cherry tomatoes
  • 60g feta
  • ½ red onion, in wedges
  • 60g giant (pearl) couscous (dry)
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp dried oregano
  • Handful parsley
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the peppers, tomatoes, onion, chickpeas, garlic, paprika and oregano with the oil in a roasting tin.
  2. Roast 25 mins until the peppers are soft and the tomatoes have burst.
  3. Meanwhile toast the couscous in a dry pan 2 mins, then simmer in salted water 8 mins until tender. Drain.
  4. Crumble the feta over the hot traybake, return 5 mins to soften, then fork through the parsley and serve over the couscous.
Give the vegetables room to spread out in the tin — crammed together they steam and go watery, but spaced out with room to breathe they roast, caramelise and concentrate into something far sweeter.

Breakfast
Cherry & almond bircher muesli
~380 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 120ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g cherries, pitted and halved
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ½ apple, grated
Method
  1. The night before, soak the oats in the milk with the honey, cover and chill.
  2. In the morning, stir the yoghurt and grated apple through the softened oats until loose and creamy.
  3. Fold in most of the cherries so they bleed a little colour through the muesli.
  4. Top with the remaining cherries, the flaked almonds and seeds.
Bircher is Switzerland's original overnight oats — the soaking softens the oats and the grated apple sweetens the bowl without a spoon of sugar, so it's ready and waiting the moment you get up.
Lunch
Puy lentil, roasted tomato & goat's cheese salad
~435 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g cooked Puy lentils (½ pouch)
  • 150g cherry tomatoes
  • 40g soft goat's cheese
  • Big handful rocket
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful parsley
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Roast the tomatoes with a little oil for 20 mins until soft and blistered.
  2. Whisk the mustard, vinegar and oil into a sharp dressing and stir most of it through the lentils while warm.
  3. Fold in the roasted tomatoes, red onion and parsley.
  4. Pile onto the rocket, crumble the goat's cheese over, and finish with the last of the dressing.
Dress lentils while they're still warm — like potatoes, they drink up a mustardy vinaigrette as they cool and season from the inside, where cold lentils just let the dressing slide off.
Dinner
Halloumi & summer vegetable fajitas
~510 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 120g halloumi, in strips
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 1 courgette, in batons
  • ½ red onion, sliced
  • 2 large tortilla wraps
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • Squeeze of lime
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Toss the pepper, courgette and onion with the oil and spices, and fry over high heat until charred and tender, 8 mins. Lift out.
  2. Fry the halloumi strips in the same hot pan until deep gold on both sides, 3 mins.
  3. Warm the wraps, and stir the lime into the yoghurt for a quick dressing.
  4. Pile the veg and halloumi into the wraps, spoon over the yoghurt, scatter with coriander, and roll up.
Cook the vegetables and the halloumi in the same pan, one after the other — the halloumi picks up the spiced, smoky residue the veg leaves behind, and you save yourself washing a second pan.

Halloumi, smashed avocado & tomato on toast
~395 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g halloumi, sliced
  • 1 avocado
  • 1 large vine tomato, sliced
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the halloumi slices in a dry non-stick pan over medium-high heat until deep gold on both sides, 3 mins.
  2. Mash the avocado with the lemon, a pinch of salt and the chilli to a rough, chunky smash.
  3. Toast the sourdough and spread the avocado thickly over it.
  4. Lay the tomato and halloumi on top, drizzle with the oil, and finish with pepper.
A dry, hot non-stick pan is all halloumi needs — it holds enough fat of its own to fry to a crisp golden crust, so there's no need to add oil and risk it spitting.
Lunch
Coronation chickpea & rocket sandwich
~430 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp mango chutney
  • ½ tsp mild curry powder
  • Handful rocket
  • ¼ cucumber, sliced
  • 1 spring onion, sliced
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Roughly crush the chickpeas with a fork so some hold their shape and some turn creamy.
  2. Stir in the yoghurt, mango chutney, curry powder, spring onion and lemon, and season.
  3. Pile onto one slice of bread with the cucumber and rocket.
  4. Top with the second slice, press gently, and cut in half.
Coronation filling is usually built on mayonnaise, but Greek yoghurt does the same creamy job with a fraction of the fat and a welcome tang — plus a good bit more protein into your lunch.
Dinner
Margherita flatbread pizzas with rocket
~510 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 flatbreads
  • 4 tbsp passata
  • 1 ball mozzarella (125g), torn
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Big handful rocket
  • 1 garlic clove
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 220°C. Warm the passata with the crushed garlic and oregano for a couple of minutes into a quick sauce.
  2. Sit the flatbreads on a hot baking tray, spread the sauce thinly, and scatter over the mozzarella and cherry tomatoes.
  3. Bake 8–10 mins until the base is crisp and the cheese is bubbling and blistered.
  4. Drizzle with the oil, and pile the rocket and basil over the top just before eating.
A flatbread makes a brilliant quick pizza base — bake it directly on a preheated tray so the underside crisps rather than steaming, and add the leaves only after it's out of the oven so they stay fresh, not wilted.

Breakfast
Sweetcorn & red pepper hash with fried eggs
~385 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 150g new potatoes, diced
  • 100g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • ½ red pepper, diced
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful parsley
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the diced potatoes 8 mins until just tender, then drain well.
  2. Fry the potatoes in the oil over high heat until crisp and golden, 6 mins, then add the pepper, sweetcorn and paprika for 4 mins more.
  3. Stir the spring onions and parsley through and push the hash to one side of the pan.
  4. Fry the eggs to your liking in the same pan, sit them on the hash, and season with pepper.
Parboil the potatoes before they hit the frying pan — they cook through in the water, so the pan's only job is to crisp the outside, giving you a golden hash without raw, chalky middles.
Lunch
Warm halloumi & runner bean salad with new potatoes
~440 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g halloumi, sliced
  • 150g runner beans, stringed and sliced
  • 150g new potatoes, halved
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Handful mint
Method
  1. Boil the new potatoes 12 mins, adding the sliced runner beans for the last 4, until both are just tender. Drain.
  2. Fry the halloumi in a dry pan until golden on both sides, 3 mins.
  3. Whisk the vinegar, oil, honey and a pinch of salt into a dressing.
  4. Toss the warm potatoes, beans, tomatoes and onion in the dressing, top with the halloumi and mint, and eat warm.
Runner beans are at their peak now — pull the stringy edge off along both sides before slicing, and cook them only until just tender and still bright green, so they keep their snap rather than going grey and soft.
Dinner
Courgette, pea & feta filo pie with salad
~505 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 sheets filo pastry
  • 1 courgette, grated
  • 80g frozen peas
  • 100g feta, crumbled
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful mint & dill
  • 25g butter, melted
  • Handful salad leaves
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Salt the grated courgette, leave 5 mins, then squeeze out the water hard.
  2. Mix the courgette, peas, feta, beaten eggs, spring onions and herbs, and season with pepper.
  3. Brush the filo sheets with melted butter and layer into a small tin, letting the edges overhang. Spoon in the filling and fold the overhang over, scrunching it.
  4. Brush with the last of the butter and bake 30 mins until deep gold and crisp. Rest 5 mins, and serve with the dressed leaves.
Keep the filo you're not using under a damp tea towel — it dries to a brittle crack within minutes of hitting the air, and torn, crumbling sheets are almost impossible to layer neatly.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
