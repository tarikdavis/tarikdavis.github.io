Breakfast
Bircher muesli with blackberries & toasted hazelnuts
~410 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150g Greek yoghurt (plus 50g to finish)
  • 80ml whole milk
  • 100g blackberries
  • 20g hazelnuts, roughly chopped
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. The night before, stir the oats, 150g of the yoghurt, the milk, cinnamon and a squeeze of lemon together in a bowl or jar. Cover and refrigerate.
  2. In the morning, toast the hazelnuts in a dry pan for 3–4 mins until they smell warm and the skins loosen. Tip onto a plate to cool.
  3. Crush half the blackberries with the honey so they collapse into a rough compote, and ripple it through the softened oats.
  4. Top with the remaining yoghurt, whole blackberries, hazelnuts and seeds.
Soaked oats swell overnight and thicken as they go — if it looks stiff in the morning, loosen it with a splash of milk rather than adding more yoghurt, which would just make it heavier.
Lunch
Courgette, sweetcorn & feta frittata with green salad
~465 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 courgette, coarsely grated
  • Kernels from 1 corn cob (~120g)
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful flat-leaf parsley
  • 1 tbsp olive oil (plus 1 tsp for the salad)
  • Big handful salad leaves
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Grate the courgette, toss with a good pinch of salt and leave in a sieve for 10 mins, then squeeze out the water in your hands — it will be a surprising amount.
  2. Beat the eggs with plenty of pepper, then fold through the squeezed courgette, corn, spring onions, parsley and most of the feta.
  3. Heat the oil in a small non-stick pan, pour in the mixture and cook gently for 6–7 mins until the edges set and only the centre wobbles.
  4. Scatter the last of the feta over and finish under a hot grill for 2–3 mins. Slide out, cool a little, and serve with leaves dressed in oil and lemon.
Salting and squeezing the courgette is the whole trick — skip it and the water it releases in the pan steams the egg instead of setting it, and the frittata comes out flabby.
Dinner
Mushroom, leek & pearl barley risotto with butter beans
~620 kcal · 32g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g pearl barley
  • 200g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 leek, sliced into half-moons
  • ½ tin (200g) butter beans, drained
  • 700ml hot vegetable stock
  • 30g vegetarian hard cheese, grated
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • Few sprigs thyme, leaves picked
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 15g butter
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Small handful parsley
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the oil over a high heat, in two batches so they colour rather than stew, and set aside with a pinch of salt.
  2. Turn the heat down, melt the butter and soften the leek for 8 mins with the garlic and thyme until sweet and collapsed.
  3. Stir in the barley, then add the hot stock a ladleful at a time, stirring now and then, for 30–35 mins until the grains are tender but still have a little bite.
  4. Return the mushrooms, fold in the butter beans to heat through, then beat in the cheese, a squeeze of lemon and the parsley off the heat.
Pearl barley never goes as creamy as arborio and doesn't need to — it holds its shape and gives you a chewier, nuttier bowl for about a third of the price.

Breakfast
Shakshuka-style baked eggs with peppers & flatbread
~430 kcal · 27g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ½ onion, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
  • Pinch of chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 flatbread
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil in a small frying pan over medium heat for 10 mins until they slump and start to catch at the edges.
  2. Add the garlic and spices, fry for 1 min until fragrant, then tip in the tomatoes with a splash of water and simmer 8–10 mins until thick enough to hold a trench.
  3. Make two wells, crack in the eggs, cover the pan and cook 5–6 mins until the whites are just set and the yolks still loose.
  4. Crumble the feta over, scatter the coriander, and warm the flatbread in a dry pan to scoop it all up.
Cook the sauce down further than feels necessary before the eggs go in — a watery base spreads the eggs thin and they poach unevenly instead of sitting proud.
Lunch
Chickpea, chard & lemon soup with a wholemeal roll
~455 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 tin (400g) chickpeas, drained
  • 150g Swiss chard, stalks diced and leaves shredded
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 700ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • Zest and juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 wholemeal roll
  • 20g mature cheddar
  • Small handful parsley
Method
  1. Soften the onion, carrot and diced chard stalks in the oil for 8 mins — the stalks take longer than the leaves, so they go in early.
  2. Add the garlic and spices for a minute, then the chickpeas and stock. Simmer 12 mins.
  3. Blitz roughly a third of the soup with a stick blender and stir it back in — you want body, not a purée.
  4. Wilt the chard leaves through for 3 mins, finish with lemon zest and juice, and serve with the roll and cheddar.
Blending only part of the pot gives you a thick, spoonable soup that still has whole chickpeas in it — the texture reads as more substantial than the calorie count suggests.
Dinner
Quorn & thyme cottage pie with cheddar mash
~620 kcal · 42g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g Quorn mince
  • 500g Maris Piper potatoes, peeled and chunked
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 carrot, finely diced
  • 100g frozen peas
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 300ml vegetable stock
  • Few sprigs thyme
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 40ml whole milk
  • 15g butter
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Boil the potatoes in salted water for 18–20 mins until a knife slides in with no resistance, then drain and steam-dry in the pan for 2 mins.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion and carrot in the oil for 8 mins, stir in the tomato purée and thyme, then add the Quorn, tomatoes, stock and bay. Simmer 15 mins until glossy and thick.
  3. Mash the potatoes with the milk, butter and half the cheddar, and season firmly.
  4. Stir the peas through the base, tip into a small dish, spread the mash over, rough the top up with a fork and bake at 200°C for 20 mins with the rest of the cheese on top.
Roughing the mash up with a fork isn't decoration — the raised ridges are what crisp and brown, so a smoothed-over top comes out pale and soft.

Breakfast
Peanut butter porridge with apple & skyr
~430 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 250ml whole milk
  • 1 tbsp crunchy peanut butter
  • 1 English apple, half grated and half sliced
  • 100g natural skyr
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • ½ tsp cinnamon
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of salt
Method
  1. Simmer the oats with the milk, a pinch of salt, the cinnamon and the grated apple for 5–6 mins, stirring, until thick and creamy.
  2. Take off the heat and beat in the peanut butter so it melts through rather than sitting in a lump.
  3. Spoon into a bowl, top with the skyr, sliced apple and seeds.
  4. Finish with the honey and an extra grind of cinnamon.
Grating half the apple into the pan sweetens the porridge from the inside as it cooks, which means you need far less honey on top than you'd expect.
Lunch
Halloumi, courgette & couscous salad with mint
~470 kcal · 27g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g halloumi, sliced
  • 1 courgette, sliced into half-moons
  • 50g couscous (dry)
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ cucumber, diced
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Small handful mint, shredded
  • Small handful parsley
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • ½ tsp ground cumin
Method
  1. Pour 70ml boiling water over the couscous with a pinch of salt and a splash of the oil, cover, and leave 8 mins, then fork through.
  2. Griddle or dry-fry the courgette over a high heat until charred in patches and just tender, 4–5 mins, and season.
  3. Wipe the pan, dry it, and fry the halloumi 1–2 mins a side until deep gold — no oil needed, it releases its own.
  4. Toss the couscous with the tomatoes, cucumber, onion, herbs, cumin, lemon and remaining oil, then lay the courgette and halloumi over the top.
Halloumi goes from golden to squeaky-rubbery in about ninety seconds, so get it out of the pan while it still gives when pressed — it firms as it sits.
Dinner
Black bean & sweetcorn chilli with rice
~610 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 tin (400g) black beans, drained
  • 150g frozen sweetcorn
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 red pepper, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp chipotle paste
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 40g soured cream
  • 20g mature cheddar, grated
  • Handful coriander
  • Juice of ½ lime
Method
  1. Rinse the rice and cook in salted water, lid on, for 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil for 8 mins, add the garlic, cumin, paprika and chipotle and fry 1 min.
  3. Add the beans and tomatoes with a splash of water and simmer 15 mins, mashing a few beans against the side of the pan to thicken it.
  4. Stir the sweetcorn through for the last 3 mins, finish with lime, and serve over the rice with soured cream, cheddar and coriander.
Mashing a spoonful of the beans is the cheapest way to thicken a chilli — the released starch does the job that a long reduction would, in a fraction of the time.

Breakfast
Poached eggs with garlicky chard on sourdough
~410 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 150g Swiss chard, stalks and leaves separated
  • 2 thick slices sourdough
  • 1 garlic clove, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 10g butter
  • Pinch of chilli flakes
  • 1 tsp cider vinegar (for the poaching water)
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • 20g vegetarian hard cheese, shaved
Method
  1. Slice the chard stalks and fry them in the oil for 4 mins before the leaves go anywhere near the pan — they need the head start.
  2. Add the garlic and chilli, then the shredded leaves and a splash of water, and cook covered for 3 mins until collapsed. Finish with lemon.
  3. Bring a pan of water to a bare simmer with the vinegar, swirl it, and slide the eggs in one at a time. Poach 3 mins for a set white and running yolk.
  4. Toast and butter the sourdough, pile the chard on, top with the eggs and shave the cheese over.
The vinegar helps the whites set fast, but the bigger factor is freshness — older eggs have looser whites that feather no matter what you add to the water.
Lunch
Roast tomato & red lentil soup with cheddar toastie fingers
~470 kcal · 27g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g red lentils
  • 6 vine tomatoes, halved
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 600ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 40g mature cheddar, grated
  • 10g butter
  • Small handful basil
Method
  1. Roast the tomatoes cut-side up with the garlic, a drizzle of oil and salt at 200°C for 25 mins until shrunken and caramelised at the edges.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion and carrot in the rest of the oil for 8 mins, add the paprika, then the lentils and stock and simmer 20 mins.
  3. Tip in the roast tomatoes and squeezed-out garlic, blitz smooth, and loosen with a little water if needed.
  4. Butter the bread on the outside, fill with the cheddar, fry until deeply golden on both sides, then cut into fingers for dunking.
Roasting the tomatoes rather than simmering them concentrates the sugars and drives off water — it's the difference between a soup that tastes of tinned tomato and one that tastes of tomato.
Dinner
Spiced cauliflower & chickpea traybake with yoghurt
~615 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ cauliflower, in florets
  • 1 tin (400g) chickpeas, drained and dried
  • 1 red onion, in wedges
  • 1 red pepper, in chunks
  • 2 tsp ras el hanout
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 120g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 20g flaked almonds
  • 1 flatbread
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Heat the oven to 210°C. Toss the cauliflower, onion and pepper with the oil, ras el hanout, turmeric and salt, and spread out on a large tray with room between them.
  2. Roast 20 mins, then add the chickpeas — patted properly dry — and roast a further 15 mins until they crackle and the cauliflower has brown edges.
  3. Stir the garlic and lemon into the yoghurt with a pinch of salt.
  4. Toast the almonds in a dry pan, scatter over the tray with the coriander, and serve with the yoghurt and a warmed flatbread.
Crowding is what stops vegetables browning — if they're touching they steam. Use the biggest tray you own, or split across two, and the same ingredients taste twice as good.

Breakfast
Cottage cheese pancakes with blackberry compote
~425 kcal · 32g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g cottage cheese
  • 2 large eggs
  • 40g rolled oats, blitzed to a rough flour
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 120g blackberries (fresh or frozen)
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • 10g butter
  • 50g Greek yoghurt, to serve
Method
  1. Blitz the cottage cheese, eggs, oat flour, baking powder and vanilla to a smooth, pourable batter and leave it 5 mins to thicken.
  2. Warm the blackberries with the honey and lemon in a small pan for 4–5 mins until they burst and the juices run syrupy.
  3. Melt the butter in a non-stick pan over medium-low heat and cook the pancakes in three or four rounds, 2 mins a side, turning when bubbles hold their shape.
  4. Stack, spoon the compote over and add the yoghurt.
These are wetter than a flour batter, so keep the heat lower than you would for ordinary pancakes — too hot and the outside sets before the middle has any chance to cook.
Lunch
Roasted courgette, butter bean & basil salad with sourdough
~460 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ tin (200g) butter beans, drained
  • 1 courgette, in thick half-moons
  • 150g cherry tomatoes
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp cider vinegar
  • Big handful basil
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 thick slice sourdough
  • 20g vegetarian hard cheese, shaved
Method
  1. Roast the courgette and tomatoes with 1 tbsp of the oil and salt at 210°C for 20 mins, until the courgette is bronzed and the tomatoes have split.
  2. Tip the warm vegetables straight onto the butter beans with the garlic, vinegar and remaining oil, and let it all sit for 5 mins.
  3. Soak the red onion in cold water for 5 mins to soften its bite, then drain and fold through with the basil and rocket.
  4. Toast the sourdough, rub it with the cut side of a garlic clove if you like, and shave the cheese over the salad.
Tipping hot roast veg onto cold beans warms them just enough to open up and take on the dressing — a trick that turns a tin of beans into something that tastes cooked.
Dinner
Leek, potato & cheddar filo pie with peas
~620 kcal · 30g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 leeks, sliced
  • 300g potatoes, in 2cm cubes
  • 150g frozen peas
  • 50g mature cheddar, grated
  • 4 sheets filo pastry
  • 200ml whole milk
  • 20g plain flour
  • 25g butter (plus 15g melted, for brushing)
  • 1 tsp Dijon or wholegrain mustard
  • Grating of nutmeg
  • Small handful chives, snipped
Method
  1. Boil the potato cubes for 8 mins until just tender, adding the peas for the final minute, then drain.
  2. Soften the leeks in the 25g butter for 10 mins over low heat until silky, stir in the flour and cook 1 min, then add the milk gradually to make a thick sauce.
  3. Off the heat stir in the cheddar, mustard, nutmeg, chives, potatoes and peas, and season well. Tip into a small pie dish.
  4. Brush each filo sheet with melted butter, scrunch loosely and lay over the top, then bake at 200°C for 22–25 mins until crisp and deep gold.
Scrunching the filo rather than laying it flat gives you far more surface area to crisp, and it's more forgiving — a torn sheet disappears into the ruffles.

Breakfast
Sweetcorn, chard & cheddar hash with a fried egg
~425 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g new potatoes, halved
  • Kernels from 1 corn cob (~120g)
  • 100g Swiss chard, shredded
  • 1 large egg
  • ½ onion, sliced
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 10g butter
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
Method
  1. Boil the potatoes for 10 mins until just tender, drain well and let the steam die off — dry potatoes crisp, wet ones don't.
  2. Fry the onion in the oil for 5 mins, add the potatoes cut-side down and leave them alone for 4–5 mins to catch and colour before turning.
  3. Add the corn and paprika, cook 3 mins, then stir the chard through until wilted and season.
  4. Scatter the cheddar over and cover for a minute to melt, while you fry the egg in the butter. Serve with the spring onions on top.
The urge to keep stirring a hash is what stops it browning — leave the pan alone in long stretches and you get the crust that makes the dish.
Lunch
Greek-style salad with butter beans, cucumber & feta
~465 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ tin (200g) butter beans, drained
  • 60g feta, in slabs
  • ½ cucumber, in thick chunks
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • 30g kalamata olives
  • 1 pitta bread
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • Small handful mint
Method
  1. Salt the tomatoes and cucumber lightly and leave 5 mins — they'll release juice that becomes half the dressing.
  2. Add the butter beans, onion and olives, then the oil, vinegar and oregano, and turn everything over gently.
  3. Lay the feta on top in slabs rather than crumbling it, and grind pepper over.
  4. Warm the pitta, tear it into pieces, and use it to mop the bowl.
Slabs of feta rather than crumbs mean you get distinct salty hits against the vegetables, instead of a uniformly salty salad where nothing stands out.
Dinner
Halloumi, courgette & cherry tomato traybake with giant couscous
~615 kcal · 32g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g halloumi, in thick slices
  • 1 courgette, in half-moons
  • 200g cherry tomatoes
  • 1 red onion, in wedges
  • 70g giant couscous
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Pinch of chilli flakes
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Handful basil
  • Handful rocket
Method
  1. Heat the oven to 210°C. Toss the courgette, tomatoes and onion with 1 tbsp oil, the oregano and chilli, and roast 20 mins.
  2. Simmer the giant couscous in salted water for 8–9 mins until tender with a little chew, then drain and toss with the remaining oil and the garlic.
  3. Lay the halloumi over the vegetables and return to the oven for 8–10 mins until it softens and takes colour.
  4. Fold the couscous through the tray, squeeze the lemon over, and serve on the rocket with the basil torn across.
Giant couscous holds its shape in a traybake where ordinary couscous would turn to mush, so it can go in with the juices rather than sitting underneath.

Breakfast
Apple & cinnamon pancakes with Greek yoghurt
~425 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g plain flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 2 large eggs
  • 100ml whole milk
  • 1 English apple, grated
  • ½ tsp cinnamon
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 10g butter
Method
  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder and cinnamon with the eggs and milk to a thick, smooth batter, then fold in the grated apple.
  2. Rest the batter 10 mins — the flour hydrates and the pancakes rise better for it.
  3. Cook in the butter over medium-low heat in small rounds, 2–3 mins a side, until risen and golden.
  4. Stack with the yoghurt, seeds and a thread of honey.
Grated apple carries water into the batter, so keep the pancakes small — a thick, wide one sets on the outside long before the middle has cooked.
Lunch
Warm new potato, green bean & egg salad
~465 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 300g new potatoes, halved
  • 150g green beans, trimmed
  • 2 large eggs
  • Handful salad leaves
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp wholegrain mustard
  • 1 tsp cider vinegar
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Small handful parsley
  • 20g vegetarian hard cheese, shaved
Method
  1. Boil the potatoes 12–14 mins, dropping the beans in for the final 3 so they stay squeaky and bright.
  2. Boil the eggs for 7 minutes exactly, then cool under cold running water and peel — the yolks should be set at the edge and fudgy in the middle.
  3. Whisk the mustard, vinegar, honey and oil with salt and pepper, and toss it through the potatoes and beans while they're still hot.
  4. Fold in the leaves, onion and parsley, halve the eggs over the top, and shave the cheese across.
Warm potatoes soak up a mustard dressing the way cold ones never will — dress them straight out of the colander and the seasoning goes all the way through.
Dinner
Lentil, walnut & mushroom roast with roast potatoes and greens
~625 kcal · 30g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g puy or green lentils (dry)
  • 150g chestnut mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 40g walnuts, chopped
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 carrot, grated
  • 1 large egg
  • 40g fresh breadcrumbs (from the sourdough)
  • Few sprigs thyme
  • 400g potatoes, in roasting chunks
  • 150g Swiss chard or spring greens
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 tsp wholegrain mustard
  • 200ml vegetable stock, for the gravy
  • 1 tsp plain flour
Method
  1. Simmer the lentils 22 mins until tender, and meanwhile parboil the potatoes 8 mins, drain, and shake them roughly in the pan to rough up the edges.
  2. Roast the potatoes in 1 tbsp of the oil at 210°C for 40 mins, turning once.
  3. Fry the onion, carrot and mushrooms in the rest of the oil until the pan is dry, about 10 mins, then stir in the tomato purée, thyme and mustard. Mix with the lentils, walnuts, breadcrumbs and egg, press into a small lined tin and bake 30 mins.
  4. Make a quick gravy by stirring the flour into the pan you fried in, then whisking in the stock. Wilt the greens in a splash of water and serve everything together.
Roughing up the parboiled potatoes creates the broken, floury surface that turns properly crunchy — smooth-sided potatoes just go leathery.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
