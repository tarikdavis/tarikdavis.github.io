Breakfast
Skyr, blackberry & toasted oat bowl
~405 kcal · 29g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 170g natural skyr
  • 100g blackberries
  • 40g rolled oats
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Toast the oats and almonds in a dry frying pan over medium heat, 3–4 mins, shaking often, until they smell nutty and turn a shade darker. Tip onto a plate to cool and crisp.
  2. Crush half the blackberries with a fork and a drop of the honey until they slump into a rough compote.
  3. Stir the vanilla through the skyr, spoon into a bowl and ripple the crushed berries through it.
  4. Pile the toasted oats, almonds, seeds and remaining whole berries on top, and finish with the rest of the honey and the cinnamon.
Toasting the oats dry rather than soaking them gives you granola texture without the sugar and oil a shop-bought bag carries — and it takes four minutes.
Lunch
Beetroot, lentil & feta salad with walnuts
~465 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g puy or green lentils (dry)
  • 200g cooked beetroot, in wedges
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • 25g walnuts, roughly broken
  • Big handful rocket
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp wholegrain mustard
  • Small handful flat-leaf parsley
Method
  1. Simmer the lentils in unsalted water for 20–22 mins until tender but still holding their shape. Drain well and season while warm.
  2. Whisk the oil, vinegar and mustard with a pinch of salt, and toss half of it through the warm lentils so they drink it up.
  3. Soak the sliced red onion in cold water for 5 mins to take the raw edge off, then drain.
  4. Fold the beetroot, onion, rocket and parsley through the lentils, scatter over the feta and walnuts, and spoon the rest of the dressing across the top.
Dress lentils while they're still warm — warm starch absorbs vinaigrette in a way cold lentils simply won't, so the flavour goes through the dish rather than sitting on it.
Dinner
Paneer, pepper & spinach curry with basmati
~625 kcal · 40g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 120g paneer, cubed
  • 2 red peppers, sliced
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp turmeric, ¼ tsp chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 3 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Rinse the rice, then cook in salted water with the lid on for 11 mins and leave to rest off the heat.
  2. Fry the paneer in half the oil over a decent heat until golden on two or three sides, then lift out — it finishes cooking in the sauce.
  3. Soften the onion in the rest of the oil for 6 mins, add the garlic, ginger and spices and fry 1 min, then add the peppers and cook 5 mins more.
  4. Tip in the tomatoes with a splash of water and simmer 10 mins, return the paneer, wilt the spinach through, and serve over the rice with the yoghurt and coriander.
Paneer squeaks and toughens if it boils, so brown it first and add it back at the end — a couple of minutes in the sauce is all it needs to take on flavour while staying tender.

Breakfast
Sweetcorn fritters with poached egg & tomato salsa
~425 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • Kernels from 1 corn cob (~150g)
  • 2 large eggs (1 for the batter, 1 to poach)
  • 40g plain flour
  • ½ tsp baking powder
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, quartered
  • Squeeze of lime
  • Small handful coriander
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Stand the cob upright and run a knife down it to strip the kernels. Mix them with the flour, baking powder, one egg, the spring onions and plenty of pepper into a thick batter.
  2. Toss the tomatoes with the lime, coriander and a pinch of salt, and leave to sit while you cook.
  3. Fry three spoonfuls of batter in the oil over medium heat, 2–3 mins a side, until deep golden and set through.
  4. Poach the second egg in barely-simmering water for 3 mins, then serve it on the fritters with the salsa spooned over.
Fresh corn cut straight off the cob has a milky sweetness frozen kernels lose — late August is peak British sweetcorn, so it's worth the two minutes with a knife.
Lunch
Runner bean, new potato & egg niçoise
~455 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g runner beans, stringed and sliced on the diagonal
  • 200g new potatoes, halved
  • 2 large eggs
  • Handful lettuce leaves
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 6 black olives
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Small handful parsley
Method
  1. Boil the potatoes for 12 mins, adding the runner beans for the last 4, then drain and let the steam escape.
  2. Lower the eggs into the same boiling water for 7½ mins for a just-set jammy yolk, then cool under the cold tap and peel.
  3. Whisk the oil, vinegar and mustard with salt and pepper, and toss it through the warm potatoes and beans.
  4. Pile onto the lettuce with the tomatoes and olives, halve the eggs on top, and finish with the parsley.
Runner beans need their strings run off with a peeler and a diagonal slice — cut that way they cook in four minutes and keep a squeaky bite rather than turning to khaki string.
Dinner
Green lentil ragù with spaghetti
~615 kcal · 33g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g green or brown lentils (dry)
  • 75g spaghetti (dry)
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 carrot, finely diced
  • 1 celery stick, finely diced
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 bay leaf
  • 20g vegetarian hard cheese, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Sweat the onion, carrot and celery in the oil over a low heat for 10 mins until soft and sweet — don't rush this, it's the whole base of the sauce.
  2. Stir in the garlic, tomato purée and oregano, cook 1 min, then add the lentils, tomatoes, bay leaf and 300ml water.
  3. Simmer, part-covered, for 30–35 mins until the lentils are soft and the sauce is thick. Top up with water if it tightens too far.
  4. Cook the spaghetti, drain reserving a splash of the water, and toss it through the ragù with the cheese and enough pasta water to make it glossy.
This makes a generous pan — freeze half in a tub and you've bought yourself a twelve-minute dinner in a fortnight's time. Lentil ragù freezes better than almost anything else you'll cook this week.

Breakfast
Cottage cheese, plum & pistachio toast
~400 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g cottage cheese
  • 2 ripe plums, sliced
  • 1 thick slice sourdough
  • 20g pistachios, chopped
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
  • A few mint leaves
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Toast the sourdough well — you want it firm enough to hold a wet topping without going limp.
  2. Season the cottage cheese with a grind of black pepper and spread it thickly over the toast.
  3. Fan the plum slices across the top and scatter over the pistachios and mint.
  4. Finish with the honey and a pinch of cinnamon.
Cottage cheese is the quiet workhorse of a high-protein vegetarian week — around 12g of protein per 100g for very few calories. If the texture puts you off, blitz it smooth with a stick blender first.
Lunch
Pearl barley salad with halloumi, cucumber & tomato
~470 kcal · 27g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g pearl barley (dry)
  • 80g halloumi, sliced
  • ½ cucumber, diced
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1 courgette, in half-moons
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 6 black olives
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Handful mint
Method
  1. Simmer the barley in salted water for 25–30 mins until tender with a slight chew, then drain and cool under the tap.
  2. Griddle or fry the courgette in a dry hot pan until charred in patches, then set aside.
  3. Fry the halloumi in the same pan, 2 mins a side, until deeply golden — no oil needed, it releases its own.
  4. Toss the barley with the cucumber, tomatoes, courgette, onion, olives, oil, lemon and oregano, lay the halloumi over and tear the mint on top.
Pearl barley costs about a third of most "grain salad" bases and holds dressing better than couscous. Cook a double batch — it keeps three days and turns any pile of vegetables into lunch.
Dinner
Runner bean, tomato & orzo one-pot with feta
~620 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g runner beans, stringed and sliced
  • 75g orzo (dry)
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • ½ onion, chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, ½ tsp chilli flakes
  • 300ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Soften the onion in the oil for 6 mins, then add the garlic, oregano and chilli flakes and fry until fragrant.
  2. Add the tomatoes and stock, bring to a simmer, then stir in the orzo and runner beans.
  3. Cook uncovered for 10–12 mins, stirring now and then so the orzo doesn't catch, until the pasta is tender and the sauce has thickened around it.
  4. Season, crumble the feta over the top, cover for 2 mins to soften it, then tear over the basil.
Orzo releases its starch straight into the sauce, which is why this thickens without cream or flour — and also why it sticks. Stir every couple of minutes and keep the heat gentle.

Breakfast
Spinach, tomato & cheddar omelette with toast
~420 kcal · 30g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 60g baby spinach
  • 1 large vine tomato, diced
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Small handful chives
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Wilt the spinach in a dry non-stick pan for a minute, tip it out and squeeze away any water — wet spinach makes a soggy omelette.
  2. Beat the eggs with salt and pepper. Melt the butter, pour in the eggs and draw the setting edges into the middle with a spatula, tilting the pan to fill the gaps.
  3. When the top is still just glossy, scatter the spinach, tomato and cheddar over one half and fold the omelette over.
  4. Slide onto a plate, rest 30 seconds so the cheese melts through, and serve with the toast and chives.
Take the omelette off the heat while the surface still looks slightly underdone — residual heat finishes it on the plate. That's the whole difference between creamy and rubbery.
Lunch
Curried sweetcorn & potato chowder with a roll
~460 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g frozen sweetcorn
  • 250g new potatoes, diced
  • 100g frozen peas
  • 200ml whole milk
  • 300ml vegetable stock
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp mild curry powder
  • 1 wholemeal roll
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Soften the onion in the butter for 6 mins, then add the garlic, ginger and curry powder and fry 1 min until it smells warm and toasted.
  2. Add the potatoes and stock and simmer 12 mins until the potatoes are tender.
  3. Add the sweetcorn, peas and milk and heat through for 3 mins — don't let it boil hard once the milk is in.
  4. Blitz about a third of the soup with a stick blender and stir it back in for body, then season, scatter the coriander and serve with the roll.
Blending only part of a chowder gives you a thick, creamy base while keeping whole kernels and chunks of potato to chew on. Blitz the lot and you've made soup; blitz a third and you've made chowder.
Dinner
Quorn & aubergine moussaka-style bake
~620 kcal · 42g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Quorn mince
  • 1 aubergine, sliced into rounds
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • ½ onion, chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 large egg
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful salad leaves to serve
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Brush the aubergine slices with oil, spread on a tray and roast 20 mins until soft and browned at the edges.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion in a pan, add the garlic, oregano, cinnamon and tomato purée, then the Quorn mince and tomatoes. Simmer 12 mins and season well.
  3. Beat the yoghurt with the egg and half the cheddar to make a quick topping.
  4. Layer the mince and aubergine in a small dish, pour the topping over, scatter the rest of the cheese and bake 20 mins until set and golden. Serve with the leaves.
The yoghurt-and-egg topping is a shortcut béchamel — no butter, no flour, no whisking at the stove — and it sets to the same creamy layer with a fraction of the fat and considerably more protein.

Greek yoghurt, plum & pistachio bowl with granola
~410 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 170g Greek yoghurt
  • 2 ripe plums, sliced
  • 40g granola
  • 15g pistachios, chopped
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. Halve one plum and cook it cut-side down in a dry pan for 2–3 mins until it softens and caramelises slightly. Slice the other raw for contrast.
  2. Stir the vanilla into the yoghurt and spoon it into a bowl.
  3. Arrange the warm and the raw plums over the top.
  4. Scatter over the granola, pistachios and seeds, and finish with the honey.
Warming half the fruit and leaving half raw gives you two textures and two levels of sweetness from the same plum — a small trick that makes a five-ingredient breakfast feel composed.
Lunch
Roasted beetroot, hummus & rocket wrap with egg
~455 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • 80g hummus
  • 150g cooked beetroot, sliced
  • 2 large eggs
  • Handful rocket
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the eggs for 8 mins, cool under the tap, peel and slice.
  2. Toast the cumin seeds in a dry pan for 30 seconds and stir them through the hummus with the lemon juice.
  3. Warm the wrap briefly in the same pan so it folds without cracking.
  4. Spread the hummus down the middle, layer on the beetroot, egg, onion and rocket, season well and roll up tightly, tucking one end in.
Warm the wrap for twenty seconds before filling it. Cold tortillas split along the fold; warm ones behave — which is the difference between a wrap and a plate of its contents.
Dinner
Aubergine parmigiana with garlic ciabatta
~630 kcal · 30g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 aubergines, sliced lengthways
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 125g mozzarella, torn
  • 20g vegetarian hard cheese, grated
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Handful basil
  • ½ ciabatta
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Brush the aubergine slices with oil and roast on trays for 25 mins, turning once, until collapsing and golden.
  2. Simmer the tomatoes with one crushed garlic clove and the oregano for 15 mins until thick, then season and stir in most of the basil.
  3. Layer aubergine, sauce and mozzarella twice over in a small dish, finishing with sauce and the grated hard cheese. Bake 20 mins until bubbling.
  4. Split the ciabatta, spread with butter mashed with the remaining garlic, and bake alongside for the last 8 mins.
Roasting the aubergine rather than frying it saves a startling amount of oil — aubergine is a sponge, and a fried slice soaks up three or four times what a roasted one does.

Corn, spring onion & cheddar breakfast quesadilla
~425 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • Kernels from 1 corn cob (~150g)
  • 40g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • ½ tsp butter
  • Handful coriander
  • 100g cherry tomatoes to serve
Method
  1. Strip the kernels from the cob and char them in a dry pan over a high heat for 3 mins until spotted brown in places.
  2. Beat the egg, pour it into the buttered pan and scramble softly, then stir the corn, spring onions and chilli flakes through.
  3. Spread the mixture over half the tortilla, scatter the cheddar on top and fold the other half over.
  4. Toast in the dry pan, 2 mins a side, pressing down, until crisp and the cheese has melted. Cut into wedges and serve with the tomatoes and coriander.
Char the corn hard before it goes anywhere near the cheese — dry, high heat brings out a smoky sweetness that a gentle sauté never will.

Ploughman's plate — cheddar, pickle & tomato salad
~465 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g mature cheddar
  • 1 wholemeal roll
  • 2 tbsp pickle or chutney
  • 1 apple, sliced
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Handful lettuce leaves
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Whisk the oil and vinegar with salt and pepper and toss it through the tomatoes, onion and lettuce.
  2. Cut the cheddar into thick slices rather than grating it — the point of a ploughman's is texture.
  3. Butter the roll and arrange everything on a board or a large plate: cheese, pickle, apple, salad.
  4. Eat slowly. This one is meant to be assembled, not cooked.
A no-cook lunch once a week is worth planning for deliberately — it protects Saturday from the tyranny of the hob, and a decent mature cheddar carries the plate on its own.
Dinner
Squash & chickpea tagine with couscous
~610 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 400g butternut squash, cubed
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g couscous (dry)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • 40g dried apricots, chopped
  • 2 tsp ras el hanout, ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 250ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Brown the squash in the oil in a wide pan for 5 mins, then add the onion and cook 5 mins more.
  2. Stir in the garlic, ras el hanout and cinnamon for a minute, then add the chickpeas, tomatoes, apricots and stock.
  3. Simmer, part-covered, for 20–25 mins until the squash is tender and the sauce has reduced and darkened.
  4. Pour boiling water over the couscous to just cover, lid on for 5 mins, then fork through. Serve the tagine over it with the yoghurt and coriander.
Dried apricots do more than sweeten — they break down slightly and thicken the sauce, which is why tagines get that jammy consistency without any thickener.

Breakfast
French toast with roasted plums & Greek yoghurt
~420 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 thick slices sourdough
  • 2 large eggs
  • 60ml whole milk
  • 3 ripe plums, halved
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • Pinch of cinnamon
  • 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Roast the plums cut-side up with the honey for 15 mins until soft and syrupy.
  2. Beat the eggs, milk, vanilla and cinnamon in a shallow dish and soak the bread for a minute a side — long enough to be saturated, not long enough to fall apart.
  3. Fry in the butter over medium heat, 2–3 mins a side, until deep golden and puffed.
  4. Serve with the roasted plums, their syrup and a spoonful of Greek yoghurt.
Stale bread makes better French toast than fresh. Dry crumb drinks up the custard where fresh bread turns to mush — so this is the useful ending for the end of the sourdough loaf.
Lunch
Sweet potato, chickpea & tahini salad bowl
~460 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g sweet potato, cubed
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • Handful rocket or salad leaves
  • ¼ red onion, sliced
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1 tbsp tahini
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • Handful parsley
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the sweet potato and chickpeas with the oil, paprika and cumin and roast 25 mins, shaking halfway, until the potato is caramelised and the chickpeas have crisped.
  2. Loosen the tahini with the lemon, honey and 2–3 tbsp cold water, whisking until it turns pale and pourable.
  3. Pile the warm sweet potato and chickpeas over the leaves with the tomatoes and onion.
  4. Spoon the tahini dressing generously over the top and finish with the parsley.
Tahini seizes and goes claggy when liquid first hits it — keep whisking and add water a splash at a time and it loosens into a smooth, pale dressing. It always looks wrong before it comes right.
Dinner
Spinach & ricotta stuffed pasta shells
~620 kcal · 32g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g large pasta shells (conchiglioni)
  • 250g ricotta
  • 200g baby spinach
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 20g vegetarian hard cheese, grated
  • ½ onion, chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • Pinch grated nutmeg
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Boil the shells for 8 mins — just short of al dente, they finish in the oven — then drain and cool enough to handle.
  2. Wilt the spinach, squeeze it dry very firmly and chop, then mix with the ricotta, nutmeg, salt and plenty of pepper.
  3. Soften the onion in the oil, add the garlic and oregano, then the tomatoes, and simmer 12 mins. Spread over the base of a small dish.
  4. Fill each shell with the ricotta mixture, nestle them into the sauce, scatter the hard cheese over and bake 20 mins until bubbling. Tear the basil over.
Squeeze the wilted spinach far harder than feels reasonable — a fistful holds a surprising amount of water, and any left behind leaks out in the oven and makes the filling watery.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

High-protein swaps, applied to this week

Small additions that lift each meal past the 30g anchor without rewriting the menu. Most extras come from things already on your shopping list — plus a tub of unflavoured whey or pea protein.

Breakfasts
Baseline

Skyr & blackberry oat bowl, sweetcorn fritters with poached egg, cottage cheese & plum toast, spinach & cheddar omelette, Greek yoghurt & plum bowl, corn & cheddar quesadilla, French toast with roasted plums.

Add / swap

Add 25g whey or pea protein to the skyr bowl and the yoghurt bowl, and a spoon of peanut butter to the oats. Use an extra egg in the fritters, the omelette, the quesadilla and the French toast custard, with a tub of cottage cheese alongside the toast.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
