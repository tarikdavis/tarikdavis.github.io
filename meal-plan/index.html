Monday
12 May 2026
Breakfast
Greek yoghurt & berries
~320 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g frozen mixed summer fruits, thawed
  • 1 tsp honey (optional)
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. Spoon yoghurt into a bowl.
  2. Top with thawed berries and juices.
  3. Drizzle with honey, scatter seeds.
Prep the night before — leave berries to thaw in the fridge overnight on top of the yoghurt.
Lunch
Lentil & tomato soup
~340 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin green lentils, drained
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 500ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tsp cumin, ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • Olive oil, salt & pepper
Method
  1. Soften onion in olive oil 5 mins.
  2. Add garlic and spices, cook 1 min.
  3. Add lentils, tomatoes, stock. Simmer 15 mins.
  4. Blend half for chunky texture. Season.
Makes 2 portions — refrigerate the second for a quick lunch later in the week.
Dinner
Quorn mince chilli with rice
~480 kcal · 32g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g Quorn mince
  • 1 × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 pepper
  • 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp chilli powder, ½ tsp cinnamon
  • 150g brown rice (dry)
Method
  1. Cook rice per packet. Set aside.
  2. Fry onion 5 mins, add pepper 3 mins.
  3. Add garlic and spices, stir 1 min.
  4. Add Quorn, cook 3–4 mins. Add beans and tomatoes.
  5. Simmer 20 mins. Serve over rice.
Cook double the rice now — use leftovers on Thursday.
Tuesday
13 May 2026
Breakfast
Scrambled eggs on wholegrain toast
~340 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 2 tbsp whole milk
  • 2 slices wholegrain bread, toasted
  • Small knob of butter
  • Salt, pepper, chives
Method
  1. Whisk eggs with milk and seasoning.
  2. Melt butter in non-stick pan on low heat.
  3. Stir eggs slowly until just set — remove while still slightly glossy.
  4. Pile onto toast, scatter chives.
Low and slow is the key to creamy scrambled eggs.
Lunch
Halloumi & roasted pepper wrap
~380 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 125g halloumi, sliced
  • 1 roasted pepper, sliced
  • 1 low-carb tortilla wrap
  • Handful babyleaf salad
  • 1 tbsp natural yoghurt or hummus
  • Chilli flakes, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Grill or pan-fry halloumi 2 mins each side.
  2. Warm wrap in a dry pan 30 seconds.
  3. Spread yoghurt, layer salad, pepper, halloumi.
  4. Add chilli flakes and lemon. Roll tightly.
Halloumi is best eaten immediately — it gets rubbery when cold.
Dinner
Tofu stir-fry with peas & soy
~420 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 150g frozen petits pois
  • 1 pepper, 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 tbsp reduced-salt soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil (optional)
  • Cooked brown rice (from Monday)
Method
  1. Press tofu 10 mins. Cube and fry until golden. Set aside.
  2. Fry onion and pepper 4 mins. Add garlic and ginger 1 min.
  3. Add peas, soy sauce and tofu. Toss 2 mins.
  4. Drizzle sesame oil. Serve over rice.
Pressing the tofu removes moisture and makes it much crispier when fried.

Four bean salad with cottage cheese
~360 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin mixed beans, drained
  • 150g reduced-fat cottage cheese
  • ½ cucumber, diced
  • 4 radishes, sliced
  • Handful babyleaf salad, 2 salad onions
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp red wine vinegar

Dinner
Pasta with cannellini & tomato sauce
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g fusilli (dry)
  • 1 × 400g tin cannellini beans
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp oregano, pinch chilli flakes

Cannellini beans add creaminess and a big protein and fibre boost.

Poached eggs & sautéed mushrooms

  • 2 large eggs
  • 200g baby chestnut mushrooms, halved
  • 1 slice wholegrain toast

Dinner
Black bean & sweet potato curry
~460 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 × 400g tins black beans, drained
  • 2 medium sweet potatoes, cubed
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, 3 garlic cloves, 1 tsp ginger
  • 2 tsp curry powder, 1 tsp cumin, ½ tsp turmeric
  • 200ml veg stock, natural yoghurt to serve
Method

  3. Add sweet potato, coat in spices. Add tomatoes and stock.
  4. Cover, simmer 20 mins until tender. Add beans, 5 mins more.
  5. Serve with yoghurt.
Even better the next day as the flavours develop.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
