Breakfast
Cherry & almond Bircher pots
~350 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g British cherries, pitted and halved
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tsp honey, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. The night before: stir oats, milk and half the yoghurt together in a jar. Refrigerate.
  2. In the morning, toss the halved cherries with honey and a squeeze of lemon. Sit 5 mins — they turn glossy and release juice.
  3. Toast the flaked almonds in a dry pan, 2 mins, shaking often.
  4. Top the oats with the remaining yoghurt, the cherries and their juices, and the almonds.
British cherry season has just opened and the early fruit is sharp-sweet — perfect against creamy oats. A cheap cherry pitter pays for itself in a fortnight.
Lunch
Halloumi & new potato salad with lemon-mint dressing
~410 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g new potatoes, halved
  • 80g halloumi, sliced
  • 50g frozen petits pois
  • 4 radishes, thinly sliced
  • Handful pea shoots
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, juice & zest of ½ lemon
  • Small handful mint, chopped; 1 tsp honey
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil potatoes 12 mins until tender, adding the peas for the final 2. Drain.
  2. Whisk olive oil, lemon juice and zest, honey and mint into a dressing. Toss the warm potatoes and peas through it.
  3. Griddle or dry-fry the halloumi slices 2 mins per side until golden and striped.
  4. Fold radishes and pea shoots through the potatoes, top with the hot halloumi and plenty of pepper.
Halloumi has a ten-minute window between golden-soft and squeaky-rubber — cook it last and eat it straight away. Warm potatoes drink the dressing; cold ones shrug it off.
Dinner
Smoky Quorn & charred pepper fajitas
~480 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g Quorn pieces
  • 1 red pepper & 1 yellow pepper, sliced
  • ½ red onion, sliced
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika, 1 tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp chilli powder
  • 2 tortilla wraps
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, ½ lime
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, fresh coriander
Method
  1. Get a large frying pan smoking hot. Char the peppers and onion in half the oil, 6 mins, stirring only occasionally — you want blackened edges.
  2. Add the rest of the oil, the Quorn pieces and the spices. Fry 4–5 mins with a splash of water so the spices coat everything.
  3. Warm the tortillas in a dry pan, 20 seconds a side.
  4. Build at the table: Quorn and peppers, cheddar, yoghurt, coriander and a big squeeze of lime.
The char is the flavour — resist stirring the peppers too often. Quorn pieces take on smoky spices brilliantly because they're porous, like little flavour sponges.

Breakfast
Mushroom, spinach & cheddar omelette
~340 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Handful baby spinach
  • 25g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tsp butter, 1 tsp olive oil
  • Few chives, snipped; black pepper
Method
  1. Fry mushrooms in the oil over high heat, 5 mins, until their water is gone and they're properly browned. Add spinach to wilt, 30 seconds. Tip out.
  2. Whisk eggs with a pinch of salt. Melt butter in the same pan over medium heat.
  3. Pour in the eggs. As the edges set, draw them to the middle and tilt the pan so raw egg fills the gaps.
  4. While the top is still slightly glossy, scatter cheddar and the mushroom mix over one half. Fold, slide onto the plate, finish with chives.
Mushrooms before eggs, always — they need high heat and dry space to brown, and an omelette needs neither. A glossy top when you fold means a creamy middle on the plate.
Lunch
Summer minestrone with basil pesto
~390 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ courgette, diced small
  • 1 carrot, diced small
  • ½ onion, diced; 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 400ml hot vegetable stock
  • 30g orzo
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 50g frozen petits pois
  • 1 tbsp vegetarian basil pesto, 10g vegetarian parmesan
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Soften onion and carrot in oil, 5 mins. Add garlic and courgette, 2 mins more.
  2. Pour in tomatoes and stock, bring to a simmer.
  3. Add the orzo, simmer 8 mins. Add beans and peas for the final 3.
  4. Ladle into a bowl, swirl the pesto through off the heat, grate parmesan over.
Summer minestrone is lighter and greener than the winter sort — the veg should still have bite. Pesto goes in off the heat so the basil stays perfumed rather than cooked.
Dinner
Crispy chilli tofu rice bowl with pickled cucumber
~480 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 1 tbsp cornflour
  • 60g jasmine rice (dry)
  • ¼ cucumber, ribboned; 1 tbsp vinegar, pinch of sugar
  • 1 carrot, ribboned with a peeler
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sweet chilli sauce
  • 1 spring onion, sliced; 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, 1 tsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. Toss the cucumber ribbons with vinegar, sugar and a pinch of salt — a quick pickle that's ready in 15 mins.
  2. Rinse and simmer the rice, lid on, 11 mins. Rest off the heat.
  3. Pat tofu dry, toss in cornflour, fry in hot oil 6–8 mins until golden and crisp all over.
  4. Add garlic, soy and sweet chilli to the pan — it bubbles to a sticky glaze in 30 seconds. Toss to coat.
  5. Bowl up: rice, glazed tofu, drained pickled cucumber, carrot ribbons, spring onion and seeds.
The pickle is doing the heavy lifting — its sharpness cuts the sweet glaze so the bowl doesn't turn cloying. Make it first and it's perfectly mellow by serving time.

Breakfast
Raspberry ripple yoghurt pots with toasted oats
~330 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 150g British raspberries
  • 25g rolled oats
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 2 tsp honey, ½ tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. Toast the oats and seeds in a dry pan over medium heat, 3 mins, until golden and nutty. Stir in 1 tsp honey, then tip out to cool — it sets into clusters.
  2. Mash two-thirds of the raspberries with the remaining honey.
  3. Stir vanilla into the yoghurt, then ripple the mashed raspberries through — two or three folds only.
  4. Layer into a glass with the whole raspberries and the oat clusters on top.
Two folds, not ten — a ripple should leave streaks of deep pink against white. Toasting oats with honey makes a two-minute granola that shames the boxed stuff.
Lunch
Pea & mint falafel pittas with tzatziki
~400 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 75g frozen petits pois, defrosted
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • ½ tsp ground cumin, 2 tbsp plain flour
  • 1 wholemeal pitta
  • 75g Greek yoghurt, ¼ cucumber, coarsely grated; ½ garlic clove
  • Few little gem leaves
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Pulse chickpeas, peas, mint, cumin and flour to a coarse, holdable mix — stop before it turns to paste. Season well.
  2. Shape into 6 small patties. Fry in oil, 3–4 mins per side, until deep golden.
  3. Squeeze the grated cucumber dry, stir into the yoghurt with garlic and lemon for the tzatziki.
  4. Toast and split the pitta, line with little gem, stuff with falafel and a generous spoon of tzatziki.
Coarse is the goal — over-blitzed falafel turns dense and pasty. The peas keep these bright green inside and sweeter than the classic.
Dinner
Roasted Mediterranean veg & goat's cheese orzo
~490 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g orzo (dry)
  • ½ aubergine, cubed
  • ½ courgette, thickly sliced
  • 1 red pepper, chunked
  • 1 red onion, cut into wedges
  • 100g cherry tomatoes
  • 50g soft goat's cheese
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, fresh basil, black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Toss aubergine, courgette, pepper and onion with the oil and oregano on a large tray. Roast 20 mins.
  2. Add the cherry tomatoes, roast 8 mins more until everything is soft and caught at the edges.
  3. Meanwhile cook the orzo, 9 mins. Drain, keeping a splash of the water.
  4. Toss the orzo through the tray with the balsamic and reserved water, scraping up the sticky bits.
  5. Crumble the goat's cheese over so it half-melts into the warm pasta. Tear basil over.
Crowd the tray and veg steams; give it space and it roasts. Use two trays if in doubt. The goat's cheese should go on at the very end — half-melted pockets, not a sauce.

Breakfast
Avocado & soft-boiled eggs on sourdough
~360 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • ½ ripe avocado
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Squeeze of lemon, pinch of chilli flakes
  • Small handful rocket
  • Sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Lower eggs into boiling water — 6½ minutes for jammy yolks. Straight into cold water after.
  2. Smash the avocado roughly with lemon, salt and pepper.
  3. Spread the avocado thickly on the toast, top with rocket.
  4. Peel and halve the eggs, sit them on top, finish with chilli flakes.
The cold-water plunge does two jobs: stops the yolk overcooking and shrinks the egg from its shell so it peels cleanly. Tap, roll, peel under a trickle of water.
Lunch
Tomato & cannellini panzanella
~400 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 vine tomatoes, cut into chunks
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 1 thick slice sourdough (stale is best), torn
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • 1 tsp capers, drained
  • Big handful fresh basil
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
Method
  1. Salt the tomato chunks in a large bowl and leave 10 mins — they release the juice that becomes your dressing.
  2. Toast the torn bread in a dry pan or low oven until golden and dry.
  3. Add beans, onion, cucumber, capers, oil and balsamic to the tomatoes. Toss.
  4. Fold in the toasted bread and torn basil, rest 5 mins so the bread soaks up the juices — softened at the edges, still crunchy in the middle.
Panzanella is a bread salad dressed in tomato juice, so the tomatoes must be salted and rested — no shortcuts. It's the right home for the end of a loaf.
Dinner
Paneer tikka skewers with cumin rice & kachumber
~490 kcal · 27g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g paneer, cut into chunks
  • 75g Greek yoghurt, 1 tbsp tikka or tandoori paste
  • ½ red pepper & ½ red onion, chunked
  • 60g rice (dry), ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • Kachumber: 1 vine tomato, ¼ cucumber, ¼ red onion, all diced; squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, fresh coriander
Method
  1. Mix yoghurt and tikka paste, coat the paneer and leave 15 mins (longer if you have it).
  2. Toast cumin seeds in a dry saucepan 30 seconds, add the rice and water, simmer lid-on 11 mins.
  3. Thread paneer, pepper and onion onto skewers. Brush with oil.
  4. Grill on high (or griddle) 10–12 mins, turning, until charred at the corners.
  5. Mix the kachumber with lemon and a pinch of salt. Serve skewers over the rice with kachumber and coriander.
Yoghurt marinades char beautifully because the milk sugars caramelise — that's the tandoori effect on a home grill. Soak wooden skewers 10 mins so they don't scorch.

Peanut butter & banana porridge
~360 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 250ml whole milk
  • 1 banana, sliced
  • 1 tbsp peanut butter
  • Pinch of cinnamon, 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Simmer oats and milk over low heat, stirring, 4–5 mins until creamy.
  2. Off the heat, swirl in the peanut butter — ribbons, not fully mixed.
  3. Top with banana, cinnamon and a thin drizzle of honey.
Peanut butter goes in off the heat — cooked through it goes claggy, swirled it stays in salty-sweet ribbons. The riper the banana, the less honey you'll want.
Lunch
Greek pasta salad with feta & olives
~410 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g fusilli (dry)
  • 40g feta, cubed
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • 6 cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 6 black olives, pitted
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon
  • Handful rocket
Method
  1. Cook the fusilli, drain and rinse briefly under the cold tap to stop it cooking.
  2. While still just warm, toss with olive oil, lemon and oregano.
  3. Fold in cucumber, tomatoes, olives and onion.
  4. Top with feta, rocket and a final pinch of oregano.
Dress pasta while it's warm and it absorbs the lemon and oil; dress it cold and the flavour just sits on the surface. Travels well if you're out on the bike.
Dinner
Pea, courgette & lemon risotto
~480 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g arborio rice
  • ½ courgette, diced small
  • 75g frozen petits pois
  • ½ onion, finely diced; 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 600ml hot vegetable stock
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated; 1 tsp butter
  • Zest & juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, few mint leaves
Method
  1. Soften onion in oil over medium-low, 5 mins. Add garlic and rice, stir 1 min until the grains turn translucent at the edges.
  2. Add the stock a ladle at a time, stirring, letting each addition absorb — about 18 mins in total.
  3. Stir in the courgette at the halfway point, the peas for the final 3 mins.
  4. Off the heat: butter, parmesan, lemon zest and juice. Beat hard for 30 seconds, lid on, rest 2 mins.
  5. Serve loose — it should spread on the plate. Tear mint over.
The final off-heat beating (the mantecatura) is what makes risotto creamy — it's agitation, not cream. If it stands like a dome on the plate, loosen it with more stock.

Breakfast
Eggs Florentine muffin
~360 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 wholemeal English muffin, split and toasted
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 50g Greek yoghurt, ½ tsp Dijon mustard, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tsp butter, splash of vinegar for poaching
  • Few chives, black pepper
Method
  1. Wilt the spinach in butter, season, and squeeze out excess water. Keep warm.
  2. Stir yoghurt, Dijon and lemon in a small bowl; warm it gently over the spinach pan for 30 seconds — a cheat's hollandaise.
  3. Poach the eggs: barely-simmering water, splash of vinegar, stir a gentle whirlpool, slide each egg in. 3 minutes.
  4. Stack: muffin halves, spinach, eggs, the warm sauce, chives and pepper.
The yoghurt sauce only needs to lose its fridge-chill — real heat splits it. Fresh eggs poach tighter; if in doubt, crack into a sieve first to drain the loose white.
Lunch
New potato & rocket tortilla
~400 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 200g new potatoes, thickly sliced
  • ½ onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful rocket, squeeze of lemon
  • Sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Simmer the potato slices 8 mins until just tender. Drain well.
  2. Soften the onion in oil in a small frying pan, 5 mins. Add the potatoes, spread evenly.
  3. Whisk and season the eggs, pour over, and cook on the lowest heat 8–10 mins until almost set.
  4. Finish under a hot grill 2 mins (or the brave plate-flip). Rest 5 mins before cutting.
  5. Serve warm with lemon-dressed rocket.
The rest is part of the recipe — a tortilla finishes setting off the heat and slices cleanly only once it's had its 5 minutes. The leftover wedge is tomorrow's snack, better cold.
Dinner
Quorn katsu curry with sticky rice
~500 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 Quorn fillets
  • 30g panko breadcrumbs, 2 tbsp plain flour, 1 egg, beaten
  • Sauce: ½ onion, 1 carrot, 1 garlic clove, 1 tsp mild curry powder, ½ tsp turmeric, 200ml vegetable stock, 1 tsp soy sauce, 1 tsp honey
  • 60g jasmine rice (dry)
  • Few slices cucumber
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Sauce first: soften chopped onion and carrot in a little oil, 8 mins. Add garlic and spices, 1 min, then stock, soy and honey. Simmer 10 mins, then blitz smooth.
  2. Rinse and simmer the rice, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  3. Coat the Quorn fillets: flour, egg, then panko, pressing the crumbs on.
  4. Fry in oil 4–5 mins per side until deep golden (or bake at 200°C for 20 mins, turning once).
  5. Slice the fillets, fan over the rice, pour the sauce over. Cucumber on the side.
Katsu sauce gets its body from blitzed onion and carrot, not flour — sweeter and silkier. Press the panko on firmly; loose crumbs burn in the pan instead of crisping on the fillet.

Breakfast
Summer berry baked oats
~350 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 1 large egg
  • 100ml whole milk
  • ½ ripe banana, mashed
  • ½ tsp baking powder, pinch of cinnamon
  • 75g British raspberries
  • 1 tsp honey, 1 tsp flaked almonds
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt to serve
Method
  1. Heat oven to 180°C. Mix oats, egg, milk, banana, baking powder and cinnamon in a small baking dish.
  2. Dot most of the raspberries through the mix, scatter almonds over.
  3. Bake 20–25 mins until risen and golden at the edges.
  4. Serve warm with yoghurt, the remaining raspberries and a thread of honey.
Baked oats are a flapjack you're allowed for breakfast — the mashed banana does the sweetening so the honey is just a finish. Longest day of the year; let the oven do the work.
Lunch
Stuffed romano peppers with herby rice & feta
~400 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 romano peppers, halved lengthways, seeds out
  • 50g rice (dry), cooked and cooled slightly
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 6 cherry tomatoes, quartered
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful each mint & basil, chopped; zest of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tbsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Rub the pepper halves with a little oil, roast empty 10 mins so they soften and sweeten.
  2. Mix the rice with tomatoes, spring onions, herbs, lemon zest, seeds and half the feta.
  3. Pile the filling into the peppers, top with remaining feta and a drizzle of oil.
  4. Roast 15 mins more until the edges of the peppers are caught and the feta is golden.
The empty pre-roast is the difference between a sweet, collapsing pepper and a crunchy raw one hiding under filling. Romanos are sweeter and thinner-walled than bell peppers — they earn their place.
Dinner
Broad bean, ricotta & lemon tagliatelle
~480 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g tagliatelle (dry)
  • 150g frozen broad beans
  • 75g ricotta
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 garlic clove, finely sliced
  • Zest & juice of ½ lemon
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the broad beans 3 mins, cool slightly, and pop them out of their grey skins.
  2. Cook the tagliatelle. Before draining, scoop out a mug of the pasta water.
  3. Warm the garlic gently in oil — golden, not brown. Off the heat, stir in ricotta, lemon zest and a ladle of pasta water to make a loose, silky sauce.
  4. Toss in the pasta, beans and mint, adding more water until it coats every ribbon. Lemon juice to finish.
  5. Plate with parmesan and a lot of black pepper.
Ricotta plus starchy pasta water is a 30-second sauce — no cream, no roux. Double-podding the broad beans takes two minutes and turns them vivid green and sweet.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

  • Creatine monohydrate (optional) 300g tub, 3–5g/day

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
