Breakfast
Pea & feta scramble on rye toast
~360 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 60g frozen petits pois
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 thick slice rye or sourdough, toasted
  • 1 tsp butter, fresh chives
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Defrost peas under hot water for 30 sec. Drain.
  2. Whisk eggs with a splash of water and black pepper.
  3. Melt butter in non-stick pan over low heat. Pour in eggs, stir gently.
  4. When eggs are still glossy, fold in peas and most of the feta. Pull off heat.
  5. Pile onto toast, scatter remaining feta and chives.
Low and slow makes the silkiest scramble — high heat turns it rubbery. Stop cooking just before it looks done.
Lunch
Asparagus, watercress & egg open sandwich
~370 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large egg
  • 6 British asparagus spears, woody ends snapped
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • 30g watercress
  • 1 tbsp Greek yoghurt, ½ tsp Dijon mustard (for spread)
  • Squeeze of lemon, olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Hard-boil egg 7 mins. Cool, peel, slice.
  2. Steam or boil asparagus 3–4 mins until just tender. Drain.
  3. Mix yoghurt with mustard, season.
  4. Spread yoghurt-mustard on toast. Layer watercress, then asparagus, then egg slices.
  5. Lemon, olive oil, plenty of black pepper.
Snap the asparagus rather than slicing — it breaks exactly where the woody part ends. Free quality control.
Dinner
Pea, lemon & butter bean orzo with feta
~490 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g orzo pasta (dry)
  • 1 × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 100g frozen petits pois
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 1 small onion, 2 garlic cloves
  • Zest & juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp butter
  • Fresh mint or chives, handful of rocket
Method
  1. Cook orzo per packet (~9 mins). Drain, reserve a ladle of water.
  2. Soften onion in oil 5 mins. Add garlic 1 min.
  3. Add butter beans and peas, splash of orzo water. Warm through 3 mins.
  4. Stir in orzo, lemon zest & juice, butter. Loosen with reserved water if needed.
  5. Plate up, scatter feta, rocket, mint and a final twist of pepper.
Orzo behaves more like risotto than pasta — a splash of its own cooking water makes the sauce silky without any cream.

Lunch
Hummus, roasted pepper & feta wrap
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large wholemeal tortilla wrap
  • 3 tbsp hummus
  • 1 small red pepper (or ½ large), sliced
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • Handful baby spinach or rocket
  • ¼ cucumber, sliced lengthways
  • Squeeze of lemon, pinch of chilli flakes
Method
  1. Heat a dry pan. Char pepper slices 4–5 mins until softened and lightly blistered. (Skip if using jarred roasted peppers.)
  2. Warm wrap in the same pan 20 seconds each side.
  3. Spread hummus across the wrap, leaving a 2cm border.
  4. Lay down spinach, peppers, cucumber and feta in a strip.
  5. Squeeze lemon, pinch of chilli. Fold the ends and roll tightly.
Charring the pepper in a dry pan in 5 minutes saves a full oven roast and gets you 80% of the smoky flavour.
Dinner
Mushroom & lentil ragù with spaghetti
~470 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g wholewheat spaghetti (dry)
  • 200g chestnut mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 1 × 400g tin green/brown lentils, drained
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 small onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stick, all finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tsp Marmite
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • Olive oil, salt & black pepper, fresh basil
Method
  1. Soften onion, carrot, celery in oil 8 mins. Add garlic and oregano 1 min.
  2. Add mushrooms, fry over high heat 5 mins until they release their water and start to brown.
  3. Stir in lentils, tomatoes and Marmite. Simmer 15 mins until thick and rich.
  4. Cook spaghetti per packet. Drain, reserving a splash of water.
  5. Toss pasta with ragù, loosen if needed. Top with parmesan and basil.
Marmite is the secret weapon in vegetarian ragù — a teaspoon adds the savoury depth that a meat sauce gets for free.

Breakfast
Soft-boiled eggs with asparagus soldiers
~340 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 8 British asparagus spears, woody ends snapped
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted and buttered
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Sea salt, cracked black pepper, lemon wedge
Method
  1. Bring a small pan of water to a rolling boil. Lower eggs in. Boil 6 minutes for soft, jammy yolks.
  2. Meanwhile, steam asparagus 3–4 mins until just tender.
  3. Toast and butter sourdough; cut into thick fingers.
  4. Set eggs in egg cups, crack the tops off. Serve with asparagus and toast for dipping.
  5. Season with salt and pepper, squeeze of lemon over the asparagus.
Asparagus makes brilliant "green soldiers" — a seasonal upgrade on the classic. Dip the tips into the yolk while it's still warm.
Lunch
Caprese-style sourdough plate
~390 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 125g ball of mozzarella, torn
  • 3 large vine tomatoes, thickly sliced
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Handful fresh basil leaves
  • 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Flaky sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Toast sourdough; rub with a cut garlic clove if you like.
  2. Layer tomato slices and torn mozzarella on a plate.
  3. Scatter basil. Drizzle with oil and balsamic.
  4. Salt the tomatoes generously. Plenty of cracked pepper.
  5. Serve with the toast on the side to mop up the juices.
Let the tomatoes sit with salt for 5 mins before adding the mozzarella — they release sweet, savoury juice that becomes the dressing.
Dinner
Sweet potato, chickpea & spinach curry
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 300g sweet potato, peeled and cubed
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 150g baby spinach
  • 1 × 400ml tin light coconut milk
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 2 tsp curry powder, 1 tsp turmeric, ½ tsp cumin
  • 80g brown rice (dry)
  • Juice of ½ lime, fresh coriander to serve
Method
  1. Cook rice per packet.
  2. Soften onion in a little oil 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger, spices — 1 min.
  3. Add sweet potato and coconut milk plus a splash of water. Simmer 15 mins until tender.
  4. Stir in chickpeas, simmer 3 mins. Add spinach by the handful until wilted.
  5. Squeeze of lime, scatter coriander, serve over rice.
Roughly mash a few of the cooked sweet potato cubes against the side of the pan — it thickens the sauce naturally without flour or cream.

Breakfast
Overnight oats with strawberries & almond butter
~360 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g British strawberries, hulled and sliced
  • 1 tbsp almond butter
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract, pinch of cinnamon
  • 1 tsp honey (optional)
Method
  1. The night before: combine oats, milk, half the yoghurt, vanilla and cinnamon in a jar or bowl. Stir well.
  2. Cover and refrigerate overnight (or at least 4 hours).
  3. In the morning: stir, top with remaining yoghurt, strawberries, a spoonful of almond butter and the seeds.
  4. Drizzle honey if using.
Make two jars at once and you've got Friday breakfast sorted too. They'll keep for 3 days in the fridge.
Lunch
Jacket potato with cottage cheese & chives
~390 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large baking potato (~300g), scrubbed
  • 200g reduced-fat cottage cheese
  • Small handful fresh chives, snipped
  • Handful rocket and a few radishes, sliced
  • 1 tsp olive oil, ½ tsp Dijon mustard, splash red wine vinegar
  • Sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Prick potato all over. Bake at 200°C for 75–90 mins until skin is crisp and inside fluffy. (Or microwave 8–10 mins then 20 mins in the oven for a quick crisp finish.)
  2. Whisk oil, mustard, vinegar with seasoning for the side salad.
  3. Cut a deep cross in the potato, squeeze to open. Salt the steamy inside.
  4. Pile in cottage cheese, scatter chives, plenty of black pepper.
  5. Serve with rocket and radish salad on the side.
Salting the freshly-opened potato makes the inside taste seasoned, not bland — most jackets fail because the inside is under-salted.
Dinner
Halloumi, courgette & pepper traybake
~480 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g halloumi, sliced into 1cm slabs
  • 1 courgette, half-moon sliced
  • 1 red pepper, cut into chunks
  • 1 red onion, cut into wedges
  • 60g couscous (dry)
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • Juice of ½ lemon, fresh mint or parsley
Method
  1. Heat oven to 220°C. Toss courgette, pepper and onion with oil, oregano, paprika, garlic and seasoning on a tray.
  2. Roast 15 mins. Add halloumi slices on top, return for 10 mins until veg is tender and halloumi golden.
  3. Meanwhile: pour 90ml boiling water over couscous, cover for 5 mins, fluff with a fork.
  4. Stir couscous into the tray, squeeze over lemon, scatter mint.
One tray, two waits, dinner is done. The lemon at the end lifts the whole thing — don't skip it.

Breakfast
Garlic mushrooms on sourdough with poached egg
~360 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large egg
  • 150g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, finely grated
  • 1 tsp butter, 1 tsp olive oil
  • Splash white wine vinegar (for poaching), fresh parsley or thyme
Method
  1. Heat butter and oil in a pan. Fry mushrooms over high heat 4–5 mins until golden and the water has cooked off.
  2. Add garlic and herbs, stir 30 seconds. Season.
  3. Simmer water with vinegar. Swirl and poach egg 3 mins.
  4. Pile mushrooms on toast. Top with egg, shave or grate parmesan over.
  5. Cracked pepper, eat immediately.
Don't crowd the mushrooms — they steam instead of brown. If your pan is small, do them in two batches and reunite at the end.
Lunch
Broad bean, bulgur & feta salad with mint
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g bulgur wheat (dry)
  • 200g podded broad beans (fresh or frozen)
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • Small handful fresh mint, chopped
  • 2 salad onions, finely sliced
  • Juice & zest of ½ lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, salt & black pepper
  • 1 tbsp toasted pumpkin seeds
Method
  1. Pour 150ml boiling water over bulgur. Cover, leave 12 mins. Fluff with fork.
  2. Blanch broad beans 2 mins. Drain, refresh in cold water. Pop out of their grey skins for the bright green inner beans.
  3. Toss bulgur with broad beans, salad onions, mint, lemon zest & juice, olive oil.
  4. Crumble over feta, scatter seeds, season generously.
Double-podding broad beans (pod, then skin) is a faff but the bright green inside tastes twice as good — peak of UK season right now.
Dinner
Tomato & mozzarella baked penne with basil
~480 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g penne pasta (dry)
  • 1 × 400g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 125g mozzarella, torn
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 small onion, 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, pinch of chilli flakes
  • Handful fresh basil leaves
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, salt & black pepper
  • Handful rocket on the side, lemon
Method
  1. Soften onion in oil 5 mins. Add garlic, oregano, chilli — 1 min.
  2. Add tomatoes, simmer 10 mins until rich. Season.
  3. Cook penne 2 mins less than packet (~7 mins). Drain.
  4. Toss pasta with sauce. Tip into a small baking dish. Top with mozzarella and parmesan.
  5. Bake at 200°C, 12 mins until bubbling and golden. Scatter basil, serve with rocket dressed in lemon and oil.
Cooking the pasta short keeps it from going mushy in the oven — it finishes baking in the sauce, which is where the magic happens.

Breakfast
Yoghurt pancakes with strawberries & honey
~380 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 80g plain flour
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 50ml whole milk
  • ½ tsp baking powder, pinch of salt
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 100g British strawberries, sliced
  • 1 tsp butter for the pan, 1 tsp honey to serve
Method
  1. Whisk eggs, yoghurt, milk and vanilla together.
  2. Sift in flour, baking powder, salt. Whisk to a thick, smooth batter.
  3. Heat a non-stick pan over medium-low. Melt a little butter.
  4. Drop heaped tablespoons of batter into the pan. Cook 1–2 mins until bubbles form on top, flip and cook 1 min more.
  5. Stack on a plate. Top with strawberries, drizzle of honey, extra yoghurt if you like.
Yoghurt makes the batter thicker than a typical pancake — so the cakes hold their shape and stay tender inside. Don't be tempted to thin it.
Lunch
Greek salad with feta, olives & warm pitta
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g feta, in one slab
  • 3 vine tomatoes, cut into chunks
  • ½ cucumber, half-moon sliced
  • ½ red onion, very thinly sliced
  • Handful Kalamata olives
  • 1 wholemeal pitta, warmed
  • 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, black pepper
Method
  1. Combine tomatoes, cucumber, red onion and olives in a bowl. Season generously.
  2. Whisk oil with vinegar, drizzle over salad. Toss.
  3. Set the feta slab on top — don't crumble it. Scatter with oregano.
  4. Warm pitta in a dry pan 30 sec each side. Tear and serve alongside.
A proper Greek salad lays the feta in one slab on top, not crumbled through. It's the brick the whole dish sits under.
Dinner
Ricotta & spinach stuffed peppers with quinoa
~470 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 red peppers, halved lengthways and de-seeded
  • 200g ricotta
  • 200g baby spinach
  • 50g quinoa (dry)
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • Zest of ½ lemon, fresh basil, pinch of nutmeg
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, salt & black pepper
  • Handful rocket on the side
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Brush pepper halves with oil, season, roast cut-side up 15 mins.
  2. Cook quinoa per packet, drain.
  3. Wilt spinach in a dry pan, 2 mins. Squeeze out water, chop roughly.
  4. Mix ricotta with spinach, quinoa, garlic, lemon zest, nutmeg, half the parmesan. Season.
  5. Spoon mixture into pepper halves. Scatter remaining parmesan. Bake 15–20 mins until golden.
  6. Scatter basil, serve with rocket on the side.
Squeezing the spinach properly is the difference between a creamy stuffing and a watery one — wring it like a flannel.

French toast with strawberries & yoghurt
~370 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 50ml whole milk
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract, pinch of cinnamon
  • 2 thick slices day-old sourdough or brioche
  • 1 tsp butter for the pan
  • 100g British strawberries, sliced
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp honey or maple syrup
Method

Day-old bread soaks the custard without falling apart — fresh bread turns to mush. The fridge is your friend here.

Dinner
Lentil shepherd's pie with sweet potato mash
~490 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 120g red lentils, rinsed
  • 500g sweet potato, peeled and cubed
  • 1 onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stick, all finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tbsp tomato purée, 1 tsp Marmite
  • 400ml veg stock
  • 100g frozen petits pois
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt or splash of milk, 1 tsp butter (for mash)
  • 250g spring greens, sliced (to steam alongside)
Method
  1. Boil sweet potato 12 mins until tender. Drain. Mash with yoghurt or milk, butter, salt.
  2. Soften onion, carrot, celery in oil 8 mins. Add garlic, thyme 1 min.
  3. Add lentils, tomato purée, Marmite and stock. Simmer 20 mins until lentils are soft and saucy.
  4. Stir in peas. Season. Tip into ovenproof dish. Spread mash on top, fork to rough peaks.
  5. Bake at 200°C, 20 mins until top is set and edges bubble.
  6. Steam spring greens 3–4 mins, season with salt and a splash of vinegar. Serve alongside.
Roughing the mash with a fork creates ridges that crisp in the oven — flat-topped mash bakes pale and sad.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
