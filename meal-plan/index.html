Breakfast
Raspberry & almond bircher
~350 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 150g raspberries
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds, toasted
  • 1 tsp honey, ½ tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. The night before: stir the oats, milk, vanilla and half the yoghurt together in a jar. Refrigerate.
  2. In the morning, crush half the raspberries with the honey to a rough purée.
  3. Ripple the purée through the oats with the remaining yoghurt — don't fully mix it.
  4. Top with the whole raspberries and the toasted almonds.
Rippling the crushed fruit through rather than stirring it in keeps bright seams of raspberry in every spoonful — and July British raspberries need nothing beyond that teaspoon of honey.
Lunch
Pea, mint & feta frittata with little gem salad
~410 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 75g frozen peas, defrosted
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 spring onion, sliced
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • Little gem leaves, 4 radishes, sliced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 slice sourdough
Method
  1. Heat the grill. Whisk the eggs, season, and fold in the peas, spring onion, mint and most of the feta.
  2. Pour into a small oiled ovenproof frying pan and cook over low heat 4–5 mins until the edges set.
  3. Slide under the grill 2–3 mins until puffed and golden. Rest 2 mins.
  4. Serve with the little gem and radishes dressed in the oil and lemon, remaining feta over, and the sourdough.
A frittata keeps cooking after it leaves the heat — pull it while the centre still has a slight wobble and it stays silky rather than rubbery.
Dinner
Chickpea & spinach curry with rice
~480 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 100g baby spinach
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cumin, ¼ tsp turmeric, pinch chilli
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger and the spices, 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Add the tomatoes, chickpeas and a splash of water; simmer 10 mins until thick.
  4. Wilt the spinach in a handful at a time, then take off the heat, swirl through the yoghurt and finish with coriander over the rice.
Take the pan off the heat before the yoghurt goes in — stirred into a bubbling sauce it splits, stirred into a resting one it turns the curry creamy.

Breakfast
Boiled eggs with rye toast & wilted spinach
~350 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 slices rye bread
  • Big handful baby spinach
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Few chives, snipped
  • Black pepper, pinch of salt
Method
  1. Lower the eggs into boiling water and cook 7 mins. Cool briefly in cold water and peel.
  2. Wilt the spinach in the butter over medium heat, 1 min. Season.
  3. Toast the rye bread and top with the spinach.
  4. Halve the eggs over the top and finish with chives and plenty of pepper.
Seven minutes is the sweet spot — a fully set white with a fudgy, just-soft centre that holds its shape on the toast.
Lunch
Greek salad with butter beans & pitta
~430 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large vine tomatoes, chunked
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • ½ × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 40g feta, in one slab
  • 6 Kalamata olives
  • 1 wholemeal pitta, toasted
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp dried oregano
Method
  1. Salt the tomato chunks and leave 5 mins to draw out their juices.
  2. Toss with the cucumber, onion, butter beans, olives, oil and vinegar.
  3. Sit the feta on top in a slab — don't crumble it — and scatter with the oregano.
  4. Serve with the toasted pitta for scooping.
The butter beans soak up the tomato juices and dressing like tiny sponges — they're what turns a side salad into lunch. Greeks serve the feta whole; it's better that way.
Dinner
Sweetcorn & courgette fritters with tzatziki
~470 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g frozen sweetcorn, defrosted
  • ½ courgette, coarsely grated and squeezed dry
  • 1 large egg
  • 3 tbsp plain flour, ½ tsp baking powder
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Tzatziki: 100g Greek yoghurt, ¼ cucumber grated & squeezed, small handful mint
  • Handful rocket, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Squeeze the grated courgette in a clean tea towel until no more water comes out.
  2. Whisk the egg, flour and baking powder into a thick batter; fold in the sweetcorn, courgette and spring onions. Season well.
  3. Fry spoonfuls in the oil over medium heat, 2–3 mins per side, until deep golden — you should get 4–5 fritters.
  4. Stir the tzatziki together and serve alongside, with the rocket dressed in lemon.
Water is the enemy of a crisp fritter — squeeze the courgette harder than feels reasonable, and squeeze the grated cucumber for the tzatziki too.

Breakfast
Cherry & toasted-oat yoghurt bowl
~350 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 150g cherries, halved and stoned
  • 30g rolled oats
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tsp honey, pinch of cinnamon
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. Toast the oats and almonds in a dry pan with the cinnamon, 3–4 mins, until golden and nutty.
  2. Off the heat, stir in the honey so it clumps into rough clusters. Cool 2 mins.
  3. Stir the vanilla through the yoghurt and spoon into a bowl.
  4. Top with the cherries and the crunchy oat clusters.
British cherries peak in mid-July and barely last a month — press each one with the flat of a knife and the stone slips out without a gadget.
Lunch
New potato, egg & watercress salad
~420 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g new potatoes, halved
  • 2 large eggs
  • Big handful watercress
  • 4 radishes, thinly sliced
  • Few chives, snipped
  • Dressing: 3 tbsp Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp Dijon, squeeze of lemon, 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Boil the potatoes in salted water 12–15 mins until tender. Boil the eggs alongside for 8 mins; cool and peel.
  2. Whisk the dressing ingredients together and season.
  3. Toss the potatoes in the dressing while still warm.
  4. Fold through the watercress and radishes, quarter the eggs over the top, and finish with chives.
Dress the potatoes warm — they drink up the dressing as they cool. Cold potatoes just wear it.
Dinner
Paneer tikka traybake with raita & pitta
~490 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g paneer, in large cubes
  • Marinade: 3 tbsp Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp smoked paprika, ¼ tsp turmeric, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 red pepper, chunked
  • ½ red onion, in wedges
  • Raita: 50g Greek yoghurt, ¼ cucumber grated, few mint leaves
  • 1 wholemeal pitta
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Stir the marinade together, coat the paneer and leave 15 mins (longer if you can).
  2. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the pepper and onion with the oil and roast 10 mins.
  3. Add the paneer to the tray and roast 12–15 mins more, until charred at the edges.
  4. Stir the raita together, warm the pitta, and pile everything in.
Paneer doesn't melt, so it can take fierce heat — the goal is charred corners and a springy middle. The yoghurt marinade is what catches and colours.

Breakfast
Mushroom, spinach & cheddar omelette
~360 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Handful baby spinach
  • 20g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 slice sourdough, toasted
  • 1 tsp butter, black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in a hot, dry pan 4–5 mins until golden; add the spinach to wilt, then tip out.
  2. Whisk the eggs with a pinch of salt. Melt the butter in the same pan over medium heat.
  3. Add the eggs, drawing the edges in as they set. While still glossy on top, scatter over the cheese and the mushroom mix.
  4. Fold, slide onto the plate, and serve with the toast.
Mushrooms brown in a dry pan and steam in a buttered one — fat comes after colour, not before. It's the difference between golden and grey.
Lunch
Roasted pepper & tomato soup with butter-bean smash toast
~420 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 red pepper, chunked
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ¼ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 300ml vegetable stock
  • ¼ tsp smoked paprika
  • ½ × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Soften the onion and pepper in 1 tbsp of the oil, 8 mins, letting the pepper catch a little at the edges.
  2. Add the garlic and paprika, 1 min, then the tomatoes and stock. Simmer 12 mins.
  3. Blitz until smooth and season boldly.
  4. Smash the butter beans on the toast with the remaining oil, the lemon and plenty of pepper. Dunk.
Letting the pepper char slightly in the pan stands in for a full roast — those caught edges are where the smoky depth comes from.
Dinner
Runner bean & crispy tofu stir-fry with noodles
~480 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g extra-firm tofu, cubed and patted very dry
  • 100g runner beans, sliced on the angle
  • 1 carrot, in matchsticks
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1 nest egg noodles
  • 1½ tbsp soy sauce, ½ lime
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, 1 tbsp mixed seeds, toasted
Method
  1. Fry the tofu in the oil over high heat, turning only when each side is deep golden, 6–8 mins total. Remove.
  2. Stir-fry the runner beans and carrot 3 mins; add garlic and ginger, 30 secs.
  3. Cook the noodles per the pack, drain, and add to the pan with the tofu, soy and a splash of water.
  4. Toss until glossy, squeeze over the lime, and finish with spring onions and seeds.
Dry tofu is crispy tofu — press it in kitchen paper, get the pan properly hot, and don't move it until it releases by itself. Crowded, damp tofu just stews.

Ingredients

  • 1 banana
  • 1 tbsp peanut butter

Mashing half the banana into the pot sweetens every spoonful from the inside — most mornings you won't need the honey at all.
Lunch
Beetroot, goat's cheese & lentil salad
~420 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g cooked beetroot, in wedges
  • 50g soft goat's cheese
  • ½ × 250g pouch cooked Puy lentils
  • Big handful rocket
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds, toasted
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
Method
  1. Fluff the lentils apart and toss with half the oil and the balsamic.
  2. Fold through the beetroot and rocket.
  3. Dot the goat's cheese over in small nuggets — add it last so it stays white against the pink.
  4. Finish with the toasted seeds and the remaining oil.
Beetroot and balsamic are old friends — the vinegar's sweetness sharpens the earthiness. Adding the cheese last keeps the salad from turning uniformly fuchsia.
Dinner
Smoky black bean burger with carrot slaw
~500 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ¾ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 1 large egg, beaten (use half to bind)
  • 30g breadcrumbs (blitzed from sourdough)
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 spring onion, finely sliced
  • 1 burger bun; little gem leaves
  • 25g mature cheddar
  • Slaw: 1 carrot, grated; 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Mash three-quarters of the beans, leaving the rest whole for texture. Mix in the half egg, breadcrumbs, spices and spring onion; season.
  2. Shape into a thick patty and chill 10 mins to firm up.
  3. Fry in the oil over medium heat, 3–4 mins per side; lay the cheddar on top for the final minute to melt.
  4. Stir the slaw together. Toast the bun and stack: little gem, patty, slaw.
Leaving a quarter of the beans whole is what separates a burger from a paste — and the 10-minute chill is what stops it falling apart in the pan.

Breakfast
Poached eggs & spinach muffin
~350 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 English muffin, split and toasted
  • 2 handfuls baby spinach
  • Quick sauce: 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, ½ tsp Dijon, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Splash of vinegar (for the poaching water), black pepper
Method
  1. Bring a pan of water with the vinegar to a bare simmer. Crack each egg into a cup and slide in gently; poach 3 mins.
  2. Wilt the spinach in the butter and season.
  3. Stir the sauce together — it's a 20-second mock hollandaise.
  4. Stack: muffin halves, spinach, eggs, sauce, plenty of pepper.
The freshest eggs poach neatest — the white clings instead of feathering. A bare simmer, not a boil: bubbles tear the white apart.
Lunch
Roasted courgette & red pepper hummus wrap
~420 kcal · 13g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 courgette, in thick batons
  • 1 red pepper, in strips
  • 1 tortilla wrap
  • 3 tbsp hummus
  • Handful rocket
  • ½ tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon, pinch of chilli flakes
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Toss the courgette and pepper with the oil and roast 22–25 mins, until caramelised at the edges.
  2. Warm the wrap briefly in a dry pan.
  3. Spread with the hummus, right to the edges.
  4. Pile on the veg and rocket, add lemon and chilli flakes, and roll tightly.
Roast the veg past merely soft and into properly caramelised — that concentrated sweetness is what carries the whole wrap.
Dinner
Aubergine & mozzarella parmigiana
~490 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 aubergine, in 1cm rounds
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 15g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • Handful basil, 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 slice sourdough
Method
  1. Brush the aubergine with most of the oil and griddle or grill until deep golden on both sides, 8–10 mins.
  2. Make a quick sauce: garlic in the remaining oil 30 secs, then tomatoes and oregano, 8 mins.
  3. Layer sauce, aubergine and basil in a small baking dish, finishing with the mozzarella and hard cheese.
  4. Bake at 200°C for 20 mins until blistered and bubbling. Serve with the sourdough to mop up.
The aubergine must be properly golden before it goes in the dish — undercooked aubergine never recovers in the oven, it just goes spongy. Golden first, then layer.

Lunch
Courgette, pea & basil soup with cheddar toast
~400 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 courgette, chopped
  • 100g frozen peas
  • ¼ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 400ml vegetable stock
  • Handful basil
  • 1 slice sourdough + 30g mature cheddar
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Soften the onion in the oil, 4 mins. Add the garlic and courgette, 4 mins more.
  2. Add the stock and simmer 8 mins.
  3. Add the peas and basil for the final 2 mins only, then blitz until smooth. Season and brighten with lemon.
  4. Grill the cheddar on the sourdough until molten, and dunk.
Peas and basil go in at the very end — long cooking turns green soup khaki. Two minutes keeps it vivid and fresh-tasting.
Dinner
Roasted tomato & ricotta puff tart with rocket
~500 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ⅓ × 320g sheet ready-rolled puff pastry
  • 100g ricotta
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Zest of ½ lemon, 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Splash of milk, black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Score a 1cm border around the pastry — score, don't cut through.
  2. Beat the ricotta with the lemon zest, oregano, salt and pepper, and spread inside the border.
  3. Arrange the tomatoes cut-side up, brush the border with milk, and bake 22–25 mins until puffed and golden.
  4. Top with the rocket tossed in the oil and balsamic, straight over the warm tart.
Seasoning the ricotta properly is what makes the tart — plain ricotta bakes bland. And the scored border puffs into a golden frame around the filling.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
