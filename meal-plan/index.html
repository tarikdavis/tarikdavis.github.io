Breakfast
Shakshuka-style baked eggs with spinach
~350 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • ½ onion, finely sliced
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • Big handful baby spinach
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 slice sourdough
  • Fresh coriander, black pepper
Method
  1. Soften onion and pepper in oil over medium heat, 6 mins. Add garlic and spices, 1 min.
  2. Pour in tomatoes with a splash of water. Simmer 5 mins until thickened. Fold in spinach to wilt. Season.
  3. Make two wells in the sauce. Crack an egg into each, lid on, cook 4–5 mins until whites set but yolks wobble.
  4. Scatter coriander. Serve straight from the pan with toasted sourdough for dunking.
The lid is the trick — it steams the egg whites from above so you don't have to overcook the base waiting for them. Take the pan off while the yolks still tremble.
Lunch
Pea, watercress & spring onion soup with cheddar toast
~380 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 300g frozen petits pois
  • 80g watercress
  • 3 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 500ml hot vegetable stock
  • 50g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 slice sourdough, 30g mature cheddar
  • 1 tsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Soften spring onions in oil, 2 mins. Add garlic, 30 seconds.
  2. Pour in stock, bring to a simmer. Add peas, cook 3 mins only.
  3. Off the heat, add watercress — it wilts in seconds. Blitz smooth with a stick blender.
  4. Stir through most of the yoghurt, season, add lemon to lift it.
  5. Toast the sourdough, top with cheddar and grill 2 mins until bubbling. Serve the soup with a swirl of remaining yoghurt and the toast alongside.
Three minutes is all the peas get — any longer and the soup turns khaki. Adding the watercress off the heat keeps its pepperiness intact.
Dinner
Quorn & black bean chilli with brown rice
~480 kcal · 30g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g Quorn mince
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ½ onion, diced; 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp chilli powder
  • 60g brown rice (dry)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp cocoa or dark soy (optional depth)
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, fresh coriander, lime wedge
Method
  1. Get the brown rice on first — simmer 25 mins in salted water, drain.
  2. Soften onion in oil, 5 mins. Add garlic and spices, 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Stir in Quorn mince straight from frozen, coating it in the spices, 2 mins.
  4. Add tomatoes, black beans and a splash of water. Simmer 12 mins until thick. Stir in the cocoa or soy if using.
  5. Serve over rice with yoghurt, coriander and a big squeeze of lime.
Quorn mince doesn't need browning — its job is to carry the spices. The half-teaspoon of cocoa sounds odd but adds the same dark depth a long-simmered meat chilli gets for free.

Breakfast
Honey-roast rhubarb with Greek yoghurt & granola
~330 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g rhubarb, cut into 5cm batons
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • Juice & zest of ½ orange
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 30g granola
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. Heat oven to 180°C. Lay rhubarb in a single layer in a small dish, drizzle with honey and the orange juice, scatter the zest.
  2. Roast 15 mins until tender but still holding its shape — don't stir.
  3. Spoon yoghurt into a bowl, top with the warm rhubarb and its syrupy juices.
  4. Finish with granola and seeds.
Roast — never stew — rhubarb you want to keep whole. Make a double batch; it keeps a week in the fridge and is just as good cold tomorrow.
Lunch
Broad bean, pea & feta smash on sourdough
~400 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g frozen broad beans
  • 50g frozen petits pois
  • 50g feta, crumbled
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Small handful fresh mint, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, juice & zest of ½ lemon
  • Pinch of chilli flakes, handful rocket
Method
  1. Boil broad beans and peas 3 mins. Drain, cool slightly, then pop the broad beans out of their grey skins.
  2. Crush both roughly with a fork — half purée, half whole.
  3. Fold in mint, lemon zest & juice, olive oil and most of the feta. Season with pepper (the feta brings the salt).
  4. Pile onto toast, top with remaining feta, chilli flakes and rocket.
Skinning broad beans feels fussy but takes two minutes and is the whole dish — the bright green inner bean is sweet and nutty where the skin is leathery and bitter.
Dinner
Sticky ginger tofu noodles with pak choi
~470 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 1 nest (60g) egg noodles
  • 1 head pak choi, quartered lengthways
  • 1 carrot, ribboned with a peeler
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 tbsp cornflour
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp honey, 1 tsp sesame oil
  • Thumb of ginger, grated; 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, 1 tsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. Pat tofu dry, toss in cornflour. Mix soy, honey, sesame oil, ginger and garlic with 3 tbsp water for the sauce.
  2. Cook noodles per packet, drain, rinse briefly.
  3. Fry tofu in hot neutral oil, 6–8 mins, turning until golden and crisp on all sides. Lift out.
  4. Same pan: pak choi cut-side down, 2 mins, then carrot ribbons, 1 min.
  5. Return tofu, pour in sauce — it bubbles and turns glossy in 30 seconds. Toss in noodles, coat everything. Top with spring onions and seeds.
Cornflour is the difference between pale, soggy tofu and a crisp shell that grips the sticky sauce. Press the tofu first — drier tofu fries, wet tofu steams.

Breakfast
Asparagus soldiers with soft-boiled eggs
~330 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 8 British asparagus spears, woody ends snapped
  • 1 slice sourdough, toasted and cut into fingers
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, shaved
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Squeeze of lemon, sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Lower eggs into boiling water. Exactly 6 minutes for set whites and molten yolks.
  2. Meanwhile, steam or griddle the asparagus 3 mins until just tender with bite.
  3. Toss the warm asparagus with butter, lemon and a pinch of salt.
  4. Sit the eggs in cups, slice the tops off. Dip asparagus spears and toast fingers into the yolks. Shave parmesan over the spears.
This is the last few weeks of British asparagus — the season closes around the longest day. Asparagus spears make better soldiers than toast ever did.
Lunch
Greek-style butter bean salad with pitta
~400 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 40g feta, cubed
  • ½ cucumber, chunked
  • 2 vine tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 8 black olives, pitted
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon
  • 1 wholemeal pitta, toasted
Method
  1. Toss tomatoes, cucumber and red onion with a pinch of salt. Sit 5 mins — they'll release sweet juices.
  2. Add butter beans and olives, then olive oil, lemon and oregano. Toss.
  3. Top with feta cubes and a final dusting of oregano and pepper.
  4. Serve with warm toasted pitta to scoop.
Butter beans turn a Greek salad into lunch — they soak up the tomato juices and dressing like little sponges. Salting the veg first makes its own vinaigrette.
Dinner
Mushroom & lentil ragu with spaghetti
~480 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g chestnut mushrooms, finely chopped
  • ½ × 400g tin green/brown lentils, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ½ onion, finely diced; 2 garlic cloves, chopped
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tsp soy sauce
  • 80g spaghetti (dry)
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, fresh basil, black pepper
Method
  1. Fry mushrooms in oil over high heat, 6 mins, until their water cooks off and they brown properly.
  2. Lower heat, add onion, 4 mins. Add garlic and oregano, 1 min.
  3. Stir in lentils, tomatoes, soy and a splash of water. Simmer 12 mins until thick and rich.
  4. Cook spaghetti per packet. Drain, reserving a ladle of water.
  5. Toss pasta through the ragu, loosening with pasta water. Plate, grate parmesan over, tear basil.
Chop the mushrooms small and brown them hard — that's where the meaty depth comes from. The teaspoon of soy is pure umami; nobody will spot it.

Breakfast
Chive scrambled eggs on toasted muffin
~340 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • Splash of whole milk
  • 1 tsp butter
  • 1 wholemeal English muffin, split and toasted
  • Handful baby spinach
  • Small bunch chives, snipped
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Wilt spinach in a dry pan 1 min, set aside. Wipe the pan.
  2. Whisk eggs with milk and a pinch of salt.
  3. Melt butter over low heat. Add eggs and stir slowly and constantly with a spatula — 3–4 mins for soft, creamy curds.
  4. Pull off the heat while still slightly loose; they finish on the way to the plate.
  5. Pile spinach then eggs onto the muffin halves, shower with chives and pepper.
Low and slow is the whole secret — scrambled eggs cooked over high heat squeeze out their moisture and turn rubbery. If you can hear them cooking, the pan's too hot.
Lunch
Caprese white bean salad with sourdough
~410 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ ball (60g) mozzarella, torn
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 2 vine tomatoes, sliced
  • Big handful fresh basil
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • 1 slice sourdough, toasted
  • Sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Lay tomato slices on a plate, season with salt, rest 5 mins.
  2. Toss cannellini beans with half the oil and a little balsamic.
  3. Scatter beans and rocket over the tomatoes, tear mozzarella over the top.
  4. Finish with basil, remaining oil and balsamic, plenty of pepper. Sourdough on the side.
A caprese is a tomato dish wearing cheese, not the other way round — so the tomatoes get seasoned and rested first. The beans quietly double the protein without changing the character.
Dinner
Quorn sausage & new potato traybake
~480 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 Quorn sausages
  • 250g new potatoes, halved
  • 1 red onion, cut into wedges
  • 1 courgette, thickly sliced
  • 100g cherry tomatoes
  • 100g spring greens, shredded
  • 2 tbsp olive oil, few sprigs fresh thyme
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard, juice of ½ lemon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Toss potatoes and red onion with 1 tbsp oil and thyme on a large tray. Roast 15 mins.
  2. Add sausages, courgette and cherry tomatoes with the remaining oil. Roast 20 mins more, turning once, until everything is golden.
  3. In the last 5 mins, scatter the spring greens over the tray — they crisp at the edges like cavolo nero.
  4. Whisk Dijon with lemon and any tray juices. Drizzle over and serve.
One tray, staggered additions — potatoes need a head start, sausages and courgette don't. Spring greens added late turn half-crisp, half-steamed: the best of both.

Breakfast
Strawberry & vanilla overnight oats
~340 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g British strawberries, hulled and chopped
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp honey, 1 tbsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. The night before: stir oats, milk, half the yoghurt and vanilla together in a jar. Refrigerate.
  2. In the morning: mash half the strawberries with a fork into a quick no-cook jam.
  3. Ripple the mashed strawberries through the oats.
  4. Top with remaining yoghurt, the rest of the strawberries, honey and seeds.
Mashing half the berries is the move — the juice ripples through every spoonful instead of sitting on top. June strawberries need no help beyond a fork.
Lunch
Courgette & carrot fritters with minted yoghurt
~400 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 courgette, coarsely grated
  • 1 carrot, coarsely grated
  • 1 large egg
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 3 tbsp plain flour
  • 1 spring onion, sliced
  • 100g Greek yoghurt, small handful mint, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, handful rocket
Method
  1. Salt the grated courgette, sit 10 mins, then squeeze out as much liquid as you can in a clean tea towel.
  2. Mix courgette, carrot, egg, feta, flour and spring onion into a thick batter. Season with pepper.
  3. Heat oil in a frying pan. Drop in heaped tablespoons, flatten slightly, fry 3 mins each side until deep golden. Makes about 5.
  4. Stir chopped mint and lemon into the yoghurt.
  5. Serve fritters with the minted yoghurt and rocket.
Squeezing the courgette is non-negotiable — it's 95% water and soggy fritters are a courgette problem, not a recipe problem. Be ruthless with the tea towel.
Dinner
Thai green tofu & broccoli curry with jasmine rice
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g firm tofu, cubed
  • 150g tenderstem broccoli, halved
  • 50g frozen petits pois
  • 2 tbsp Thai green curry paste (check it's vegetarian — no fish sauce)
  • 200ml half-fat coconut milk
  • 60g jasmine rice (dry)
  • 1 tsp soy sauce, juice of ½ lime
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, fresh coriander
Method
  1. Rinse rice, then simmer in 120ml salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest off the heat.
  2. Fry tofu in oil until golden on all sides, 6 mins. Lift out.
  3. Fry curry paste in the same pan, 1 min, until fragrant. Pour in coconut milk and 100ml water, simmer 3 mins.
  4. Add broccoli, 4 mins. Add peas and tofu, 2 mins more.
  5. Season with soy and lime. Serve over rice, scattered with coriander.
Many supermarket Thai pastes hide fish sauce or shrimp — Thai Taste and Blue Dragon both do vegetarian-friendly green pastes. Frying the paste first wakes the aromatics up.

Breakfast
Blueberry & banana oat pancakes
~360 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 ripe banana
  • 1 large egg
  • 50g rolled oats
  • ½ tsp baking powder, pinch of cinnamon
  • 75g blueberries
  • 1 tsp butter for the pan
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp honey to serve
Method
  1. Blitz banana, egg, oats, baking powder and cinnamon to a thick batter. Rest 5 mins — the oats swell.
  2. Heat a non-stick pan over medium-low, melt a little butter.
  3. Drop in heaped tablespoons of batter, press 3–4 blueberries into each. Cook 2 mins until bubbles appear, flip, 1 min more.
  4. Stack, top with yoghurt, remaining blueberries and a drizzle of honey.
Resting the batter lets the oats drink up the banana and bind — skip it and the pancakes fall apart at the flip. Pressing berries in (rather than mixing through) stops the batter going grey.
Lunch
Courgette, pea & mint frittata with rocket
~390 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • ½ courgette, coarsely grated and squeezed dry
  • 50g frozen petits pois, defrosted
  • 20g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • Small handful fresh mint, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful rocket, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Heat the grill. Whisk eggs with parmesan, mint and pepper.
  2. Soften the courgette in oil in a small ovenproof frying pan, 2 mins. Add peas, 1 min.
  3. Pour in the eggs, stir once, then cook undisturbed over low heat 4 mins until the base sets.
  4. Slide under the grill 2 mins until puffed and golden on top.
  5. Serve in wedges with lemon-dressed rocket.
A frittata wants low heat and patience — high heat makes the bottom leathery before the middle sets. The grill finish gets you a soufflé-light top with no risky flip.
Dinner
Garden flatbread pizzas with mozzarella & rocket
~500 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 folded flatbreads (or 1 large)
  • 4 tbsp passata
  • ½ ball (60g) mozzarella, torn
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ½ courgette, ribboned with a peeler
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, pinch of chilli flakes
  • Handful rocket, 1 tsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 220°C with a baking tray inside — a hot tray crisps the base like a pizza stone.
  2. Stir oregano into the passata, spread thinly over the flatbreads.
  3. Top with mozzarella, cherry tomatoes and courgette ribbons. Season.
  4. Slide onto the hot tray, bake 8–10 mins until edges char and cheese bubbles.
  5. Scatter chilli flakes and rocket, drizzle of olive oil, eat immediately.
The preheated tray is the entire trick — flatbreads go cracker-crisp underneath instead of steaming soggy. Less sauce than feels right; thin is the way.

Breakfast
Strawberry French toast with Greek yoghurt
~370 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 slices sourdough (slightly stale is perfect)
  • 2 large eggs
  • 50ml whole milk
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract, pinch of cinnamon
  • 1 tsp butter
  • 100g British strawberries, sliced
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Whisk eggs, milk, vanilla and cinnamon in a shallow dish.
  2. Soak each bread slice 30 seconds per side — properly saturated, not just dipped.
  3. Melt butter over medium heat. Fry the bread 2–3 mins each side until deep golden and puffed.
  4. Top with strawberries, yoghurt and a thin drizzle of honey.
Day-old bread soaks up the custard without collapsing — fresh bread turns to mush. This is the right home for the end of the week's sourdough.
Lunch
New potato, radish & egg salad
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 250g new potatoes, scrubbed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 4 radishes, thinly sliced
  • Handful watercress
  • 1 tsp capers, drained
  • 50g Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp Dijon, squeeze of lemon
  • Small bunch chives, snipped; black pepper
Method
  1. Boil potatoes 12 mins until tender. Lower the eggs into the same pan for the final 7 mins.
  2. Drain. Cool eggs under cold water, peel and quarter. Halve the potatoes.
  3. Whisk yoghurt with Dijon, lemon and most of the chives into a dressing.
  4. Toss warm potatoes in the dressing — they drink it up while warm.
  5. Fold in radishes, capers and watercress. Top with eggs, remaining chives and pepper.
Dress potatoes while they're still warm — cold potatoes shrug dressing off, warm ones absorb it. Radishes are at their crisp, peppery best right now.
Dinner
Spiced bean & beetroot burger with minted slaw
~480 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin kidney beans, drained (makes 2 patties — freeze one)
  • 1 cooked beetroot, coarsely grated and squeezed dry
  • 40g rolled oats
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 wholemeal roll, halved and toasted
  • ½ little gem lettuce; ¼ cucumber, ribboned
  • 50g Greek yoghurt, small handful mint, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Mash beans roughly — half paste, half whole. Mix in beetroot, oats, egg and spices. Season well.
  2. Shape into 2 patties, chill 15 mins to firm up. Freeze one for a no-effort dinner another week.
  3. Stir mint and lemon into the yoghurt; toss half with the lettuce and cucumber for a quick slaw.
  4. Fry one patty in oil over medium heat, 4 mins per side, until crusted and hot through.
  5. Build: toasted roll, minted yoghurt, patty, slaw on top and alongside.
Chilling the patty is the difference between a burger and a spatula full of crumbs. The beetroot keeps it moist and gives a colour shop-bought versions only dream of.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

  • Creatine monohydrate (optional) 300g tub, 3–5g/day

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
