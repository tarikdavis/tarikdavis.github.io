Breakfast
Strawberry & almond overnight oats
~350 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g British strawberries, hulled and sliced
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tsp honey, ½ tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. The night before: stir oats, milk, vanilla and half the yoghurt together in a jar. Refrigerate.
  2. In the morning, toss half the strawberries with the honey and lightly crush — they slump into a quick compote.
  3. Toast the flaked almonds in a dry pan, 2 mins, shaking often.
  4. Top the oats with the remaining yoghurt, the crushed and fresh strawberries, and the almonds.
British strawberries are at their peak right now — the riper ones at the bottom of the punnet are perfect for crushing into compote. Soaking the oats overnight makes them gentler on digestion.
Lunch
Courgette & feta frittata with green salad
~410 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 small courgette, coarsely grated
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful mint & dill, chopped
  • Handful rocket & ¼ cucumber, to serve
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Squeeze the grated courgette in a clean cloth to wring out the water — this keeps the frittata from going soggy.
  2. Whisk the eggs, fold in courgette, spring onions, herbs, feta and pepper.
  3. Heat the oil in a small ovenproof pan, pour in the mix, cook on low 6–7 mins until the edges set.
  4. Finish under a hot grill 3 mins until puffed and golden. Serve warm or cold with lemon-dressed rocket and cucumber.
Wringing out the courgette is the one step people skip and then wonder why it's watery. Grated courgette is also a sneaky way to get a portion of veg into breakfast or lunch.
Dinner
Black bean & roasted pepper quesadillas
~490 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • ½ red onion, sliced
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 2 large tortilla wraps
  • 50g mature cheddar, grated
  • ½ avocado, sliced; 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, ½ lime, fresh coriander
Method
  1. Char the pepper and onion in the oil over high heat, 6 mins. Add the beans and spices, mash lightly with a splash of water to a chunky, scoopable mix.
  2. Spread over half of each tortilla, scatter cheddar, fold into a half-moon.
  3. Dry-fry each quesadilla 2–3 mins per side, pressing down, until crisp and the cheese melts.
  4. Cut into wedges. Serve with avocado, yoghurt, coriander and a squeeze of lime.
Lightly mashing the beans is what holds the filling together so it doesn't tumble out when you cut it. Press the quesadilla with a spatula as it cooks for an even, crisp crust.

Breakfast
Blueberry & lemon ricotta pancakes
~360 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large egg
  • 75g ricotta
  • 40g self-raising flour
  • 50ml whole milk
  • 75g blueberries
  • Zest of ½ lemon, 1 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp butter for the pan
Method
  1. Whisk egg, ricotta, milk and lemon zest, then fold in the flour to a thick, lumpy batter — don't overmix.
  2. Melt the butter in a non-stick pan over medium-low. Drop in spoonfuls, scatter a few blueberries on each.
  3. Cook 2 mins until bubbles appear on top, flip, 1–2 mins more until golden.
  4. Stack and serve with the remaining blueberries and a thread of honey.
Ricotta keeps these pancakes light and tender where flour alone would make them dense. A lumpy batter is the right batter — overworked gluten turns them rubbery.
Lunch
Caprese giant couscous salad
~400 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g giant (pearl) couscous
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Big handful fresh basil
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Black pepper, pinch of salt
Method
  1. Simmer the giant couscous in plenty of salted water, 8–9 mins, until tender with a little bite. Drain and cool under the tap.
  2. Salt the halved tomatoes and leave 5 mins to release their juice.
  3. Toss the couscous with the tomatoes and their juice, oil and balsamic.
  4. Fold through the torn mozzarella, basil and rocket just before eating.
Add the mozzarella and basil at the last moment — basil bruises and mozzarella weeps if they sit dressed for too long. Cooling the couscous under the tap stops it clumping into a paste.
Dinner
Saag paneer with cumin rice
~490 kcal · 27g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g paneer, cubed
  • 200g baby spinach
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cumin, ¼ tsp turmeric
  • 100g (¼ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 60g basmati rice (dry), ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Toast cumin seeds in a dry pan, add the rinsed rice and water, simmer lid-on 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Fry the paneer cubes in half the oil until golden on all sides, then set aside.
  3. Soften onion in the rest of the oil, 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger and ground spices, 1 min, then tomatoes, cook 3 mins.
  4. Wilt the spinach in batches, then blitz half the pan smooth for a silky sauce. Return the paneer, stir in the yoghurt off the heat.
  5. Serve over the cumin rice.
Frying the paneer first gives it a golden skin that holds up in the sauce instead of going spongy. Stir the yoghurt in off the heat so it enriches rather than splits.

Breakfast
Soft scrambled eggs & cherry tomatoes on toast
~340 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • 8 cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1 tsp butter, splash of milk
  • Few chives, snipped
  • 1 tsp olive oil, sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Blister the tomatoes in the olive oil over high heat, 3 mins, until they slump and char. Season.
  2. Whisk the eggs with the milk and a pinch of salt.
  3. Melt the butter in a low pan, add the eggs and stir slowly with a spatula, taking them off the heat while still glossy and just-set.
  4. Pile onto the toast, spoon the tomatoes alongside, finish with chives and pepper.
Low and slow with constant stirring is the whole secret to creamy scrambled eggs — pull them off the heat a touch underdone, as they keep cooking on the plate.
Lunch
Beetroot, lentil & feta salad
~410 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin green lentils, drained
  • 150g cooked beetroot (vac-packed, not in vinegar), diced
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • Handful rocket
  • 2 tbsp walnut pieces
  • Small handful mint & dill, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ tsp Dijon, black pepper
Method
  1. Whisk the oil, vinegar and Dijon into a dressing in the bottom of a bowl.
  2. Toss the lentils through the dressing while you add the beetroot last, so it doesn't stain everything pink.
  3. Fold in the rocket and herbs.
  4. Scatter the feta and walnuts over the top and finish with plenty of black pepper.
Beetroot is at its best now — buy it cooked and vacuum-packed (not the pickled jar sort) for an instant earthy sweetness. Dress the lentils while you assemble so they soak up the vinegar.
Dinner
Tofu pad thai
~480 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 70g flat rice noodles
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 1 carrot, ribboned; 1 spring onion, sliced; handful beansprouts
  • Sauce: 1½ tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp tamarind paste (or lime juice), 1 tsp honey
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 1 tbsp chopped peanuts, ½ lime, fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Soak the noodles in just-boiled water until pliable, 6–8 mins. Drain.
  2. Fry the tofu in the oil until golden, push to one side, pour the beaten egg into the gap and scramble briefly.
  3. Add garlic, carrot and the sauce ingredients, toss 1 min.
  4. Add the noodles and a splash of water, tossing until coated and glossy. Fold through the beansprouts and spring onion.
  5. Top with peanuts, coriander and a squeeze of lime.
Have everything chopped before the pan goes on — pad thai cooks in three minutes flat and there's no time to prep mid-stir. Tamarind gives the authentic sour tang, but lime juice is a fine stand-in.

Breakfast
Strawberry & banana smoothie bowl
~350 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g British strawberries, hulled
  • ½ frozen banana
  • 150g Greek yoghurt
  • 50ml whole milk
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds, 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Blend most of the strawberries with the frozen banana, two-thirds of the yoghurt and the milk until thick and scoopable — add milk a splash at a time.
  2. Pour into a bowl; it should be thick enough to hold a spoon.
  3. Spoon the rest of the yoghurt on top, slice the remaining strawberries over.
  4. Scatter the seeds and almonds, finish with a thread of honey.
A frozen banana is the trick to a thick, ice-cream-textured bowl without watering it down with ice. Keep peeled, halved bananas in the freezer for exactly this.
Lunch
Whipped feta, white bean & roasted tomato toast
~400 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g feta
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 150g cherry tomatoes
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Handful basil, ½ garlic clove
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Roast the cherry tomatoes with a little oil and the garlic, 15 mins, until bursting.
  2. Blitz (or mash hard) the feta with the yoghurt and lemon until whipped and smooth.
  3. Warm the beans through in the tomato pan, crushing a few for texture.
  4. Spread the whipped feta thickly on the toast, pile on the beans and roast tomatoes, tear basil over, finish with pepper.
Whipping feta with yoghurt turns a crumbly cheese into a luxurious spread in 30 seconds. Roasting concentrates the tomatoes' sweetness — worth the oven time even for a quick lunch.
Dinner
Quorn bolognese with spaghetti
~500 kcal · 28g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g Quorn mince
  • 70g spaghetti (dry)
  • ½ onion, 1 carrot, 1 celery stick, all finely diced
  • 1 garlic clove, chopped
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes, 1 tbsp tomato purée
  • 150ml vegetable stock, 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 bay leaf
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Soften the onion, carrot and celery in the oil over medium-low, 8 mins — this soffritto is the flavour base, don't rush it.
  2. Add garlic and Quorn mince, fry 3 mins. Stir in the purée, 1 min.
  3. Add tomatoes, stock, oregano and bay. Simmer gently 15–20 mins until thick.
  4. Cook the spaghetti, drain, keeping a splash of water. Toss the pasta through the sauce with the reserved water.
  5. Serve with parmesan and black pepper.
Quorn mince needs less time than meat, but the soffritto and a proper simmer still matter — that's where the depth comes from. A splash of pasta water helps the sauce cling to every strand.

Summer berry granola & yoghurt pot
~350 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 30g rolled oats
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds, 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 75g blueberries
  • 75g strawberries, sliced
  • 2 tsp honey, pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Toast the oats, seeds and almonds in a dry pan over medium heat, 4 mins, until golden. Stir in 1 tsp honey and the cinnamon, tip out to cool into clusters.
  2. Spoon half the yoghurt into a glass, add half the berries.
  3. Repeat the layers, finishing with the granola clusters on top.
  4. Thread the remaining honey over just before eating so it stays crunchy.
A two-minute pan granola beats the bagged stuff and has no hidden sugar. Keep the clusters off the yoghurt until serving — granola goes soft the moment it sits in liquid.
Lunch
Pea, broad bean & feta smash on sourdough
~400 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 75g frozen petits pois
  • 100g frozen broad beans
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • Zest of ½ lemon, 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Pinch of chilli flakes, black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the peas and broad beans 3 mins. Drain, cool slightly, and pop the broad beans out of their grey skins.
  2. Roughly crush them with the oil, lemon zest, mint and most of the feta — keep it chunky.
  3. Pile onto the toast.
  4. Scatter the remaining feta, chilli flakes and a good grind of pepper over the top.
Double-podding the broad beans takes two minutes and turns them vivid green and sweet — both peas and broad beans are at their seasonal best now. Keep the smash coarse for texture.
Dinner
Stuffed courgettes with rice, tomato & mozzarella
~470 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 courgettes, halved lengthways
  • 60g rice (dry), cooked
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, handful basil
  • 15g vegetarian parmesan, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Scoop the seedy centres from the courgette halves and roughly chop the flesh.
  2. Soften the onion in oil, 5 mins, add garlic, chopped courgette flesh and tomatoes, cook 5 mins to a chunky sauce. Stir through the cooked rice and oregano.
  3. Sit the courgette shells in a dish, pile in the filling.
  4. Top with torn mozzarella and parmesan, roast 20–25 mins until the courgettes are tender and the cheese is golden. Tear basil over.
Courgettes are everywhere and cheap right now — the bigger ones are perfect for stuffing. Don't waste the scooped flesh; chopping it back into the sauce keeps the dish economical.

Breakfast
Shakshuka with crusty bread
~370 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • ½ onion, sliced; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 30g feta, crumbled
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil, 8 mins. Add garlic and spices, 1 min.
  2. Pour in the tomatoes, simmer 8 mins until thick and jammy. Season.
  3. Make two wells, crack in the eggs, cover and cook gently 5–6 mins until the whites set but the yolks stay soft.
  4. Crumble the feta over, scatter coriander, and serve from the pan with the bread for dunking.
Cover the pan once the eggs go in — the trapped steam sets the tops without overcooking the yolks. The sauce should be thick enough to hold the wells in place before you crack the eggs.
Lunch
Halloumi & roasted veg wrap with hummus
~410 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g halloumi, sliced
  • ½ courgette & ½ red pepper, sliced
  • 1 large tortilla wrap
  • 2 tbsp hummus
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Toss the courgette and pepper with the oil and oregano, roast at 200°C for 18 mins (or griddle), until soft and charred.
  2. Dry-fry the halloumi slices 2 mins per side until golden.
  3. Warm the wrap, spread with hummus, top with the roasted veg, halloumi and rocket.
  4. Squeeze over lemon, roll up tightly, and halve on the diagonal.
Spread the hummus right to the edges — it's the glue that stops the filling escaping. Cook the halloumi last so it's still warm and golden when the wrap is rolled.
Dinner
Chickpea & apricot tagine with couscous
~480 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 50g couscous (dry)
  • ½ onion, 1 carrot, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 4 dried apricots, chopped
  • 1 tsp ras el hanout (or ½ tsp cumin + ½ tsp cinnamon)
  • 200ml vegetable stock, 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, fresh coriander, flaked almonds
Method
  1. Soften the onion and carrot in the oil, 6 mins. Add garlic and spices, 1 min.
  2. Add chickpeas, tomatoes, apricots and stock. Simmer 15 mins until thick and the carrot is tender.
  3. Pour just-boiled water over the couscous to cover, lid on 5 mins, then fluff with a fork.
  4. Spoon the tagine over the couscous, swirl on yoghurt, scatter coriander and almonds.
Dried apricots melt into the sauce and give that signature sweet-savoury balance — a little goes a long way. A spoon of cold yoghurt at the end cuts the richness perfectly.

Breakfast
Courgette & cheddar fritters with poached egg
~360 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 small courgette, coarsely grated
  • 1 large egg (for the batter) + 1 to poach
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 tbsp plain flour, 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Handful rocket to serve
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, splash of vinegar for poaching
  • Sea salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Squeeze the grated courgette dry in a cloth. Mix with one egg, the cheddar, flour and spring onions; season.
  2. Fry heaped spoonfuls in the oil, 2–3 mins per side, until golden and set.
  3. Poach the second egg in barely-simmering water with a splash of vinegar, 3 mins.
  4. Stack the fritters, sit the poached egg on top, add rocket and a grind of pepper.
Wring the courgette hard or the batter won't hold — the same rule as the Monday frittata. A lazy Sunday is the time for a poached egg; the broken yolk becomes the sauce.
Lunch
Tomato & white bean bruschetta salad
~400 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 vine tomatoes, chunked
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted and rubbed with garlic
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Big handful basil
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp balsamic vinegar, black pepper
Method
  1. Salt the tomato chunks and leave 10 mins to release their juice.
  2. Add the beans, onion, oil and balsamic, and toss through the tomatoes and their juice.
  3. Tear in the mozzarella and most of the basil.
  4. Cut the garlic-rubbed toast into chunks, fold through, top with the rest of the basil and a grind of pepper.
Rubbing the warm toast with a cut garlic clove is a one-second trick that perfumes the whole salad. Like panzanella, the tomato juice is the dressing — let it run.
Dinner
Spinach & ricotta cannelloni
~490 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 dried cannelloni tubes (or 4 fresh lasagne sheets, rolled)
  • 200g baby spinach, wilted and squeezed dry
  • 100g ricotta
  • 25g vegetarian parmesan, grated; pinch of nutmeg
  • Sauce: 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes, ½ onion, 1 garlic clove, 1 tsp oregano
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, fresh basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Soften the onion in oil, add garlic, tomatoes and oregano, simmer 10 mins for a quick sauce.
  2. Mix the squeezed spinach with the ricotta, half the parmesan and the nutmeg. Season.
  3. Spoon the filling into the cannelloni tubes (a piping bag or freezer bag with the corner snipped makes this easy).
  4. Spread half the sauce in a dish, lay the tubes on top, cover with the rest of the sauce.
  5. Scatter cheddar and the remaining parmesan, bake 30 mins until bubbling and golden. Tear basil over.
A freezer bag with a snipped corner pipes the filling cleanly into the tubes — far less fiddly than a teaspoon. Squeezing the spinach properly dry keeps the filling firm, not watery.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

  • Cottage cheese 300g
  • Mozzarella 1 ball (125g)
  • Extra mature cheddar +50g (150g total)
  • Creatine monohydrate (optional) 300g tub, 3–5g/day

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
