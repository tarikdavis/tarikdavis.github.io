Breakfast
Blueberry, banana & flaxseed yoghurt bowl
~350 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 150g blueberries
  • 1 small banana
  • 30g rolled oats
  • 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Toast the oats and seeds in a dry pan for 3–4 mins until they smell nutty. Cool.
  2. Mash the banana into the yoghurt with the flaxseed and honey.
  3. Crush a third of the blueberries with the back of a fork and ripple them through.
  4. Spoon into a bowl and top with the remaining berries and the toasted oats and seeds.
Ground flaxseed only gives up its omega-3s once the seed coat is broken — whole seeds pass straight through. Buy it ground, and keep it in the fridge.
Lunch
Broad bean, courgette & ricotta toast
~410 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g podded broad beans (about 400g in the pod)
  • ½ courgette, shaved into ribbons
  • 80g ricotta
  • 2 thick slices sourdough
  • Zest and juice of ½ lemon
  • Small handful mint, torn
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the broad beans 3 mins, drain, and slip them out of their grey skins — worth every second.
  2. Beat the ricotta with the lemon zest, a pinch of salt and plenty of pepper.
  3. Toss the beans and courgette ribbons with the oil, lemon juice and mint.
  4. Toast the sourdough, spread thickly with the ricotta, and pile the beans on top.
Double-podding broad beans is the difference between bitter and buttery — late-July beans are big enough that the skins have turned tough.
Dinner
Puy lentil & roasted tomato ragù with spaghetti
~490 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 250g pouch cooked Puy lentils
  • 250g cherry tomatoes
  • 70g spaghetti (dry)
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 carrot, finely diced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, pinch chilli flakes
  • 15g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Roast the cherry tomatoes with half the oil for 20 mins until collapsed and jammy.
  2. Meanwhile soften the onion and carrot in the rest of the oil, 8 mins. Add the garlic, oregano and chilli, 1 min.
  3. Tip in the roasted tomatoes with all their juices and the lentils; simmer 8 mins, crushing the tomatoes as you go.
  4. Cook the spaghetti, then toss it through the ragù with a splash of pasta water. Finish with the cheese and basil.
That splash of starchy pasta water is what binds sauce to strand — without it the ragù sits in a puddle at the bottom of the bowl.

Breakfast
Soft scrambled eggs with chives on sourdough
~360 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp whole milk
  • 1 tsp butter
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • Few chives, snipped
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Whisk the eggs with the milk and a pinch of salt.
  2. Melt the butter in a cold non-stick pan, add the eggs, and set it over low heat.
  3. Stir slowly and constantly with a spatula, dragging the curds off the base. Take it off the heat while it still looks slightly underdone.
  4. Pile onto the toast and finish with the chives and pepper.
Low and slow is the whole trick — high heat squeezes water out of the curds and you get scrambled eggs sitting in a puddle. Off the heat 10 seconds early; the pan finishes them.
Lunch
Sweetcorn, black bean & avocado salad bowl
~430 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • ½ avocado, diced
  • 1 large vine tomato, chunked
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Handful coriander
  • Juice of ½ lime, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Little gem leaves
Method
  1. Char the sweetcorn in a dry, very hot pan for 4–5 mins, shaking now and then, until spots of it blacken.
  2. Whisk the lime juice, cumin and oil with a good pinch of salt.
  3. Toss the warm corn with the beans, tomato and onion in the dressing and leave 5 mins.
  4. Fold through the avocado and coriander at the last moment and serve on the little gem leaves.
Charring the corn in a dry pan gives you the smoky edge of a barbecue with none of the setup — and dressing it while warm means the beans drink up the lime.
Dinner
Halloumi & courgette skewers with couscous tabbouleh
~500 kcal · 26g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 110g halloumi, in thick cubes
  • 1 courgette, in thick rounds
  • ½ red pepper, chunked
  • 50g couscous (dry)
  • Big handful flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • 1 large vine tomato, finely diced
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
Method
  1. Cover the couscous with 75ml boiling water, lid on, 5 mins. Fluff with a fork and cool.
  2. Thread the halloumi, courgette and pepper onto skewers, brush with half the oil and the oregano.
  3. Griddle or grill 8–10 mins, turning, until the halloumi is deeply golden and the courgette is soft.
  4. Stir the herbs, tomato, spring onions, remaining oil and the lemon through the couscous — it should be more herb than grain. Serve the skewers on top.
Real tabbouleh is a herb salad with a little grain in it, not the reverse. Be braver with the parsley than feels sensible.

Breakfast
Baked eggs with peppers & tomato
~370 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ¼ onion, sliced; 1 garlic clove
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, ¼ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil in a small frying pan, 8 mins, until the pepper is properly slumped.
  2. Add the garlic and spices, 1 min, then the tomatoes. Simmer 8 mins until thick enough to hold a trench.
  3. Make two wells, crack in the eggs, cover the pan and cook 5–6 mins until the whites set but the yolks still wobble.
  4. Scatter with coriander and serve with the sourdough for mopping.
The sauce has to be thick before the eggs go in — if it's still loose they sink and poach into it instead of sitting proudly on top.
Lunch
Tomato, white bean & basil panzanella
~430 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large ripe vine tomatoes, chunked
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 2 thick slices day-old sourdough, torn into chunks
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • Big handful basil
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 garlic clove, halved
Method
  1. Salt the tomatoes generously in a bowl and leave 15 mins — you want a pool of juice at the bottom.
  2. Toast the torn bread in a dry pan or hot oven until crisp at the edges but chewy inside, then rub with the cut garlic.
  3. Add the bread to the tomatoes with the beans, cucumber, onion, oil and vinegar. Toss and leave 10 mins.
  4. Fold the basil through just before eating.
Panzanella was invented to rescue stale bread — the tomato juice is the dressing. Fresh soft bread turns to mush, so use yesterday's loaf and toast it first.
Dinner
Green curry with tofu & French beans
~490 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g extra-firm tofu, cubed and patted dry
  • 100g French beans, topped and halved
  • ½ red pepper, sliced
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 1½ tbsp green curry paste (check it's vegetarian)
  • 150ml coconut milk
  • 1 tsp soy sauce, squeeze of lime
  • Handful Thai or regular basil
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest off the heat.
  2. Fry the tofu in the oil over high heat until golden on several sides, 6–8 mins. Set aside.
  3. Fry the curry paste in the same pan 1 min until it smells fragrant, then pour in the coconut milk and bring to a simmer.
  4. Add the beans and pepper, 4 mins, then return the tofu with the soy. Finish with lime and basil, over the rice.
Frying the paste before the liquid goes in is non-negotiable — it blooms the spices in the oil. Skip it and the curry tastes flat and raw no matter how long it simmers.

Breakfast
Nectarine & toasted seed yoghurt pot
~350 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 200g Greek yoghurt
  • 1 ripe nectarine, sliced
  • 2 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Toast the seeds and almonds in a dry pan with the cinnamon, 3 mins, until they start to pop.
  2. Off the heat, stir in the honey so it coats them into brittle clusters. Cool 2 mins.
  3. Spoon the yoghurt into a bowl or jar.
  4. Layer over the nectarine slices and scatter the clusters on top.
A nectarine that's hard at the shop will soften on the windowsill but never sweeten — sugar stops developing the moment it's picked. Buy fragrant ones.
Lunch
Romesco with charred French beans & egg
~420 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g French beans
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 red pepper
  • 30g blanched almonds
  • 1 slice sourdough, torn
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 wholemeal pitta
Method
  1. Grill or roast the pepper at 220°C for 20 mins until blistered. Steam it in a covered bowl 5 mins, then slip off the skin.
  2. Toast the almonds and the torn sourdough in a dry pan until golden.
  3. Blitz the pepper, almonds, bread, garlic, vinegar, paprika and oil to a coarse, rubbly sauce. Season boldly.
  4. Boil the egg 7 mins. Char the beans in a hot dry pan 4 mins. Spoon the romesco onto a plate, pile the beans over, halve the egg on top, and serve with the pitta.
Romesco should be rubbly, not smooth — the almonds and bread are there for texture. Stop the blender while it still has some grit. It keeps a week in the fridge.
Dinner
Mushroom & pea risotto
~500 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g arborio rice
  • 200g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • 80g frozen peas
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 500ml hot vegetable stock
  • 25g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 1 tsp butter
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon, handful parsley
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in a hot dry pan until golden and squeaky, 5 mins. Season and set aside.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, 6 mins. Add the garlic, then the rice, stirring 1 min until the grains look glassy at the edges.
  3. Add the hot stock a ladle at a time, stirring, waiting for each to be absorbed — about 18 mins.
  4. Stir in the peas and mushrooms for the last 2 mins, then beat in the butter, cheese and lemon off the heat. Rest 2 mins; it should slump, not stand.
That final beating off the heat — the mantecatura — is what makes risotto creamy without any cream. Rest it two minutes and it loosens to exactly the right consistency.

Breakfast
Cinnamon French toast with strawberries
~390 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 1 large egg
  • 60ml whole milk
  • ½ tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 150g strawberries, halved
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp butter
  • 1 tsp honey
Method
  1. Whisk the egg, milk, cinnamon and vanilla in a shallow dish with a pinch of salt.
  2. Soak the bread 30 secs a side — long enough to drink, not so long it collapses.
  3. Fry gently in the butter over medium-low heat, 2–3 mins a side, until set through and deep gold.
  4. Toss the strawberries with the honey and serve over the toast with the yoghurt.
The pinch of salt in the custard is what stops French toast tasting like sweet wet bread. And medium-low heat cooks the middle before the outside burns.
Lunch
Gazpacho with goat's cheese & seed toast
~400 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 ripe large vine tomatoes
  • ½ cucumber, peeled
  • ½ red pepper
  • ¼ red onion
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • 1 slice stale sourdough, torn
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1 slice sourdough + 40g soft goat's cheese
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. Blitz the tomatoes, cucumber, pepper, onion, garlic and torn bread until completely smooth — give it a full 2 minutes.
  2. With the motor running, pour in the oil so it emulsifies and the soup turns coral-pink rather than red.
  3. Add the vinegar, season hard with salt, and chill at least an hour. Taste again cold — it will need more salt than you think.
  4. Toast the sourdough, spread with the goat's cheese, scatter with the seeds, and serve alongside.
Cold blunts seasoning, so a gazpacho that tastes right at room temperature will taste of nothing from the fridge. Salt it until it's almost too much, then chill.
Dinner
Spinach & feta filo pie with tomato salad
~500 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 sheets filo pastry
  • 200g baby spinach
  • 80g feta, crumbled
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Small handful mint and dill, chopped
  • Grating of nutmeg
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 2 vine tomatoes + ¼ red onion, sliced, for the salad
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
Method
  1. Wilt the spinach in a dry pan, then — crucially — squeeze it bone dry in a tea towel and chop it.
  2. Mix with the feta, egg, spring onions, herbs, nutmeg and plenty of pepper.
  3. Brush each filo sheet with oil and layer into a small tin, letting the edges overhang. Add the filling and scrunch the overhang over the top.
  4. Bake at 190°C for 25–30 mins until deep gold and crisp. Serve with the tomatoes and onion dressed in the vinegar.
Wet spinach is what turns filo into a soggy raft. Squeeze it until you're sure there's nothing left, then squeeze again — you'll be surprised.

Breakfast
Garlic mushrooms on toast with poached egg
~370 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 large egg
  • 200g chestnut mushrooms, torn
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 15g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • Small handful parsley
  • 1 tsp butter, ½ tbsp olive oil
  • Splash of vinegar (for poaching)
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in a hot dry pan 5 mins until golden, then add the oil, butter and garlic for 1 min more.
  2. Bring a pan of water with the vinegar to a bare simmer. Slide in the egg from a cup and poach 3 mins.
  3. Toast the sourdough and pile the mushrooms over.
  4. Sit the egg on top and finish with the cheese, parsley and plenty of pepper.
Tear mushrooms rather than slice them — the ragged surfaces catch more heat and brown far better than a clean cut face.
Lunch
Tomato, mozzarella & basil salad with sourdough
~430 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large ripe vine tomatoes, in thick slices
  • 1 ball mozzarella (125g), torn
  • Big handful basil
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
  • Flaky salt, black pepper
Method
  1. Slice the tomatoes thickly, lay them on a plate, and salt them 10 mins ahead.
  2. Tear the mozzarella over — torn edges hold dressing where sliced ones shed it.
  3. Tuck the basil leaves in whole, not chopped.
  4. Pour over the oil and balsamic, add flaky salt and pepper, and serve with the sourdough to catch the juices.
Never refrigerate a tomato you're about to eat raw — cold destroys the aromatic compounds that make it taste of tomato, and the damage doesn't reverse.
Dinner
Stuffed peppers with rice, feta & pine nuts
~490 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 red peppers, halved lengthways, seeds out
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • 1 tbsp pine nuts
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 courgette, finely diced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, pinch chilli flakes
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful parsley
  • Handful rocket to serve
Method
  1. Cook the rice, 11 mins, and drain. Toast the pine nuts in a dry pan until golden — watch them, they turn in seconds.
  2. Soften the onion and courgette in the oil, 8 mins. Add the garlic, oregano and chilli, 1 min.
  3. Stir through the rice, half the feta, the pine nuts and the parsley. Season.
  4. Pack into the pepper halves, top with the rest of the feta, and bake at 190°C for 30 mins until the peppers slump and blister. Serve with the rocket.
Peppers hold their shape best halved lengthways through the stalk — the stalk acts as a buttress. Whole ones tend to topple and spill.

Breakfast
Cottage cheese pancakes with blackcurrant compote
~380 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g cottage cheese
  • 1 large egg
  • 40g rolled oats, blitzed to a rough flour
  • ½ tsp baking powder, ½ tsp vanilla
  • 150g blackcurrants
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Simmer the blackcurrants with the honey and 1 tbsp water for 5 mins until they burst into a loose compote. Set aside.
  2. Blitz the cottage cheese, egg, oat flour, baking powder and vanilla to a thick batter.
  3. Fry spoonfuls in the butter over medium-low heat, 2 mins a side, until risen and golden — you'll get 4 or 5.
  4. Stack and spoon the compote over.
Cottage cheese pancakes are the highest-protein pancake there is, and blitzing them means you never taste the curds. Blackcurrants are only around for a few July weeks — freeze any spare.
Lunch
Sweetcorn & red pepper quesadilla with salsa
~430 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 tortilla wraps
  • 100g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • ½ red pepper, diced
  • 50g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • Salsa: 1 vine tomato diced, ¼ red onion, coriander, juice of ½ lime
  • Pinch chilli flakes
  • ½ tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
Method
  1. Char the sweetcorn and pepper in a hot dry pan 4 mins. Tip into a bowl with the cheese, spring onions and chilli.
  2. Stir the salsa together and season with salt and the lime.
  3. Spread the filling over one wrap, top with the second, and press.
  4. Cook in the oiled pan over medium heat, 3 mins a side, pressing with a spatula, until crisp and the cheese has welded it shut. Cut into wedges and serve with the salsa and yoghurt.
Press it firmly while the cheese is molten — that's what glues the two tortillas together. Cut it before the cheese sets and the whole thing unpacks itself.
Dinner
Courgette, tomato & butter bean gratin
~490 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 1 courgette, in thin rounds
  • 3 large vine tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 30g breadcrumbs (blitzed from sourdough)
  • 20g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Handful basil
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Warm the garlic in 1 tbsp of the oil 30 secs, then stir through the butter beans with the oregano and a splash of water; crush a third of them lightly.
  2. Spread the beans in a small baking dish and layer the courgette and tomato over the top, overlapping like roof tiles.
  3. Mix the breadcrumbs, cheese and remaining oil and scatter over.
  4. Bake at 200°C for 30–35 mins until the crumb is bronzed and the vegetables have shrunk into each other. Rest 5 mins, then finish with basil.
Crushing a third of the beans gives you a creamy bed underneath without a drop of cream — the whole ones on top keep their shape and bite.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

  • Extra free range eggs +6 (18 total)
  • Extra Greek yoghurt +500g (1.5kg total)
  • Extra cottage cheese +300g (600g total)
  • Extra feta +100g (300g total)
  • Extra tofu +1 block (2 total)
  • Extra hard cheese +100g (200g total)
  • Creatine monohydrate (optional) 300g tub, 3–5g/day

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
