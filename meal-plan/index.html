Breakfast
Strawberry & vanilla overnight oats
~350 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 150ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 150g strawberries, hulled and sliced
  • ½ tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp honey, squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
Method
  1. The night before: stir the oats, milk, vanilla and half the yoghurt together in a jar. Refrigerate.
  2. In the morning, toss the strawberries with the honey and a squeeze of lemon; leave 10 mins to macerate.
  3. Loosen the oats with a splash more milk if needed and top with the remaining yoghurt.
  4. Spoon over the strawberries and all their syrupy juices, and scatter with the seeds.
British strawberries are at their July peak — macerating them with honey and lemon draws out a glossy syrup, so you need no extra sugar at all.
Lunch
Broad bean, pea & ricotta smash on toast
~410 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g podded broad beans (about 400g in pods)
  • 50g frozen peas
  • 75g ricotta
  • 1 thick slice sourdough
  • Small handful mint, chopped
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch of chilli flakes, black pepper
Method
  1. Blanch the broad beans and peas in boiling water, 3 mins. Drain, cool briefly, and slip the pale skins off at least half the broad beans.
  2. Roughly crush the beans and peas with a fork with the mint, oil and lemon; season well.
  3. Toast the sourdough and spread thickly with the ricotta.
  4. Pile the smash on top and finish with chilli flakes and a thread of oil.
Double-podding is worth the two minutes — the bright-green inner bean is sweeter and creamier than its grey jacket. Keep it a rough crush, not a purée; the texture is the point.
Dinner
Quorn keema with peas & rice
~490 kcal · 30g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 150g Quorn mince
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cumin, ¼ tsp turmeric, pinch chilli
  • 100g (¼ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 75g frozen peas
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt, fresh coriander
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins. Rest.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, 5 mins. Add garlic, ginger and the spices, 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Stir in the Quorn mince, tomatoes and a good splash of water. Simmer 8 mins until thick and clinging.
  4. Add the peas for the last 3 mins. Season, swirl through the yoghurt, and finish with coriander over the rice.
Quorn takes up spice fast and doesn't need browning — straight into the sauce keeps it juicy. The peas going in at the very end keep their sweetness and pop.

Breakfast
Scrambled eggs & chives with grilled tomatoes
~350 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, toasted
  • 6 cherry tomatoes, on the vine if you can
  • 1 tsp butter, splash of milk
  • Few chives, snipped
  • Black pepper, pinch of salt
Method
  1. Grill the tomatoes under a hot grill, 6–8 mins, until soft and lightly blistered.
  2. Whisk the eggs with the milk and a pinch of salt.
  3. Melt the butter over medium-low heat, add the eggs, and fold slowly with a spatula until barely set.
  4. Pile onto the toast while still glossy, top with chives and pepper, and add the tomatoes.
Take scrambled eggs off the heat when they still look slightly underdone — they finish cooking on the toast. Low and slow is the whole secret.
Lunch
Tuscan panzanella with cannellini beans
~410 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large ripe vine tomatoes, chunked
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 1 thick slice sourdough (slightly stale is best), torn
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • ¼ cucumber, chunked
  • Big handful basil, torn
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp red wine vinegar, ½ garlic clove, grated
Method
  1. Salt the tomato chunks and leave 10 mins so they release their juices — that juice is the dressing's backbone.
  2. Toast the torn bread in a dry pan (or low oven) until golden and dry.
  3. Whisk the oil, vinegar and garlic into the tomato juices.
  4. Toss everything together and rest 10 mins so the bread drinks the dressing. Fold the basil through last.
Panzanella improves as it sits — the 10-minute rest is when stale bread turns into the best part of the salad. July vine tomatoes need almost nothing else.
Dinner
Halloumi & summer veg traybake with lemon couscous
~490 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g halloumi, thickly sliced
  • 1 courgette, chunked; 1 red pepper, chunked
  • ½ red onion, wedged
  • 150g cherry tomatoes
  • 60g couscous
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • Lemon (zest & juice), small handful mint
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Toss the courgette, pepper and onion with the oil and oregano; roast 20 mins.
  2. Add the halloumi slices and cherry tomatoes and roast 10–12 mins more, until the cheese is golden at the edges.
  3. Meanwhile soak the couscous in 90ml boiling water with the lemon zest, covered, 5 mins. Fluff with a fork.
  4. Pile the traybake over the couscous, squeeze over the lemon, and scatter with mint.
Add the halloumi for only the last 10 minutes — it turns golden and squidgy rather than tough and squeaky. The tomatoes burst into a ready-made dressing.

Breakfast
Avocado & cottage cheese on rye with seeds
~350 kcal · 17g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 slices rye bread, toasted
  • ½ ripe avocado
  • 100g cottage cheese
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds, toasted
  • Squeeze of lemon, pinch of chilli flakes
  • Few chives, snipped
Method
  1. Toast the seeds in a dry pan, 2 mins, until they start to pop. Set aside.
  2. Mash the avocado roughly with the lemon and a pinch of salt.
  3. Spread the cottage cheese over the toasted rye, then pile the avocado on top.
  4. Finish with the seeds, chilli flakes and chives.
Putting the cottage cheese underneath the avocado is a quiet protein upgrade to avocado toast — creamy where it counts, and it stops the toast going soggy.
Lunch
Egg mayo & watercress open sandwich
~400 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp mayonnaise, 1 tsp Dijon
  • 2 slices rye bread (or 1 thick slice sourdough)
  • Big handful watercress
  • 4 radishes, thinly sliced
  • Few chives, snipped; black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the eggs 8 mins, then cool in cold water and peel.
  2. Roughly chop and fold with the mayonnaise, Dijon, chives and plenty of pepper.
  3. Pile onto the bread with the watercress underneath and over the top.
  4. Scatter with the radishes for crunch.
Eight minutes gives a just-set yolk that keeps the mayo mixture rich rather than dry. Peppery watercress does the work a pinch of mustard powder used to.
Dinner
Summer minestrone with pesto
~470 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin cannellini beans, drained
  • 40g small soup pasta (ditalini or similar)
  • 1 carrot, diced; ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 courgette, diced; 100g runner beans, sliced
  • 100g (¼ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 500ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tbsp green pesto (vegetarian), 20g Italian-style hard cheese
  • 1 slice sourdough, 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Soften the onion and carrot in the oil, 6 mins. Add the garlic, 1 min.
  2. Add the tomatoes and stock; simmer 10 mins.
  3. Add the pasta and runner beans, 8–9 mins, then the cannellini and courgette for the final 4 mins.
  4. Season, swirl the pesto through each bowl, and finish with the grated cheese and bread for dunking.
Courgette only needs the last few minutes or it slumps. Check the pesto label — traditional pesto uses non-vegetarian parmesan, so look for one marked vegetarian.

Breakfast
Cinnamon French toast with strawberries
~370 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 thick slice sourdough
  • 1 large egg, 60ml whole milk
  • ½ tsp cinnamon, ½ tsp vanilla
  • 100g strawberries, sliced
  • 50g Greek yoghurt, 1 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Whisk the egg, milk, cinnamon and vanilla in a shallow dish.
  2. Soak the bread 30 seconds per side — it should be saturated but not falling apart.
  3. Fry in the butter over medium heat, 2–3 mins per side, until deep golden.
  4. Top with the yoghurt, strawberries and a thread of honey.
Slightly stale bread is actually better here — it soaks up the custard without collapsing. Medium heat, not high: you want the middle set before the outside catches.
Lunch
Gazpacho with a cheddar toastie
~420 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 300g ripe vine tomatoes, chopped
  • ¼ cucumber, ½ red pepper, chopped
  • ½ garlic clove
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, 2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 slices sourdough + 40g mature cheddar
  • Few basil leaves, black pepper
Method
  1. Blitz the tomatoes, cucumber, pepper, garlic, oil and vinegar until smooth.
  2. Season boldly and chill 20 mins (or add an ice cube or two).
  3. Make the toastie: cheddar between the bread, toasted in a dry pan under a weight until molten.
  4. Pour the gazpacho into a bowl, top with basil and a thread of oil, and dunk.
Cold mutes flavour, so gazpacho needs more salt and vinegar than seems sensible at room temperature. Taste it again once chilled and adjust.
Dinner
Egg-fried rice with edamame & sweetcorn
~480 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g basmati rice (dry), cooked and fully cooled
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 100g frozen edamame
  • 75g frozen sweetcorn
  • 1 carrot, finely diced; 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 1 garlic clove & thumb of ginger, grated
  • 1½ tbsp soy sauce, ½ lime
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
Method
  1. Cook the rice ahead and cool it completely — cold rice is what fries properly.
  2. Stir-fry the carrot in the oil over high heat, 2 mins. Add garlic and ginger, 30 secs.
  3. Add the edamame and sweetcorn straight from frozen, 2 mins, then the rice, tossing until piping hot.
  4. Push everything to one side, scramble the eggs in the gap, then fold through with the soy, spring onions and a squeeze of lime.
Day-old-style cold rice keeps its grains separate; warm fresh rice turns gummy. Cook it in the morning, spread it on a plate, and it's perfect by dinner.

Breakfast
Blueberry oat pancakes
~360 kcal · 15g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 40g rolled oats, blitzed to a rough flour (or 40g plain flour)
  • 1 large egg
  • 75ml whole milk, 50g Greek yoghurt
  • ½ tsp baking powder, ½ tsp vanilla
  • 100g blueberries
  • 1 tsp honey, 1 tsp butter
Method
  1. Whisk the oat flour, egg, milk, half the yoghurt, baking powder and vanilla into a thick batter. Rest 5 mins.
  2. Melt a little butter over medium heat and drop in small rounds of batter.
  3. Press a few blueberries into each pancake as the top sets; flip and cook 2 mins more.
  4. Stack with the remaining yoghurt, the rest of the berries and a thread of honey.
Drop the berries onto the pancakes in the pan rather than stirring them through the bowl — the batter stays unbruised and each berry turns jammy where it hits the heat.
Lunch
Puy lentil tabbouleh with feta
~420 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 250g pouch cooked Puy lentils
  • Big handful flat-leaf parsley + small handful mint, chopped
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, chopped
  • ¼ cucumber, finely diced; 1 spring onion, sliced
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, juice of ½ lemon, black pepper
Method
  1. Tip the lentils into a bowl and fluff apart with a fork.
  2. Chop the herbs generously — they're a salad ingredient here, not a garnish.
  3. Toss the lentils with the tomatoes, cucumber, spring onion, herbs, oil and lemon; season.
  4. Crumble the feta over the top.
Real tabbouleh is mostly herbs — be braver with the parsley than feels natural. Ready-cooked Puy lentils hold their shape and make this a five-minute lunch.
Dinner
Gnocchi, tomato & mozzarella bake
~500 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 300g fresh gnocchi
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 courgette, diced; ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 15g Italian-style hard cheese, grated
  • Handful basil, 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
Method
  1. Heat the grill (or oven to 200°C). Soften the onion in the oil, 5 mins; add the garlic and courgette, 4 mins more.
  2. Add the tomatoes and oregano; simmer 8 mins to a thick sauce and season.
  3. Boil the gnocchi 2 mins — slightly under — drain, and fold into the sauce in an ovenproof dish.
  4. Top with the mozzarella and hard cheese; grill 12–15 mins until blistered and bubbling. Finish with basil.
Pull the gnocchi out of the water a touch early — they finish cooking in the sauce and stay pillowy instead of bloated. A blistered, patchy-brown top is what you're after.

Breakfast
Shakshuka-style baked eggs
~360 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • ½ red pepper, sliced; ¼ onion, sliced
  • ½ tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp smoked paprika, pinch chilli
  • 1 wholemeal pitta, toasted
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, few coriander leaves
Method
  1. Soften the pepper and onion in the oil, 6 mins. Add the spices, 30 secs.
  2. Add the tomatoes and simmer 8 mins until properly thick.
  3. Make two wells and crack in the eggs. Lid on, gentle heat, 5–6 mins for set whites and soft yolks.
  4. Scatter with coriander and scoop up with the toasted pitta.
Get the sauce genuinely thick before the eggs go in — watery sauce poaches them into rags. The lid is what sets the whites without hardening the yolks.
Lunch
Ploughman's plate
~430 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g mature cheddar, in chunks
  • 1 apple, sliced
  • 1 tbsp chutney
  • 1 thick slice sourdough, buttered
  • Little gem leaves, 4 radishes, few cherry tomatoes
  • 2 pickled onions (optional)
Method
  1. Take the cheddar out of the fridge 30 mins ahead — cold mutes it.
  2. Slice the apple and toss with a squeeze of lemon so it doesn't brown.
  3. Arrange everything on a board or plate: cheese, apple, salad, bread, chutney.
  4. Assemble each bite as you go — a bit of everything is the rule.
A ploughman's stands or falls on the cheese being at room temperature. Thirty minutes on the counter turns the same cheddar from waxy to full-flavoured.
Dinner
Broad bean & lemon linguine with ricotta
~490 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 70g linguine
  • 100g podded broad beans (about 250g in pods)
  • 50g frozen peas
  • 75g ricotta
  • Zest & juice of ½ lemon
  • Small handful mint, 15g Italian-style hard cheese
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, black pepper
Method
  1. Cook the linguine in well-salted water; add the broad beans and peas for the final 3 mins.
  2. Before draining, scoop out a mugful of the starchy pasta water.
  3. Beat the ricotta with the lemon zest, juice, oil and a splash of pasta water into a loose, silky sauce.
  4. Drain, toss everything together (loosening with more pasta water as needed), and finish with mint, hard cheese and pepper.
The starchy pasta water is the whole trick — it turns ricotta from a spoonable blob into a sauce that clings. Add it a splash at a time until glossy.

Halloumi & egg breakfast muffin
~380 kcal · 23g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 English muffin, split and toasted
  • 45g halloumi (2 slices)
  • 1 large egg
  • Handful rocket
  • 1 tsp butter, 1 tsp chutney

Method

Halloumi is the bacon of the vegetarian breakfast — get the pan properly hot and dry so it takes on a deep golden crust rather than sweating.

Watermelon, feta & mint salad
~390 kcal · 14g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 300g watermelon, chunked and chilled
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • ¼ cucumber, ribboned
  • Small handful mint, torn
  • 1 wholemeal pitta, toasted and torn
  • 1 tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lime, black pepper
Method
  1. Chill the watermelon well — this salad is best properly cold.

A little salt and pepper on watermelon sounds wrong and tastes right — it sharpens the sweetness against the salty feta. Dress at the last minute so nothing weeps.
Dinner
Stuffed romano peppers with herby bulgur & whipped feta
~480 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 romano peppers, halved lengthways and deseeded
  • 60g bulgur wheat
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, chopped; 1 spring onion, sliced
  • Handful parsley + small handful mint, chopped
  • 60g feta + 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds, toasted
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Rub the pepper halves with a little oil and roast 15 mins, cut-side up.
  2. Soak the bulgur in 120ml boiling stock or water, covered, 15 mins, then fluff.
  3. Fold the tomatoes, spring onion, herbs, lemon and remaining oil through the bulgur; season.
  4. Fill the peppers and return to the oven 10 mins. Mash the feta with the yoghurt until smooth and creamy; dollop over and finish with the toasted seeds.
Whipped feta is a 30-second upgrade — mashing it with yoghurt turns a salty crumble into a creamy sauce. Romano peppers are sweeter and thinner-walled than bell peppers, so they roast faster.

Build Muscle — Strength Track
An optional overlay on this week's plan for when you start working with a trainer. Higher protein, a modest calorie bump, and smart swaps that keep your shopping list almost the same.
Think of building muscle as progressive enhancement: keep the working baseline (this week's plan), layer protein in where it has the biggest payoff, and use feedback signals — energy, sleep, lift logs — to iterate. You're enhancing the plan, not rebuilding it.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
