Breakfast
Blueberry & almond baked oats
~400 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 50g rolled oats
  • 1 large egg
  • 100ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 100g blueberries
  • 1 tbsp flaked almonds
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 190°C. Whisk the oats, egg, milk, honey and cinnamon together in a bowl.
  2. Fold in most of the blueberries, tip into a small baking dish and scatter the rest of the berries and the almonds over the top.
  3. Bake 22–25 mins until set with a golden, springy top and the berries have burst into it.
  4. Let it settle a couple of minutes, then spoon the Greek yoghurt on top and finish with the seeds and a last thread of honey.
Baked oats give you a warm, cake-like breakfast from the same store-cupboard oats — and the whole dish reheats beautifully, so bake a double batch on Monday and you've got Tuesday sorted too.
Lunch
Roasted courgette, giant couscous & feta salad
~440 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g giant (pearl) couscous (dry)
  • 1 courgette, in half-moons
  • 60g feta, crumbled
  • Big handful rocket
  • ¼ red onion, finely sliced
  • 1 tbsp pine nuts
  • Small handful mint
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the courgette with a little oil and roast 20 mins until soft and golden at the edges.
  2. Toast the couscous in a dry pan 2 mins, then simmer in salted water 8 mins until tender. Drain and cool slightly.
  3. Toast the pine nuts in the same dry pan until golden, a minute, watching closely.
  4. Toss the couscous, roasted courgette, red onion and mint with the oil and lemon, fold in the rocket, and top with the feta and pine nuts.
Giant couscous holds a salad together far better than the fine kind — its little pearls stay separate and chewy rather than clumping, so this is just as good eaten cold from a lunchbox the next day.
Dinner
Chana masala with basmati rice
~500 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, 6 mins, then add the garlic, ginger and spices and fry 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Tip in the tomatoes and chickpeas with a splash of water, and simmer 12–15 mins, crushing a few chickpeas to thicken the sauce.
  4. Season well, stir through most of the coriander, and serve over the rice with the yoghurt spooned on top.
Crushing a handful of the chickpeas against the side of the pan is the trick to a thick, clingy sauce — the starch they release does the same job as flour or cream, with no extra ingredient.

Breakfast
Feta & cherry tomato baked eggs with sourdough
~385 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • Handful baby spinach
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • ½ tsp dried oregano
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Warm the oil in a small ovenproof pan and soften the garlic and tomatoes over medium heat, 5 mins, until they collapse into a sauce. Wilt the spinach through.
  2. Make two wells, crack an egg into each, and scatter the feta and oregano around them.
  3. Cover and cook gently 4–5 mins, or slide under a hot grill, until the whites are set but the yolks still soft.
  4. Season with pepper and eat straight from the pan with the toasted sourdough for dipping.
Cover the pan once the eggs go in — the trapped steam sets the tops of the whites without overcooking the yolks, giving you that glossy, just-runny finish without having to flip anything.
Lunch
Sweetcorn, black bean & avocado salad bowl
~430 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 100g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • 1 avocado, diced
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ¼ red onion, finely diced
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful coriander
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Char the sweetcorn in a dry pan over high heat until spots of it blacken, 4 mins, then tip into a bowl to cool.
  2. Whisk the lime juice, oil, a pinch of salt and the chilli into a sharp dressing.
  3. Add the black beans, tomatoes and red onion to the corn and toss in the dressing.
  4. Fold the avocado through last so it stays in neat pieces, and finish with the coriander.
Add the avocado at the very end and turn it gently — folded in early it mashes and greys the whole bowl, but added last it keeps its buttery green cubes intact.
Dinner
Red lentil dhal with rice & yoghurt
~495 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g red lentils, rinsed
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 2cm ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • Handful coriander
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Soften the onion in the oil, 6 mins, then add the garlic, ginger and spices for 1 min until fragrant.
  3. Stir in the lentils, tomatoes and 300ml water, and simmer 18–20 mins, stirring now and then, until the lentils collapse into a thick, soupy dhal.
  4. Season generously, stir in most of the coriander, and serve over the rice with the yoghurt swirled through.
Red lentils need no soaking and cook down in twenty minutes — season them only once they're soft, as salt added too early can keep pulses firm and stop them turning to that silky purée.

Breakfast
Peanut butter & banana breakfast smoothie
~395 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 ripe banana
  • 1 tbsp peanut butter
  • 200ml whole milk
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 25g rolled oats
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Pinch of cinnamon
  • A few ice cubes
Method
  1. Break the banana into a blender with the peanut butter, milk, yoghurt and oats.
  2. Add the honey, cinnamon and ice, and blend 30–40 secs until completely smooth and frothy.
  3. Check the thickness — add a splash more milk if you like it looser.
  4. Pour into a tall glass and drink straight away, while it's cold and thick.
The spoon of oats is what makes this a breakfast rather than a snack — they blend in invisibly but add slow-release energy and body, so it keeps you full through to lunch.
Lunch
Caprese pasta salad with mozzarella & basil
~445 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 75g fusilli (dry)
  • ½ ball mozzarella (60g), torn
  • 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Handful rocket
  • Handful basil
  • 1 tbsp green pesto
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Cook the fusilli in well-salted water until al dente, then drain and cool under cold running water so it stops cooking.
  2. Loosen the pesto with the oil and lemon into a dressing and toss the cooled pasta through it.
  3. Fold in the cherry tomatoes and torn mozzarella.
  4. Just before eating, stir through the rocket and basil so the leaves stay fresh and bright.
Rinse pasta for a cold salad under cold water the moment it's drained — it washes off the surface starch that would otherwise glue it into a solid clump as it cools, keeping every piece separate.
Dinner
Sweetcorn & courgette frittata with new potatoes
~505 kcal · 25g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 100g sweetcorn (frozen, defrosted)
  • ½ courgette, in half-moons
  • 30g mature cheddar, grated
  • 2 spring onions, sliced
  • 150g new potatoes
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful salad leaves
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the new potatoes 12 mins until just tender, drain, and slice into thick coins.
  2. Soften the courgette in the oil in an ovenproof pan, 5 mins, then add the potatoes and sweetcorn and spread them out flat.
  3. Beat the eggs with the spring onions, most of the cheese, salt and pepper, and pour over the veg. Cook gently 5 mins until the edges set.
  4. Scatter the last of the cheese over and finish under a hot grill 3–4 mins until puffed and golden. Serve in wedges with the leaves.
Finish a frittata under the grill rather than trying to flip it — the top sets into a golden, soufflé-like puff and there's no nerve-racking moment of turning a half-cooked egg cake out of the pan.

Breakfast
Scrambled eggs with feta, tomato & chives on toast
~385 kcal · 22g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 3 large eggs
  • 40g feta, crumbled
  • 1 large vine tomato, chopped
  • 2 spring onions (or chives), sliced
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tsp butter
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Beat the eggs with a pinch of salt and the spring onions.
  2. Melt the butter in a non-stick pan over low-medium heat, pour in the eggs and stir slowly, pushing them into soft folds as they set.
  3. When they're still a touch loose, take the pan off the heat and fold the feta and tomato through so the residual heat just warms them.
  4. Pile onto the toasted sourdough and finish with black pepper.
Scramble eggs low and slow, and pull them off the heat while they still look slightly underdone — they keep cooking in the hot pan, so this is what saves them from turning rubbery on the way to the plate.
Lunch
Halloumi & roasted pepper pitta with salad
~440 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 80g halloumi, sliced
  • 1 red pepper, sliced
  • 1 pitta bread
  • Handful rocket
  • ¼ cucumber, sliced
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Fry the pepper in the oil over high heat until soft and blackening at the edges, 6 mins, and lift out.
  2. Fry the halloumi in the same hot pan until deep gold on both sides, 3 mins.
  3. Stir the lemon and chilli into the yoghurt for a quick dressing, and warm the pitta.
  4. Split the pitta and fill with the rocket, cucumber, peppers and halloumi, then spoon the yoghurt dressing through.
Warm the pitta briefly before splitting it — a cold one tears and crumbles as you prise it open, but thirty seconds in the toaster or pan makes it supple enough to open into a neat, sturdy pocket.
Dinner
Butter bean, tomato & spinach stew with garlic toast
~490 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 1 × 400g tin butter beans, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • ½ onion, chopped; 2 garlic cloves
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika, pinch chilli flakes
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful parsley
Method
  1. Soften the onion in half the oil, 6 mins, then add one clove of garlic, the paprika and chilli for 1 min.
  2. Tip in the tomatoes and butter beans with a splash of water, and simmer 12 mins, crushing some beans to thicken the stew.
  3. Wilt the spinach through at the end and season well.
  4. Toast the sourdough, rub with the cut face of the remaining garlic clove and drizzle with the rest of the oil, then serve alongside for dunking, scattered with parsley.
Rubbing a raw garlic clove over hot toast is an old Italian trick — the rough, crisp surface grates the garlic to nothing, leaving a savoury edge that's far gentler than a spoonful of the chopped raw stuff.

Ricotta, blueberry & honey toast with seeds
~380 kcal · 16g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 100g ricotta
  • 100g blueberries
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch of cinnamon
Method
  1. Beat the ricotta with the lemon, cinnamon and half the honey until smooth and spreadable.
  2. Warm half the blueberries in a small pan for 2 mins until a few pop and turn syrupy.
  3. Toast the sourdough and spread the whipped ricotta over it thickly.
  4. Spoon over both the warm and fresh blueberries, scatter with the seeds, and finish with the last of the honey.
A squeeze of lemon and a quick whisk transform stodgy ricotta into something light and almost mousse-like — it spreads far more easily and tastes brighter against the sweet berries.
Lunch
Falafel & salad pitta with lemon-tahini yoghurt
~440 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 falafel
  • 1 pitta bread
  • Handful salad leaves
  • ¼ cucumber, sliced
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp tahini
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Pinch chilli flakes
Method
  1. Warm the falafel through in a hot oven or pan until crisp on the outside and hot in the middle, about 8 mins.
  2. Whisk the yoghurt, tahini, lemon and a splash of water into a loose, pourable dressing.
  3. Warm and split the pitta, and fill with the salad leaves, cucumber and tomatoes.
  4. Tuck in the falafel, spoon the tahini yoghurt over, and finish with the chilli flakes.
A spoon of tahini stirred into yoghurt makes an instant, nutty dressing that lifts any pitta or salad — loosen it with water a little at a time, as it thickens dramatically before it thins.
Dinner
Veggie chilli with rice & yoghurt
~505 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin red kidney beans, drained
  • ½ × 400g tin black beans, drained
  • 200g (½ tin) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 red pepper, diced
  • ½ onion, chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, ½ tsp smoked paprika, pinch chilli flakes
  • 60g basmati rice (dry)
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt; handful coriander
Method
  1. Cook the rinsed rice in salted water, lid on, 11 mins, then rest off the heat.
  2. Soften the onion and pepper in the oil, 6 mins, then add the garlic and spices for 1 min.

  4. Season well, and serve over the rice with the yoghurt and coriander on top.
Chilli is better the next day, so this makes a deliberate double portion's worth of flavour in one pot — the spices mellow and marry overnight, and it reheats into an even better lunch.

Breakfast
Poached egg, mushroom & spinach on toast
~390 kcal · 20g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 100g chestnut mushrooms, sliced
  • Large handful baby spinach
  • 1 large vine tomato, halved
  • 1 slice sourdough
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp white wine vinegar
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Fry the mushrooms in the oil over medium-high heat until golden, 5 mins, adding the tomato halves cut-side down alongside, then wilt the spinach through and season.
  2. Bring a small pan of water to a bare simmer with the vinegar, swirl it, and slip the eggs in one at a time. Poach 3 mins for soft yolks.
  3. Toast the sourdough and pile the mushrooms, spinach and tomato on top.
  4. Lift the eggs out with a slotted spoon, drain, sit them on the toast, and finish with plenty of pepper.
Get the poaching water to a gentle shimmer, not a rolling boil — a fierce boil tears the whites into ragged threads, whereas a barely-trembling pan lets them wrap neatly around the yolk.
Lunch
Egg mayo & cress sandwich with cucumber salad
~430 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 2 tbsp Greek yoghurt
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Handful cress
  • ¼ cucumber, sliced
  • 1 spring onion, sliced
  • Squeeze of lemon
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Boil the eggs 8 mins for just-set yolks, then cool under cold water, peel and roughly chop.
  2. Mix the chopped egg with the yoghurt, mustard, spring onion and plenty of salt and pepper.
  3. Pile onto one slice of bread, scatter the cress over, and top with the second slice.
  4. Dress the cucumber with the lemon and a pinch of salt, and serve alongside the sandwich, cut in half.
Cool boiled eggs quickly under cold running water the moment they're done — it stops that grey-green ring forming around the yolk and makes the shells slip off cleanly instead of taking chunks of white with them.
Dinner
Halloumi & chickpea burgers with oven wedges
~520 kcal · 24g protein▾
Ingredients
  • ½ × 400g tin chickpeas, drained
  • 80g halloumi, coarsely grated
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp breadcrumbs (from the loaf)
  • 2 spring onions, sliced; ½ tsp ground cumin
  • 200g potatoes, in wedges
  • 100g cherry tomatoes; handful rocket
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Squeeze of lemon
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the potato wedges with 1 tbsp oil and salt and roast 30 mins, turning once, until crisp and golden.
  2. Mash the chickpeas roughly, then mix in the halloumi, egg, breadcrumbs, spring onions and cumin, and shape into two patties.
  3. Fry the patties in the rest of the oil over medium heat, 3–4 mins a side, until deep gold and firm.
  4. Serve the burgers and wedges with the cherry tomatoes and rocket, dressed with a squeeze of lemon.
Grated halloumi does double duty in these patties — it seasons them from the inside with its salty tang and, as it heats, melts just enough to bind everything so the burgers hold together in the pan.

Breakfast
Yoghurt pancakes with blackberries & seeds
~400 kcal · 18g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 60g self-raising flour (or plain + ½ tsp baking powder)
  • 1 large egg
  • 100g Greek yoghurt
  • 50ml whole milk
  • 100g blackberries
  • 1 tbsp mixed seeds
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp butter, for the pan
Method
  1. Whisk the egg, yoghurt and milk together, then fold in the flour to a thick, dollopable batter — don't overmix.
  2. Melt a little butter in a non-stick pan over medium heat and drop in spoonfuls of batter.
  3. Cook 2 mins until bubbles rise and pop on top, then flip and give them 1 min more until golden and springy.
  4. Stack up, scatter over the blackberries and seeds, and finish with a drizzle of honey.
Yoghurt makes these pancakes thick and fluffy rather than thin and floppy — and wait for bubbles to appear and burst across the surface before flipping, the sign the underside is set and golden.
Lunch
Roasted tomato & red pepper soup with cheese toastie
~445 kcal · 19g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 4 large vine tomatoes, quartered
  • 1 red pepper, in chunks
  • ½ onion, in wedges; 1 garlic clove
  • 200ml vegetable stock
  • 2 slices sourdough
  • 40g mature cheddar, grated
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful basil
Method
  1. Heat oven to 210°C. Toss the tomatoes, pepper, onion and garlic with the oil and roast 25 mins until soft and caught at the edges.
  2. Tip into a jug with the stock and most of the basil, and blitz with a stick blender until smooth. Loosen with a little more stock if needed and season.
  3. Butter the outsides of the bread, fill with the cheddar, and fry in a dry pan until golden and molten inside.
  4. Reheat the soup, pour into a bowl, top with the last of the basil, and serve the toastie alongside for dipping.
Roasting the vegetables before blending is what gives this soup its deep, sweet flavour — the oven caramelises their edges in a way simmering never can, and a stick blender saves you decanting anything hot into a jug.
Dinner
Feta & pine nut stuffed peppers with couscous
~500 kcal · 21g protein▾
Ingredients
  • 2 red peppers, halved and deseeded
  • 80g feta, crumbled
  • 60g giant (pearl) couscous (dry)
  • 1 tbsp pine nuts
  • ¼ red onion, finely chopped; 1 garlic clove
  • 100g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Small handful mint & parsley
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Handful salad leaves
Method
  1. Heat oven to 200°C. Rub the pepper halves with a little oil and roast, cut-side up, for 15 mins to soften.
  2. Meanwhile toast the couscous in a dry pan 2 mins, then simmer in salted water 8 mins until tender. Drain, and toast the pine nuts in the same pan.
  3. Fry the onion and garlic in the rest of the oil, 4 mins, then mix through the couscous with the tomatoes, herbs, pine nuts and half the feta.
  4. Spoon the filling into the peppers, scatter the rest of the feta over, and roast 15 mins more until golden. Serve with the dressed leaves.
Roast the empty pepper halves for a spell before filling them — they need longer than the stuffing to turn tender and sweet, so a head start stops you serving a soft filling inside a still-crunchy, raw-tasting shell.

Protein guideline: ~1.6–2.0g per kg of body weight when training to build muscle. The "anchor" idea: ~30g of protein per eating window is roughly the upper limit your body can use efficiently in one sitting, so spreading intake matters as much as the daily total. At 55+ this matters more, not less — older bodies are slightly resistant to anabolic signals, so each dose has to be solid.

The four levers

01
Train

2–3 resistance sessions a week. Compound lifts first (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). Progressive overload — tiny, steady increases week to week. Your trainer programs this.

02
Eat

A protein anchor at every meal (~30g). Modest calorie surplus on training days. Carbs around the session — you'll perform better and recover faster.

03
Recover

7–9 hours of sleep. At least 48 hours between sessions hitting the same muscle group. Walking and easy cycling are active recovery, not training.

04
Track

A simple log: weights & reps, body weight once a week, energy & sleep on a 1–5 scale. Review monthly. Adjust one variable at a time so you can tell what's actually working.

Creatine monohydrate is one of the most evidence-backed supplements in the field — 3–5g a day, taken any time. It's vegetarian and especially useful as we get older, since vegetarians tend to have lower baseline stores. Worth flagging to your GP if you have any kidney concerns, but otherwise extremely well tolerated.

Safety & risk management

Get cleared first

Before starting structured resistance training at 55+, book a check-up with your GP. Mention blood pressure, joints, and any medications.

Progress slowly

The first 4–6 weeks are for movement quality, not heavy weight. Tiny increases week-to-week beat aggressive jumps that strain joints.

Listen to signals

Sharp pain (not muscle burn), joint pain, persistent fatigue, or poor sleep: back off and review. Don't push through.

Recovery is training

Muscle is built between sessions, not during them. Treat sleep, walks, and rest days as load-bearing parts of the plan.

Hydration

Especially with extra protein and creatine: aim for clear-to-pale-yellow urine across the day. Two litres of water is a sensible floor.

Easy reversibility

Everything here is a small enhancement to a plan that already works. If a swap doesn't suit you, roll it back. No commitment is permanent.

Finding the right trainer

Look for someone with experience training clients 50+, ideally with a recognised qualification (CIMSPA Level 3+, or equivalent). Most trainers offer a free intro session — use it.

Five questions to ask before signing up:
  1. How many clients in their 50s+ do you currently train, and what does a typical first month look like for them?
  2. How do you approach progressive overload for someone newer to resistance training?
  3. What's your protocol if a client reports joint pain or unusual fatigue?
  4. Are you comfortable programming around a vegetarian diet and discussing protein targets?
  5. How do we measure progress beyond weight on the bar — strength, mobility, energy, body composition?
